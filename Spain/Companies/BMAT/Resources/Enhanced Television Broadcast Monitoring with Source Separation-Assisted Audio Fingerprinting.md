Continuing BMAT's core problem of recognizing music buried under TV dialogue or sound effects, this 2025 paper (published in Multimedia Tools and Applications) tests whether "music source separation" — a technique that automatically splits a mixed recording back into separate parts, like isolating the music from the speech — can be used as a helper step before audio fingerprinting (the process of turning audio into a searchable identifying code). The case study shows that stripping out speech and noise before generating a fingerprint improves how reliably the system can identify what music is actually being broadcast, directly supporting the kind of TV monitoring BMAT sells to rights holders and broadcasters.
