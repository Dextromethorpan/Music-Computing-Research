TV shows and ads often use background music at very different volumes — sometimes clearly noticeable, sometimes barely there under dialogue. This matters for BMAT because royalty payments depend partly on whether a piece of music was genuinely "audible" to viewers, not just technically present in the audio mix. Grown out of BMAT's LoudSense project (with the Music Technology Group at Universitat Pompeu Fabra), this paper investigates how to estimate that audibility automatically, especially in the tricky "barely audible" grey zone. The researchers show that audibility is a matter of human perception and attention, not just a raw volume measurement, and work toward building a labeled dataset and computational methods that estimate how noticeable background music actually is to a listener.
