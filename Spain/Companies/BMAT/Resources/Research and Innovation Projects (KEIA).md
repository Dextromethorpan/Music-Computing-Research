BMAT publicly lists its research and development projects, most funded by EU or Spanish/Catalan public research programs, and invests over 3 million euros a year in in-house R&D. One current project, KEIA (funded by the Barcelona Music Lab Foundation), aims to build techniques for auditing AI-generated music: checking whether AI music-generation systems were trained on copyrighted songs without permission, and developing ways to detect and identify that copyrighted material inside AI-generated output. The goal is to make AI music generation more transparent and give rights holders and regulators a way to verify that generative AI systems are using music ethically. Other listed projects (like TROBA and Playing G.A.I.M.E.s) extend this same theme of adapting music identification technology to a world where audio can be edited, remixed, or generated by AI.
