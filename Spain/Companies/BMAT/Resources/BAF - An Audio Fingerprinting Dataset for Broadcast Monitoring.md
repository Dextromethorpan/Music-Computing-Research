BMAT's business is monitoring what music radio and TV stations actually broadcast, so royalties get paid to the right people. This ISMIR 2022 paper introduces BAF, the first public dataset built specifically to test "audio fingerprinting" (a way of recognizing a song from a short, noisy clip, similar to how Shazam works) in the messy conditions of real broadcast — where the song of interest is often mixed quietly under a TV host talking, sound effects, or other music. The dataset contains 74 hours of production/stock music (the kind used as background music in shows and ads) plus 57 hours of real TV audio recordings, so researchers can measure how well fingerprinting systems cope with this specific, hard "background music" case rather than the easier scenario of a song playing cleanly on its own. The paper also benchmarks several existing fingerprinting methods on this new dataset to show where they struggle.
