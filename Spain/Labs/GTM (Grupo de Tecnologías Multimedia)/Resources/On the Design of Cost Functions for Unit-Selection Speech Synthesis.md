Unit-selection speech synthesis (the technique behind Cotovía) works by picking the best short recorded speech fragments from a large database and joining them to form new sentences. Choosing "the best" fragments requires a mathematical scoring rule, called a cost function, that predicts how natural a given combination will sound before it's actually played. This paper studies how to design that scoring rule well, so the synthesized speech avoids audible glitches or mismatched tone where fragments are joined — a core engineering challenge behind making synthetic voices sound smooth and natural.
