This study investigates whether where a sound seems to come from in space (its "source location," e.g. in front of you versus behind you) changes how a listener emotionally reacts to music. The researchers recorded listeners' brain activity with EEG (electroencephalography — electrodes on the scalp that measure electrical brain signals) while playing music from four different spatial positions, then used machine learning to find patterns linking brain activity to the sound's position and emotional tone. They found that unpleasant ("negative valence") music placed outside a listener's field of view produced a noticeably different brain response, suggesting spatial audio placement itself can shape emotional impact.
