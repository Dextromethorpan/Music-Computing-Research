This paper defines a new way to create sound using only mathematical "spatial transformation matrices" that describe how sound moves and spreads in 2D and 3D space, rather than starting from a recorded or synthesized audio waveform. In effect, the spatial movement pattern itself becomes the raw material for generating a new sound, which is unusual — most sound synthesis starts from a waveform and adds spatial effects afterward, while this method builds the sound from the spatial behavior directly. Published in the Journal of the Audio Engineering Society, it points toward spatial audio becoming its own kind of musical instrument.
