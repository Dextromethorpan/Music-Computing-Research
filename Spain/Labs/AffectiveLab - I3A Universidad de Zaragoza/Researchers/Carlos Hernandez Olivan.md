Carlos Hernández Oliván completed his PhD in Music Information Retrieval at Universidad de Zaragoza, supervised by Jose R. Beltrán, and previously trained as a classical viola player. His work combines music technology and deep learning: automatic music transcription, symbolic music structure analysis, and tools for generating and evaluating AI-composed music. He created musicaiz, an open-source Python toolkit used throughout the lab's research, and spent time as a visiting researcher at NTT Communication Science Laboratories in Japan.
