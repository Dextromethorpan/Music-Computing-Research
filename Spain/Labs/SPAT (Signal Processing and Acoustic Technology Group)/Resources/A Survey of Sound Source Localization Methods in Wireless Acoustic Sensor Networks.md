A wireless acoustic sensor network is a set of independent, wireless microphones scattered around a space (rather than a single fixed microphone array) that work together to sense sound. This paper reviews the many different mathematical methods researchers have developed for figuring out where a sound is physically coming from using such a scattered network — a harder problem than with a single microphone array because the microphones' exact positions and clock timings aren't perfectly known or synchronized. As a survey paper, it summarizes and compares existing approaches rather than proposing a brand-new one, giving readers a map of the field.
