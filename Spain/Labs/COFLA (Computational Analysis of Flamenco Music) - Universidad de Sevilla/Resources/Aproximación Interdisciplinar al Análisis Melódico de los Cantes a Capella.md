This early COFLA project paper looks at "cantes a capella" — flamenco singing styles performed with no instrumental accompaniment, just the human voice. Because there's no backing instrument to lock onto, these unaccompanied vocal lines are especially hard for automatic melody-tracking software to follow accurately, since the singer bends pitch freely and doesn't stick to a fixed scale in the way Western sheet music assumes. The paper takes an "interdisciplinary" approach, combining musicological expertise (people who understand flamenco singing conventions) with signal-processing tools (software that measures pitch from an audio recording) to analyze these melodies more reliably. It's one of the founding studies that shaped how the COFLA group later built its larger corpus and annotation methods.
