Flamenco is a Southern Spanish music tradition that is largely improvised and almost never written down as sheet music, which makes it hard to study with the usual tools of computer music research (those tools mostly assume you have a score to compare the audio against). This paper introduces "corpusCOFLA," a large collection of flamenco audio recordings plus hand-made annotations (like manually marked melody lines), built specifically so researchers can train and test computer programs that try to automatically describe, categorize, or analyze flamenco singing. The authors explain how they chose which recordings and annotations to include, and give examples of smaller research datasets carved out of the larger corpus for specific tasks, such as automatically transcribing a singer's melody. The paper is a foundational resource for a niche corner of Music Information Retrieval: teaching computers to understand a genre that doesn't follow the conventions of most music research (like Western sheet music or metronomic rhythm).
