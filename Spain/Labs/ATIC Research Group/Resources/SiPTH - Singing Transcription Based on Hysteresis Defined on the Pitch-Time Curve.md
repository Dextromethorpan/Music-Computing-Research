SiPTH is a method for automatically turning a recording of someone singing into written musical notes (a transcription). The tricky part of this task is deciding exactly where one sung note ends and the next begins, since a human voice glides smoothly between pitches rather than jumping cleanly like a piano. The authors solve this using a trick borrowed from electronics called "hysteresis" — a note boundary is only registered once the pitch has moved far enough away from the current note to avoid false triggers from small wobbles in the voice, like vibrato. This produces more accurate transcriptions than earlier, simpler note-segmentation methods, and the technique has been used as a building block for other ATIC research on singing analysis and scoring.
