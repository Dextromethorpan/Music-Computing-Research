This paper tackles a basic but useful problem: given a chunk of audio, automatically tell whether it's speech, instrumental music, or a song (singing with music). The authors introduce a new set of measurements called PHEQ, which look at how the statistical distribution of short-term audio features changes across a whole segment of audio (rather than just moment to moment), and compare it to a reference pattern. They show that this approach classifies speech, music, and song more accurately than the standard technique of the time (MFCCs, a common way of summarizing the tone/timbre of a sound), especially when combined with it.
