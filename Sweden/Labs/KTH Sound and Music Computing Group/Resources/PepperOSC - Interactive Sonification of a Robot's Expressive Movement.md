This paper describes a system that lets a humanoid robot (the "Pepper" robot) generate real-time sound based on how it moves its body — a technique called sonification, where movement or data is translated into audio instead of pictures or numbers. The idea is that giving a robot's gestures an audible "voice" can make its behavior feel more expressive and easier for people to read, similar to how a dancer's movement can be felt through accompanying music. The researchers built a toolkit (PepperOSC) that other robot designers can reuse to attach sound to robot motion.
