This doctoral thesis, published by Lund University Press, studies what happens when a computer program improvises music together with a human musician in real time — for example, a software system that listens to a live saxophone player and generates a fitting response on the spot. It uses this kind of live, unscripted computer-music interaction as a lens for rethinking how humans and computers can work together more generally, beyond just music.
