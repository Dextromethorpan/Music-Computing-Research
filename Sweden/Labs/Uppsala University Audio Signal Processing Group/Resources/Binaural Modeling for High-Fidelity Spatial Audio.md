This PhD thesis is about making headphones fool your brain into thinking sound is coming from real places around you (like a concert hall), instead of sounding like it's stuck inside your head — a technique called binaural audio. It introduces better ways to measure how each person's own head and ears change incoming sound (called a "head-related transfer function," or HRTF), which is what makes 3D headphone audio convincing. The thesis also proposes a new microphone-array-based measurement method that is more accurate and comfortable than older techniques, and a way to clean up unwanted tonal artifacts that used to make virtual speaker simulations sound slightly "off." This work matters for virtual reality, augmented reality, and any headphone-based spatial audio product.
