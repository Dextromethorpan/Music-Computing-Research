When sound engineers place a single "object" (like one instrument or voice) somewhere between two stereo speakers using volume tricks (called panning), the tone of that sound can subtly change compared to how it would sound from a single speaker directly in front of you — this is a known coloration problem in stereo and multichannel mixing. This paper, published in IEEE/ACM Transactions on Audio, Speech, and Language Processing, proposes a correction filter that fixes this tonal shift so panned sounds keep their intended timbre no matter where they're placed in the stereo image. It builds directly on the group's long-running room- and loudspeaker-correction research.
