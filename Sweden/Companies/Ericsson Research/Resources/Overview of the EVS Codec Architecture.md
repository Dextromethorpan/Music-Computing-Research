A codec is a piece of technology that compresses audio into a much smaller file or data stream, then decompresses it again for playback — this is what lets phone calls and streaming audio work without eating enormous amounts of data. This paper explains the design of EVS (Enhanced Voice Services), the audio compression standard used in most modern mobile calls, which was built to make voice — and even background music — sound clearer over mobile networks than older phone-call codecs did.
