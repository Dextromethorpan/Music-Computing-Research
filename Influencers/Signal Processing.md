# Influencers — Signal Processing

## Geraint Luff (Signalsmith Audio)
**Type:** Both
**Platform(s):** Blog (signalsmith-audio.co.uk/writing), X @SignalsmithDSP, recurring guest on The Audio Programmer podcast, conference speaker (Audio Developer Conference)
**Focus:** Independent audio DSP consultant who writes deeply technical, practical breakdowns of things like pitch-shifting, limiters, and reverb design, and open-sources the algorithms he explains.
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** One of the most-cited independent voices for practical, implementable DSP techniques used across the plugin-development community.

## Jan Wilczek (WolfSound)
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube channel "WolfSound", blog thewolfsound.com (dedicated DSP article section), paid DSP course (Wolf Sound Academy)
**Focus:** Audio programming consultant producing tutorials, book reviews, and long-form articles specifically on digital audio signal processing and C++ plugin development.
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** Runs one of the few YouTube/blog operations built entirely around teaching audio-specific DSP fundamentals to practitioners.

## Will Pirkle
**Type:** Practitioner/thought leader
**Platform(s):** willpirkle.com, author of "Designing Audio Effect Plugins in C++" and related DSP-theory textbooks widely used in the plugin-dev community
**Focus:** Audio engineering educator whose books pair DSP theory (filters, modulation, distortion) directly with C++ plugin implementation.
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** His books are a de facto standard reference practitioners cite when learning to turn DSP theory into working audio code.

## Andy Farnell
**Type:** Practitioner/thought leader
**Platform(s):** Author of "Designing Sound" (MIT Press), public talks (e.g. Music Hackspace "Research methods in interactive sound design"), teaching materials at aspress.co.uk
**Focus:** Procedural audio and DSP-for-sound-design educator who teaches signal-processing-based synthesis (rather than sample playback) using Pure Data.
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** His book and talks are widely referenced as the entry point for treating sound design as applied signal processing/synthesis rather than sample manipulation.

## The Audio Programmer
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube channel "The Audio Programmer" (JUCE/C++ tutorials), podcast (theaudioprogrammer.com/podcast) with DSP-engineer guests, community/Discord
**Focus:** A community and content hub for audio software developers, publishing JUCE tutorials and podcast interviews with working DSP engineers on filter design, pitch shifting, and plugin architecture.
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** Functions as a hub that surfaces and amplifies other DSP practitioners (e.g. its Geraint Luff and DSP-engineer episodes), making it a go-to aggregator for the audio-DSP practitioner community.

## Julius O. Smith III
**Type:** Both
**Platform(s):** Freely published online books (ccrma.stanford.edu, dsprelated.com/freebooks — "Physical Audio Signal Processing," "Spectral Audio Signal Processing," etc.)
**Focus:** Stanford CCRMA researcher whose free, widely-read online textbooks are a standard self-teaching reference for audio DSP theory (filters, spectral processing, physical modeling).
**Why relevant to Signal Processing:** Already tracked in this vault as a CCRMA Researcher ([[Julius Smith]]), but included here separately because his free public-facing books — not his lab output — are what practitioners across the whole audio-DSP community actually read and cite; noted here for that distinct public-voice role rather than duplicating the Researcher note.
