# Influencers — Open Source Research Tools

## Valerio Velardo
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube — "Valerio Velardo - The Sound of AI"; thesoundofai.com
**Focus:** Teaches audio signal processing and audio machine learning from the ground up, walking through open Python tooling (librosa and similar) step by step rather than just showing finished demos.
**Why relevant to Open Source Research Tools:** His tutorials are one of the main on-ramps practitioners use to actually learn to work with open-source MIR/audio-analysis libraries.

## Geraint Luff
**Type:** Both
**Platform(s):** Signalsmith Audio (signalsmith-audio.co.uk/writing), GitHub (Signalsmith-Audio/dsp), Audio Developer Conference speaker, guest on the WolfTalk podcast
**Focus:** Writes and open-sources DSP algorithms (reverbs, pitch-shifting, filters) with detailed public write-ups explaining the design, then releases the code as reusable open libraries.
**Why relevant to Open Source Research Tools:** A practitioner whose personal open-source DSP libraries and public design write-ups are directly the kind of tool-sharing this Concept describes, done outside any single lab.

## Jatin Chowdhury
**Type:** Both
**Platform(s):** Chowdhury DSP (chowdsp.com), GitHub (Chowdhury-DSP, jatinchowdhury18), Medium blog, conference talks (e.g. QUB)
**Focus:** Builds and open-sources audio DSP libraries (Wave Digital Filter tooling, plugin frameworks) and writes about the engineering behind them; also does research-adjacent work out of CCRMA.
**Why relevant to Open Source Research Tools:** Straddles research and public practice — his open-source libraries and blog posts are widely cited by other audio-plugin/DSP developers as reference implementations.

## The Audio Programmer
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube channel "The Audio Programmer," GitHub org (github.com/theaudioprogrammer), companion podcast/community (theaudioprogrammer.com)
**Focus:** Long-running tutorial series and community built around JUCE and open-source C++ audio plugin development, with podcast episodes interviewing DSP/tool authors.
**Why relevant to Open Source Research Tools:** One of the most-referenced public teaching resources for working with JUCE, itself a major open-source research/production audio framework.

## WolfSound
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube @WolfSoundAudio, thewolfsound.com, "WolfTalk" podcast
**Focus:** Interview-driven content and tutorials on audio signal processing and audio programming careers, regularly featuring people who build and maintain open-source audio tools.
**Why relevant to Open Source Research Tools:** Functions as a public amplifier for open-source audio-tool authors (e.g. interviewed Geraint Luff, Eric Tarr, Stefano D'Angelo), surfacing their work to a wider practitioner audience.
