# Influencers — New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME)

## Sam Battle (Look Mum No Computer)
**Type:** Content creator
**Platform(s):** YouTube (LOOK MUM NO COMPUTER)
**Focus:** Builds and documents wildly unconventional electronic instruments — from a 1000-oscillator drone machine to Furby-based organs and lightsaber theremins — showing the build process step by step.
**Why relevant to New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME):** His whole channel is a public, hands-on demonstration of exactly what NIME research studies academically — new, physically unconventional interfaces for making sound — reaching a huge maker/musician audience outside academia.

## Imogen Heap
**Type:** Practitioner/thought leader
**Platform(s):** Public talks and press coverage (CNN, TechCrunch, Dezeen) around the Mi.Mu Gloves project; mimugloves.com
**Focus:** Co-created and champions the Mi.Mu Gloves, a gesture-controlled wearable performance instrument, and speaks publicly about designing expressive, body-driven control of music technology.
**Why relevant to New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME):** Mi.Mu Gloves is a widely cited real-world example of gestural NIME-style instrument design, and Heap is its most visible public advocate, shaping mainstream awareness of the field.

## Onyx Ashanti
**Type:** Practitioner/thought leader
**Platform(s):** TED speaker profile; ongoing press coverage (CDM, 3D Printing Industry, Design Indaba) of live performances
**Focus:** Performs live using "beatjazz" — a self-built, 3D-printed, gesture-controlled wearable instrument/interface that generates and manipulates sound in real time.
**Why relevant to New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME):** An independent (non-lab) practitioner whose entire practice is building and performing with a custom new musical interface — a direct, publicly visible embodiment of the NIME concept.
