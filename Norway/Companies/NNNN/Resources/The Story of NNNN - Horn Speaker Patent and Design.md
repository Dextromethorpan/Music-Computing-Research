A "horn speaker" is a loudspeaker design that uses a shaped horn (like the bell of a trumpet) in front of the driver to make it louder and more efficient, and to control which direction the sound goes. The catch is that for a horn driver to work well, the empty chamber behind its diaphragm normally needs to be very large — often bigger than the horn itself — which is why most horn speakers end up bulky. This company blog post, written by co-founder and CTO Rune Skramstad, explains NNNN's patented fix: a specially engineered resistance behind the diaphragm that makes the driver behave as though its rear chamber were infinitely large, even when the actual chamber is small. This lets NNNN's speakers keep a horn's benefits (efficiency, low distortion, controlled directivity) in a much smaller, more practical package, aimed at making touring and installed sound systems lighter and more energy-efficient.
