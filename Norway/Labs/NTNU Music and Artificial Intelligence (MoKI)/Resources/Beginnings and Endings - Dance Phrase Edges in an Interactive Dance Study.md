When a dancer performs, their movement naturally has a shape — moments where a gesture clearly starts and clearly ends, like a musical phrase. This paper, published in the journal Personal and Ubiquitous Computing, studies how a computer system can detect these "phrase edges" in a dancer's movement using motion sensors, so that an interactive sound system knows when to trigger, change, or fade musical material in response. The goal is to make computer-generated music feel like a real, sensitive musical partner responding to a dancer's intention, not just a generic reaction to any movement. This is part of NTNU's broader work combining AI, movement tracking, and live music generation.
