This paper looks back at more than 1,200 papers published over the first 15 years of the NIME conference (New Interfaces for Musical Expression) — an academic conference about inventing new ways to control and make music with electronics. The author, who edited a book collecting NIME's most important papers, uses this history to spot patterns: which topics come up again and again, which ideas turned out to be short-lived trends, and how the field's priorities have shifted over time. It is a useful entry point for understanding how the community that designs new digital musical instruments has evolved.
