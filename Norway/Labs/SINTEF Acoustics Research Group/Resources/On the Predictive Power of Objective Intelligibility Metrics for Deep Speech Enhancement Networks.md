When researchers build a deep-learning system that cleans up noisy speech, they usually score it with quick computer-calculated numbers called "objective intelligibility metrics," instead of running full listening tests with real people every time. This paper checks whether five of the most popular scoring formulas actually predict how real human listeners rate speech cleaned up by modern deep neural network speech-enhancement systems. It finds gaps between what the automatic scores say and what people actually experience, which matters because it means today's shortcuts for judging AI speech-cleanup tools can be misleading. Published in IEEE/ACM Transactions on Audio, Speech and Language Processing.
