US copyright law requires meaningful human authorship for a work to be registrable. Music generated by an AI tool from a prompt alone, without further human creative intervention, does not qualify for copyright protection. This creates a structural asymmetry rather than just a legal technicality: a sync licensor, ad agency, or platform can use fully AI-generated music for free (no royalty owed, no copyright to clear) while equivalent human-composed music still carries a license fee — meaning the law itself tilts commercial buyers toward AI-generated tracks purely on cost, independent of quality.

## Evidence
The U.S. Copyright Office's own report on AI-generated works reiterates that human authorship remains the deciding factor in copyrightability, and applicants must now affirmatively disclose any AI-generated content in a work and explain the human author's specific contribution — with AI-generated content beyond "a tiny portion" required to be excluded from the claim entirely. Legal commentary analyzing the report describes the resulting asymmetry directly: because AI-generated music doesn't attract copyright protection, it is "in a better position to undercut human-made copyright music" commercially, since users can use it freely without royalty or licensing obligations that apply to protected human works.
