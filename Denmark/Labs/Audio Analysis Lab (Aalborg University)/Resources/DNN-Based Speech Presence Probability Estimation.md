Before a system can clean up or enhance speech in a noisy recording, it first needs to know exactly when speech is actually present versus when it's just background noise. This paper (presented at IEEE WASPAA, a leading audio signal-processing workshop) uses a "DNN" (deep neural network — a type of AI model with many layers) to estimate the probability that speech is present at any given moment in an audio signal, improving on older statistical methods. This kind of detection feeds into noise reduction for hearing aids, headphones, and voice assistants.
