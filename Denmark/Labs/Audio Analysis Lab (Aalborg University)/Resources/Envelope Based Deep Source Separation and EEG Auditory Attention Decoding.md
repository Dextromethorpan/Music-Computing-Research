This paper (presented at EUSIPCO, a major European signal-processing conference) combines two ideas: "deep source separation," where a neural network pulls apart a mixed audio recording into its separate sound sources (like isolating one instrument or voice from a mix), and "EEG-based attention decoding," where brain-signal recordings reveal which sound a listener is focusing on. Together, this points toward hearing devices that could automatically isolate whichever sound source — speech or music — a person is actually paying attention to, guided by their own brain activity.
