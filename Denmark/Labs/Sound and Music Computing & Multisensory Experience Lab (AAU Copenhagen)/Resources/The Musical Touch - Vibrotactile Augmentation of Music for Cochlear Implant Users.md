Cochlear implants are electronic devices that give people with severe hearing loss a sense of sound, but they often struggle to convey the rich pitch and texture of music. This PhD thesis explores adding "vibrotactile" feedback — vibrations felt through the skin, like the buzz of a phone — as an extra channel to let cochlear implant users feel aspects of music (rhythm, pitch changes) that are hard to hear through the implant alone. It combines engineering (building the vibration devices) with user studies testing whether the added touch sensation genuinely improves people's musical experience.
