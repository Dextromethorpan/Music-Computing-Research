This paper describes the "Tubularium," a large, human-sized musical instrument made of seven glowing acrylic tubes that anyone can play with no musical training. Touching a tube plays a note or chord, and lights show you which tube to touch next, so you can make pleasant-sounding music using your whole body instead of fine finger movements on a keyboard. The researchers tested it with 32 people at a youth cultural center and found that even total beginners could quickly make music they enjoyed, felt a sense of ownership over the sounds, and described the experience as immersive and memorable a month later. The instrument also has an "AI jamming" mode: record yourself playing, and a machine-learning model trained on your recording improvises an endless continuation you can play along with. This work won a Best Paper Award and is part of a broader research direction the group calls "musicking" — treating music-making as something everyone should get to actively do, not just listen to.
