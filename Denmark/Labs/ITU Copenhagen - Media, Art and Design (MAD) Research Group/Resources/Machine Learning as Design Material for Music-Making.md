This paper reflects on the group's experience building several music-making tools (including Tubularium and Picnic) and argues that machine learning should be treated as a "design material" — something a designer sculpts and shapes intentionally, the same way a furniture designer thinks about wood grain — rather than a black-box add-on bolted onto a finished product. The authors share practical lessons learned from repeatedly training and testing machine-learning models inside creative, hands-on interfaces meant for people with no musical background. It is aimed at other designers and researchers who want to use AI in creative tools without losing sight of the human experience of making the tool.
