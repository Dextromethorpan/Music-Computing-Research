This book asks a simple but overlooked question: how does sound shape who we think we are? Schulze argues that from childhood, the sounds we grow up with and the ways we're taught to listen quietly shape our sense of identity, long before we're consciously aware of it — he calls this shaped listening self the "sonic persona." The book draws on real listening habits, from voice assistants to music subcultures, to explore how sound and listening are never neutral background noise but active forces in culture and identity. It's a foundational text for anyone studying sound from a cultural/social angle rather than a purely technical one.
