Intensive Care Unit (ICU) hospital wards are often noisy and full of hard, sound-reflecting surfaces, which can make it difficult for staff and patients to understand speech clearly — a real safety concern in a medical setting. This paper examines which physical and acoustic factors in ICU wards most affect how understandable speech is, applying room-acoustics analysis methods to a practical healthcare-design problem rather than a music or entertainment context.
