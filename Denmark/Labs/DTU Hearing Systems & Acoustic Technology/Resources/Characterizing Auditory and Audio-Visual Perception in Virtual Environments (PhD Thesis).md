This PhD thesis studies how well virtual reality (VR) systems can recreate a convincing sense of sound and combined sound-and-sight ("audio-visual") experience compared to the real world. It runs controlled listening experiments comparing perception in real rooms versus simulated/virtual versions of those rooms, helping establish how accurate VR sound technology needs to be before people perceive it as believable — directly relevant to building realistic spatial audio for games, VR training, and virtual concerts.
