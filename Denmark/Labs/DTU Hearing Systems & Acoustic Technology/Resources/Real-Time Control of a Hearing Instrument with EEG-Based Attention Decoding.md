In a noisy room with several people talking, our brains can focus on ("attend to") just one voice — this is sometimes called the "cocktail party effect." This paper shows a hearing instrument that reads a wearer's brain activity via EEG (electrodes measuring electrical signals from the scalp) to figure out, in real time, which speaker the person is trying to listen to, and then automatically boosts that voice. It's an early step toward "smart" hearing aids that adapt to what the wearer actually wants to hear, rather than treating all sounds equally.
