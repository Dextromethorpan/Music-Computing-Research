This study places people in virtual, computer-simulated rooms with varying numbers of simultaneous talkers and background sounds, then tracks how much they physically move their heads while trying to follow speech. It finds that the complexity of the sound scene (how many things are competing for attention) changes how much people move to help themselves hear better — for example turning toward a speaker. This matters for designing realistic virtual and augmented reality sound environments and for understanding how people naturally cope with noisy, cluttered soundscapes.
