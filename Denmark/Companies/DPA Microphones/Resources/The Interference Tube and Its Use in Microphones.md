An "interference tube" is the long slotted tube you see on shotgun microphones (the kind used in film and TV boom poles) that makes the microphone much more sensitive to sound coming from directly in front of it than sound coming from the sides. This DPA Mic University article explains the physics of how the tube works (sound arriving from off-axis angles cancels itself out inside the slots) and describes how DPA has refined the design over decades to make that directional pickup more precise and consistent across different pitches. It is genuine applied-acoustics engineering content, not a general marketing page.
