Different hearing-aid sound settings can be noticeably different to the same listener, but simply asking "which do you prefer?" in a lab is hard to do rigorously. This paper introduces LEAP, a structured laboratory test where listeners directly compare pairs of hearing-aid sound settings played live, letting researchers measure genuine sound-quality preference rather than relying on vague verbal descriptions. It gives hearing-aid companies a repeatable way to check whether a new processing algorithm actually sounds better to real listeners before it ships in a product.
