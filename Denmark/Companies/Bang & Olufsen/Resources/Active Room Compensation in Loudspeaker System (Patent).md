This granted US patent describes the method behind Bang & Olufsen's "Active Room Compensation" feature (used in products like the BeoLab 90 and Beosound Balance): a loudspeaker system with a built-in microphone measures the sound reflecting back off the walls and furniture in the actual room it's placed in, and a signal-processing algorithm then automatically builds a correction filter so the speaker's output sounds balanced regardless of where it's positioned. In plain terms, it's a speaker that "listens" to the room it's in and adjusts itself, rather than requiring the room to be professionally treated for good sound. This is a concrete example of published, patented DSP research turning into a real shipped consumer feature.
