This open-access Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper studies how everyday audio processing steps (like compression or equalization) change the way listeners perceive "spatial" qualities of sound — for example how wide, enveloping, or well-placed a sound seems. Researchers ran listening tests where people rated recordings that had been through different common processing chains, to find out which processes damage the sense of space the most. This matters because Bang & Olufsen builds products meant to reproduce a realistic, spacious sound field, so knowing which processing steps hurt that experience helps them design better products and mixing guidance. It's part of a longer running research partnership between Bang & Olufsen and academic audio labs.
