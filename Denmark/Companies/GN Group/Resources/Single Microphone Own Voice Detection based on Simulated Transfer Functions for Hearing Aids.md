Hearing aids sometimes need to know whether the wearer is talking versus hearing someone else — this is called "own voice detection," and it lets the device automatically change behavior (like reducing amplification) while the wearer speaks, making conversations less harsh and more comfortable. This paper, funded by GN (parent of the ReSound hearing-aid brand), shows how to detect a wearer's own voice using just one microphone, instead of the multiple microphones or extra sensors many existing systems rely on. The team trained an AI classifier on computer-simulated versions of how sound travels from a person's mouth to a hearing-aid microphone through the air and around the head, gradually making the simulations more physically realistic, then tested the trained model on real hearing-aid recordings. The system correctly identified the wearer's own voice roughly 80-95% of the time depending on test conditions, showing that carefully simulated training data can substitute for expensive real-world recordings.
