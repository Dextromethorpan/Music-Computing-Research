Hearing aids can suffer from feedback — the annoying whistle you sometimes hear when a hand or object gets close to someone's ear. This happens because the sound coming out of the hearing aid's speaker leaks back into its own microphone and gets re-amplified in a loop. This US patent, assigned to GN ReSound A/S (part of GN Group), describes a technique for "decorrelating" the hearing aid's input and output signals — deliberately making the outgoing sound less similar to a delayed version of itself — so the device's feedback-cancellation system can tell real feedback apart from normal speech and music more reliably. It's a concrete example of GN's patented signal-processing engineering for hearing-aid audio quality.
