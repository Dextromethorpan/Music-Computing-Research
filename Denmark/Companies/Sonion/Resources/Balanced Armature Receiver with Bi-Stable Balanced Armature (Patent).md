A "balanced armature receiver" is the tiny electromagnetic driver that turns electrical signal into sound inside a hearing aid or in-ear headphone — instead of a normal loudspeaker's cone, it moves a thin metal strip (the armature) balanced between two magnets. This patent covers a receiver design with two of these armature mechanisms working together, one of them deliberately built to be "unstable" so it snaps to a position rather than resting in the middle, which the inventors use to get more output or a different frequency response out of a very small part. It's a good example of how much of hearing-aid audio engineering is precision mechanical design at millimeter scale, not just circuitry.
