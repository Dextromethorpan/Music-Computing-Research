Hearing aids don't just make sound louder — they need to reshape sound in complex, individualized ways to compensate for exactly how a person's hearing is damaged. This paper, co-authored by two Eriksholm Research Centre (Oticon) scientists, trains a deep neural network — an AI model loosely inspired by how neurons connect and pass signals — to learn this reshaping automatically. The network is trained by comparing how a computer model of a "normal" ear and a computer model of a "hearing-impaired" ear each respond to the same speech signal, then teaching the network to process sound so the impaired-ear model's response matches the normal one's. In a listening test with real hearing-impaired participants, this AI approach improved speech understanding and was preferred over a conventional hearing-aid compression method.
