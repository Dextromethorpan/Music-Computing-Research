Following a conversation in a noisy room can be mentally tiring even when you technically hear every word — researchers call this "listening effort." One way to measure it is pupillometry: your pupil dilates slightly more when a listening task is harder, even if you don't consciously notice. This study, with an Eriksholm Research Centre (Oticon) co-author, tested how reliable pupil-size measurements are as a personal listening-effort gauge, comparing different ways of processing the raw eye-tracking data. The findings matter for hearing-aid design because a dependable way to measure listening effort in individuals could eventually help fit hearing aids to reduce mental fatigue, not just to make sound loud enough to hear.
