A laptop orchestra is an ensemble where each performer's "instrument" is a laptop computer, usually running custom software, and often paired with a special speaker so the computer's sound can be heard live rather than just through headphones. Because the sound comes from software rather than a physical instrument like a violin or trumpet, laptop orchestras raise new questions composers must solve: how to notate the music, how to make performers' actions visible and meaningful to an audience, and how to give an all-electronic ensemble a shared musical feel. The Princeton Laptop Orchestra (PLOrk), founded in 2005, was one of the first such ensembles and helped establish laptop orchestras as their own teaching and performance tradition, later inspiring similar groups at other universities.
