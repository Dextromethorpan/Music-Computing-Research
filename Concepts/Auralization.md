Auralization is the process of turning a room-acoustic model or measurement into an actual sound you can listen to, as if you were standing in that room. If room acoustic simulation predicts how sound waves would bounce around a virtual space, auralization is the last step: rendering an audio clip — like a piece of music or speech — as it would actually be heard inside that simulated or measured room. This is used to let architects and acousticians "listen" to a concert hall before it's built, to recreate the sound of historic or lost performance spaces, and to test whether room-acoustic simulation software actually sounds convincing to a human listener, not just mathematically correct.
