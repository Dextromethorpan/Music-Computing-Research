Unconventional and Biological Computing for Music means building music-making circuits out of things other than standard silicon computer chips — for example, using a living organism like slime mould, or other biological or chemical processes, as a working part of the electronics. In some experiments, researchers have grown a slime mould called Physarum polycephalum onto electrodes and used its natural electrical behaviour as a component (called a "memristor," a kind of circuit element whose resistance changes based on its history) inside a synthesizer-like system, so the organism's biological activity actually shapes the sound produced. This is a niche, experimental corner of music computing that explores what "computing" and "instrument-building" can mean beyond conventional hardware.
