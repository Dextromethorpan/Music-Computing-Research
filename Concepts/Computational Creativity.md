Computational creativity is a research field that studies whether and how computers can take part in creative activities — like composing music, writing, or painting — in a way that goes beyond simply following a fixed recipe. Rather than just asking "can software make something that sounds like music?", researchers in this field also study what creativity actually is, how to evaluate whether a machine's output is genuinely creative, and how AI can act as a creative collaborator or partner for a human artist rather than a replacement. In music computing, this shows up in tools for AI-assisted composition, improvisation partners, and questions about whose musical values and traditions get built into these systems.
