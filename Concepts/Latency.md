Latency is the delay between an action happening and its effect being heard or seen — for example, the tiny gap between a musician plucking a string and a computer's speaker actually producing the corresponding sound, or between one musician playing a note in one city and a collaborator hearing it in another city over the internet. In music computing, latency matters because human beings are extremely sensitive to timing: delays of even a few hundredths of a second can make it hard to play "in time" with other musicians or with a digital instrument's own sound. Much engineering effort in real-time audio software, networked performance tools, and digital instruments goes into keeping latency as low and as consistent as possible.
