Speech synthesis is the general name for technology that turns text or symbolic instructions into spoken audio — essentially, making a computer "talk." Early systems (like MBROLA) worked by stitching together short recorded snippets of a real human voice and adjusting their pitch and duration; modern systems increasingly use deep learning to generate a voice from scratch, including "expressive" versions that can sound happy, sad, or sing a melody. This overlaps with music computing wherever a system needs to produce or analyse a singing or speaking voice — for example, in voice cloning, virtual singers, or expressive text-to-speech for audiobooks and games.
