Timbre is the quality that lets you tell two instruments apart even when they play the exact same note at the exact same loudness — it's "everything about a sound except its pitch and volume." A violin and a trumpet playing middle C sound completely different, and that difference is timbre. Researchers study it because it isn't one simple thing: it depends on the shape of a sound's spectrum (which overtones are loud or quiet), how the sound starts (the "attack," like the pluck of a string or the bow catch of a violin), and how it changes over time. Timbre is notoriously hard to pin down scientifically — one influential paper even calls it an auditory "wastebasket," the category psychoacousticians dump anything into that isn't pitch, loudness, or duration. Understanding timbre matters for things like how composers blend or contrast instruments in an orchestra, how our brains recognize a familiar voice or instrument, and how synthesizers and AI audio tools try to recreate or manipulate specific sound qualities.
