Voice biometrics is the idea of using someone's voice as a kind of fingerprint to confirm their identity — the underlying technique is called speaker verification, and it works by analysing the unique acoustic characteristics of a person's voice (pitch, resonance, speaking patterns) and comparing them to a stored voice profile. It's used for things like unlocking a phone by speaking, authenticating a caller on a support line, or detecting whether a synthetic ("deepfake") voice is impersonating someone. It shares underlying technology with voice conversion and singing-voice research, since both fields need to model what makes a specific person's voice unique.
