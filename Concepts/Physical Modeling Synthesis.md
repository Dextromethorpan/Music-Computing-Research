Physical modeling synthesis creates sound by mathematically simulating the actual physics of how a real instrument makes noise — the vibration of a string, the airflow in a wind instrument, the collision of two objects — rather than recording a real instrument or approximating its sound with generic waveforms. This lets a musician control a synthesized instrument's realistic physical parameters (like the tension of a virtual string) and hear how the simulated instrument responds.
