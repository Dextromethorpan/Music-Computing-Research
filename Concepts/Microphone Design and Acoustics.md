A microphone converts physical sound waves in the air into an electrical signal, and the details of how it's built physically change what it can hear well. Engineers shape tiny internal parts — the capsule (the part that actually vibrates), tubes, vents, and enclosures — to control things like which directions the microphone is more or less sensitive to (its "polar pattern"), how loud a sound it can handle before distorting, and how much unwanted handling noise or wind noise gets through. This is a mechanical and acoustic engineering discipline as much as an electronics one: getting a microphone to sound clean and accurate under real-world conditions (a loud drum, a windy outdoor shoot, a boom pole far from an actor) is largely a matter of careful physical design, not just good circuitry.
