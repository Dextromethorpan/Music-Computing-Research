A phase vocoder is a signal-processing technique that splits a sound into many narrow frequency bands (a bit like separating white light into a rainbow), which lets software independently stretch or compress a sound's duration, or shift its pitch, without the unwanted side effects — like changing pitch when you only meant to change speed — that come from just simply speeding up or slowing down a raw audio file.
