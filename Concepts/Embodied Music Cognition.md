Embodied music cognition is the idea that we understand and experience music through our whole body, not just our ears. When you tap your foot, nod your head, dance, or feel a shiver during a song, your body is actively taking part in "understanding" the music — it's not a separate reaction that happens after the listening. Researchers in this field use sensors (like motion trackers, accelerometers, or biosignal monitors) to measure how people move and physically react while listening to, performing, or imagining music. This matters for building better music technology: sound and music systems that respond to a listener's movement can be used in music education, physical rehabilitation (e.g. helping stroke patients regain movement through rhythm), sports training, and interactive art installations.
