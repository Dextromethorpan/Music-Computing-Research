concept: Beamforming (Microphone Arrays)
date_first_seen: 2026-08-09
---

Beamforming is a technique for combining the signals from many microphones (a "microphone array") so the combined system can be digitally "pointed" at a specific direction, making sound coming from that direction much clearer while suppressing sound from other directions — like a spotlight, but for hearing instead of seeing. A single microphone alone cannot tell what direction a sound came from; at least two microphones positioned differently are needed for that, and the more microphones an array has, the more precisely it can isolate a direction. The same underlying array data can also be used to visually locate where a sound is coming from, track a moving sound source, or feed a machine-learning model that classifies what the sound actually is. The same mathematical principle also works in reverse, for sending sound rather than receiving it: a "line array" of loudspeakers can have its output beam electronically steered in a chosen direction, letting installers aim sound coverage without physically repositioning any hardware.
