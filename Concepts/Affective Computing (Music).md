Affective computing is the branch of computer science that tries to get computers to recognize, interpret, or generate emotion. In music, this means building software that can detect how a piece of music (or a specific performance of it) makes people feel, or that can change how a piece is played back — its timing, loudness, and articulation — to shift its emotional character, for example making the same melody sound sadder or more energetic. It matters because emotion is central to why humans make and listen to music, so tools that understand or control it open up applications in adaptive music players, expressive performance software, therapy, and games.
