Active acoustic enhancement (AAE) is a way of changing how a real, physical room sounds while people are actually inside it, using microphones, digital signal processing, and loudspeakers installed in the room. Microphones pick up the sound happening on stage, a computer adds extra, carefully controlled reflections and reverberation to it, and loudspeakers hidden around the room play that processed sound back in real time — on top of the room's own natural acoustics. This differs from artificial reverberation added to a recording afterward: AAE changes the live, in-room listening experience itself, which lets a venue that was built for speech or sports (with a "dry," non-echoey sound) also work well for orchestral concerts, or lets one hall be tuned for very different kinds of music.
