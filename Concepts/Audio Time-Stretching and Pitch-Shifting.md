concept: Audio Time-Stretching and Pitch-Shifting
date_first_seen: 2026-08-09
---

Time-stretching means changing how fast or slow an audio recording plays back without changing how high or low it sounds (its pitch) — useful for matching two songs to the same tempo for DJ mixing, or fitting a piece of music to a fixed video length. Pitch-shifting is the reverse: changing how high or low something sounds without changing its speed, for example transposing a vocal recording into a different key. Doing either well is hard, because naively speeding up or slowing down a recording changes both speed and pitch together (like a record played at the wrong RPM); good algorithms have to separate a sound's timing from its pitch so each can be adjusted independently, while avoiding robotic or "underwater" sounding artifacts.
