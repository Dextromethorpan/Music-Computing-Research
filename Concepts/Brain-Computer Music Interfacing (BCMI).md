Brain-Computer Music Interfacing (BCMI) is technology that lets a person control or generate music using signals detected directly from their brain, instead of moving a hand or pressing a key. Usually this works by placing electrodes on the scalp (a technique called EEG, or electroencephalography) to pick up patterns of electrical activity, which software then translates into musical decisions — like triggering notes, changing tempo, or shaping the mood of a piece. It was originally developed so that people with severe motor impairments (who cannot move their hands well enough to play a normal instrument) could still create and perform music, but it has since grown into its own area of creative and clinical research.
