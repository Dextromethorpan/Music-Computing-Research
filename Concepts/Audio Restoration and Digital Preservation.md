Old audio recordings — especially analogue magnetic tape — physically decay over time and can be lost forever if nothing is done. Audio restoration and digital preservation uses computing to rescue this material: digitizing tapes before they degrade further, automatically detecting problems like splices (places where tape was physically cut and joined) or dropouts, and figuring out the correct playback settings (like equalization, the balance of low and high tones) so the digital copy sounds as close as possible to the original. It matters because it's often the only way to save historically important music and sound recordings before the physical media becomes unusable.
