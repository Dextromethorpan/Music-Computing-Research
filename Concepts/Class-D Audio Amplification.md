date_first_seen: 2026-08-09

An audio amplifier boosts a weak electrical signal so it's strong enough to drive a loudspeaker. A "Class-D" amplifier does this by rapidly switching the output on and off (like a very fast digital on/off signal) rather than smoothly varying it the way older amplifier designs do — this makes Class-D amplifiers much more energy-efficient and produce less waste heat, which matters a lot for battery-powered speakers, phones, and compact devices. The engineering challenge is keeping the sound clean despite this switching process, usually solved with careful digital feedback circuits that continuously check and correct the actual output.
