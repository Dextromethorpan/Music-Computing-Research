Artificial reverberation is technology that adds or matches the echo-like "room sound" (reverb) of a space to a recording using software or hardware, rather than actually recording in that physical space. Early versions used physical devices like metal plates or springs to create the effect; modern versions use digital algorithms, including ones trained with machine learning, that can analyze a reference recording and figure out what room it was recorded in, then apply a matching artificial reverb to another track so the two sound like they belong in the same space. This is useful in film/TV post-production (making dialogue recorded on different days or locations sound consistent), music production, and game/VR audio.
