Sound Studies is a humanities field that treats sound and listening as cultural and social phenomena, not just physical or technical ones. Instead of asking "how do we measure this sound?" (the psychoacoustics/engineering question), it asks things like "how does sound shape identity, memory, and power?", "what does a particular sound mean to a particular community?", or "how has our relationship to listening changed with new technology?" Researchers in this field come from musicology, anthropology, media studies, and art history, and they study everything from voice assistants to sound art installations to protest chants. It matters for music computing because it provides the cultural and critical context for why people build the tools and technologies they do.
