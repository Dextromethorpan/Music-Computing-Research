The source-filter model is a classic and influential way of describing how many sounds — especially the human voice — are produced: an initial "excitation" source (like vocal cord vibration) creates a raw buzz-like sound, which then passes through a resonant "filter" (like the shape of the mouth and throat) that shapes it into a recognizable vowel or tone. It's widely used as the basis for both analyzing and synthesizing voices.
