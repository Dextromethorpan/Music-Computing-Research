Audio fingerprinting is a technique for turning a piece of audio into a compact, distinctive "fingerprint" — a short summary of its most identifiable features — so a computer can recognize the same recording later, even from a noisy few-second clip. The classic approach (invented for Shazam) finds the loudest points in a song's spectrogram (a picture of which frequencies are loud at which moments), then encodes pairs of those points into hash codes. To identify an unknown clip, the same process is run on it and its codes are checked against a giant pre-built database, looking for many matches that line up in time. This is what lets an app "hear" a few seconds of a song playing nearby and tell you its title almost instantly, even over background noise or a bad phone microphone.
