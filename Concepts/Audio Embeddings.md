An audio embedding is a way of turning a sound clip into a short list of numbers that captures "what the sound is about" — its style, content, or character — without needing a human to label it first. Think of it like a fingerprint: two similar-sounding clips get similar number-lists, while very different sounds get very different ones. Researchers create embeddings by training a neural network (a machine-learning model) on lots of audio, then reusing that trained model as a shortcut for other tasks, instead of building a brand-new AI system from scratch every time. This saves enormous effort and is now a standard building block across sound search, music recommendation, and sound-event recognition systems.
