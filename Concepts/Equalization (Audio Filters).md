Equalization (often shortened to "EQ") is the process of adjusting the balance of different frequencies in a sound — for example, boosting the bass, cutting a harsh high-frequency ring, or carving out space for a vocal in a busy mix. It is done using filters, which are pieces of math (implemented in software or electronic hardware) that let some frequencies through unchanged while turning others up or down. Equalization shows up everywhere in audio technology: music mixing and mastering, hearing aids that compensate for a person's specific hearing loss, loudspeaker correction that compensates for how a particular speaker or room distorts sound, and headphone tuning. Designing a good equalizer involves trade-offs — for instance, a filter that targets a frequency very precisely often needs more computation and can introduce a small delay, so engineers must balance accuracy against speed and simplicity.
