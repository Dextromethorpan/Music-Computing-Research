A Head-Related Transfer Function (HRTF) describes how the shape of a person's head, torso, and outer ear (pinna) changes a sound's frequencies depending on which direction it comes from. Because everyone's ears and head are shaped slightly differently, sound reaching each eardrum is filtered in a personal way — and this filtering is actually one of the main things the brain uses to figure out where a sound is coming from. In audio computing, HRTFs are measured (or estimated) for a person or a dummy head, then used to process ("filter") a sound so that when played over headphones it seems to come from a specific point in 3D space around the listener — the technical basis for realistic spatial/3D audio and binaural sound in VR, games, and immersive music mixes.
