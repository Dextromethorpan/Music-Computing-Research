date_first_seen: 2026-08-09

Aliasing is a digital audio glitch that happens when a sound contains frequencies higher than a computer's sampling rate can accurately capture or reproduce. Those too-high frequencies don't just disappear — they "fold back" and show up as new, unwanted, often harsh-sounding tones mixed into the audio. Digital synthesizers that emulate old analog gear have to work hard to avoid or filter out aliasing so the emulation stays clean.
