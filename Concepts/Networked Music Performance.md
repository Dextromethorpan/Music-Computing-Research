Networked music performance is the practice of musicians playing together live while located in different physical places, connected only by an internet or network link that carries their audio (and sometimes video) to each other in real time. The central technical challenge is "latency" — the delay it takes sound to travel from one player's microphone to another player's speaker — because even delays as small as a few tens of milliseconds can make it hard for musicians to stay in rhythm together. Researchers build specialized software (rather than using ordinary video-call apps, which are usually too slow and too compressed) to minimize this delay and maximize audio quality, enabling remote rehearsals, lessons, and cross-continent concerts.
