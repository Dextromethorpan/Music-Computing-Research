concept: Real-Time Sound Synthesis
date_first_seen: 2026-08-10
---

Real-time sound synthesis means generating audio on the fly, fast enough that a listener (or a performer) hears the result essentially instantly, rather than waiting for a computer to render a sound file and play it back afterward. This is what happens inside a software synthesizer or a live-coding language like ChucK: the program is computing the actual waveform of the sound continuously, sample by sample, while a musician is actively playing or editing it. It's technically demanding because the computer has a strict, unforgiving deadline — if it can't produce the next slice of audio in time, listeners hear clicks, glitches, or silence — which is why programming languages and systems built for this purpose are designed around precise timing guarantees.
