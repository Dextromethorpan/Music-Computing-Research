Virtual analog modeling is the practice of making a computer imitate the sound of analog (electronic, non-digital) music gear — like a vintage guitar amplifier, a distortion pedal, or an old analog synthesizer circuit — so that a musician can get that classic sound from software instead of owning the original hardware. There are two broad approaches. In "white-box" modeling, engineers study the actual electronic circuit and simulate its physics and math directly. In "black-box" modeling, which has become much more common with machine learning, a neural network (a model that learns patterns from example data) simply listens to recordings of the real device's input and output and learns to imitate its behavior, without anyone needing to know exactly how the circuit works inside. Good virtual analog models need to run fast enough to process a live instrument signal in real time, so a musician playing through the plug-in doesn't notice any delay.
