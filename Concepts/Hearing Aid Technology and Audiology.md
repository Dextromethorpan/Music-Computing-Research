Audiology is the science of hearing and hearing loss, and hearing aids are the main technology built on top of it. A modern hearing aid is a tiny, real-time audio computer: it must pick up sound, decide what's noise and what's speech, reshape the sound to compensate for exactly how a particular person's hearing is damaged, and do all of this with almost no delay, on a battery the size of a coin. This concept covers the engineering side (signal processing, AI-based noise reduction, own-voice detection, chip design) as well as the clinical and outcomes side (how well a hearing aid actually helps someone in daily life, how satisfied they are, how much mental effort listening still takes). Companies like GN, Demant/Oticon, and WS Audiology/Widex run dedicated research units combining both angles.
