concept: Audio-over-IP Broadcast Infrastructure
date_first_seen: 2026-08-13
---

Traditionally, professional audio equipment (in TV studios, live venues, etc.) was connected with dedicated cables carrying one signal each. Audio-over-IP infrastructure instead sends many channels of audio over standard computer networking equipment, the same kind used for internet traffic, using specialized standards like AES67 and SMPTE ST 2110-30 that guarantee very low, predictable delay and keep audio tightly synchronized with picture. Building this kind of infrastructure well requires solving unglamorous but important engineering problems: keeping delay extremely low and consistent, converting between older formats like Dolby E and newer network formats without audible glitches, and measuring/correcting loudness so broadcasts meet regulatory standards.
