Absolute pitch (sometimes called "perfect pitch") is the rare ability to identify or reproduce a musical note just by hearing it, without needing a reference note to compare it to — for example, immediately knowing that a sound is a "B-flat." It is uncommon among non-musicians in Western countries (roughly 1 in 10,000 people) but much more common among professional musicians and among speakers of "tonal languages" (languages where changing the pitch of a syllable changes its meaning, common across parts of Asia). Researchers study absolute pitch both to understand how the brain represents musical pitch and because it offers a clear, testable example of how nature (innate wiring) and nurture (musical or linguistic training) might combine to produce a specific mental skill.
