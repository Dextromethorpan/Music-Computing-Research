concept: Acoustic Metamaterials
date_first_seen: 2026-08-09
---

An acoustic metamaterial is a material or structure that gets its unusual sound-related behavior from how it's physically arranged, rather than from what it's chemically made of. For example, attaching a careful pattern of small magnets or resonators onto a drumhead can create a zone that blocks, or "traps," certain frequencies from passing through it — something an ordinary, uniformly-built drumhead cannot do. Researchers use this idea to redesign musical instruments so a single instrument can produce genuinely new or controllable timbres, going beyond what its natural materials and shape would normally allow.
