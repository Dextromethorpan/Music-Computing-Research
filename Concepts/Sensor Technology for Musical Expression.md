A sensor is a small component that converts a physical action — pressure, bending, tilting, stretching, proximity — into an electrical signal a computer can read. This is the foundational hardware layer underneath every digital musical instrument: before any sound can be shaped by a gesture, that gesture has to be measured by some kind of sensor, then usually cleaned up and translated into a usable control value (see also mapping and gesture-based interaction). This concept covers the study and practice of choosing, building, and calibrating those sensors specifically for musical use — where the demands are unusual: sensors often need to respond fast enough to feel "live," survive being handled roughly on stage, and capture the kind of nuance (small changes in pressure or angle) that lets a performer be genuinely expressive rather than just triggering on/off events.
