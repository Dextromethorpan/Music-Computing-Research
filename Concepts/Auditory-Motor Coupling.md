concept: Auditory-Motor Coupling
date_first_seen: 2026-08-10

Auditory-motor coupling is the close, two-way link in the brain between hearing sound and controlling physical movement — it's why tapping your foot to a beat feels almost automatic, and why a drummer's hands can stay locked to a rhythm they hear. Neuroscientists study this by watching brain activity (with tools like EEG or MEG) while people listen to or make music, to see how the parts of the brain that process sound and the parts that plan movement talk to each other. This matters for music computing because it helps explain how musicians synchronize with each other or with a machine, and it informs technology like rehabilitation tools that use rhythm to help retrain movement after conditions such as a stroke.
