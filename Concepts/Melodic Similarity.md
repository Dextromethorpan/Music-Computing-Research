Melodic similarity is a way of measuring how alike two melodies are — useful for finding variants of the same folk song passed down differently in different regions, spotting when a modern song borrows a melody from an older one, or organizing a large archive of tunes so similar ones are grouped together. Computing this automatically is tricky because two melodies can be "the same" even with different notes, rhythms, or keys, so researchers build mathematical models of what parts of a melody matter most for a listener's sense of similarity.
