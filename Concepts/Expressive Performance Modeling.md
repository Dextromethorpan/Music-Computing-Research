Expressive performance modeling is the study of the small, deliberate deviations a human musician makes from the literal notated score — subtle changes in timing (speeding up or slowing down, called tempo rubato), loudness, and articulation — that turn a mechanically exact rendition into an emotionally compelling performance. Researchers in this area use machine learning and data mining on recordings of real performances (often piano, since it can be captured precisely via special "reproducing" instruments) to try to find and explain the hidden patterns behind expert musical expression, and sometimes to generate expressive performances automatically.
