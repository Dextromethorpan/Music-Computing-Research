Audio similarity is the task of getting a computer to judge how much two pieces of music "sound alike" by analyzing their actual audio signal — not their metadata (artist, genre tags) and not how listeners have behaved toward them. Typical approaches extract numeric "features" describing timbre, rhythm, or loudness patterns from each track and then measure the distance between those numbers. It underlies music recommendation, automatic playlist generation, and tools for browsing large unlabeled music collections. A well-known pitfall in this area is "hubness": certain tracks end up mathematically close to almost everything due to quirks of comparing many numeric features at once, so they get wrongly recommended constantly regardless of whether they actually sound similar.
