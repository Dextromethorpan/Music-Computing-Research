Music and spoken language share more than people often realize: both use patterns of pitch, rhythm, and timing to convey meaning and emotion. The rising and falling pitch of a spoken sentence is called "prosody," or sometimes informally the "melody of speech." Researchers in this area study whether skills used in music — like perceiving and remembering pitch patterns — transfer to language abilities, such as learning a foreign language or a "tonal language" (a language, common in parts of Asia, where the pitch pattern of a syllable changes its meaning). This overlap suggests that musical training and language ability may draw on shared brain mechanisms.
