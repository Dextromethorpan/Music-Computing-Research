Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) is a technique used in headphones and earphones to reduce unwanted outside noise electronically, rather than only blocking it physically with padding. A small microphone built into the headphone "listens" to the incoming noise, and the device's electronics generate an inverted copy of that noise's sound wave and play it through the speaker. When the two waves meet, they cancel each other out (the same way two matching ripples on water can flatten into stillness), so the listener hears much less of the original noise. Getting this to work well is tricky in practice: the moment a headphone's seal against the ear shifts slightly, or manufacturing tolerances vary slightly between units, the cancellation can become inaccurate — so companies build calibration and adaptive-filter systems to keep it accurate.
