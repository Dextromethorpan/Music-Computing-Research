concept: Consonance and Dissonance
date_first_seen: 2026-08-09

Consonance and dissonance describe how pleasant/stable ("consonant") or tense/unstable ("dissonant") a combination of musical notes sounds to a listener. For example, two notes an octave apart usually sound very consonant, while two notes a semitone apart often sound dissonant and "want" to resolve to something calmer. Researchers have long debated why this happens — whether it's mostly a matter of physics and how our ears process overlapping sound waves (a psychoacoustic explanation), or mostly a matter of what we're used to hearing in our culture (a learned explanation). Understanding consonance and dissonance is central to explaining musical tonality (the sense of a "home" key or note) and to building computer models that judge or generate harmony.
