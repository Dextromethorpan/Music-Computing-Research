Machine listening is the broad idea of building computer systems that can make sense of everyday sound the way a person does just by listening — recognising what's happening (a door closing, birdsong, traffic), where a sound is coming from, or what kind of environment a recording was made in. It's an umbrella term covering more specific tasks like sound event detection (spotting individual sounds), acoustic scene classification (labelling the overall environment), and audio captioning (describing a clip in a sentence). Unlike speech recognition (which focuses on words) or music information retrieval (which focuses on musical content), machine listening is mostly concerned with the ordinary, non-speech, non-music sounds that fill everyday environments.
