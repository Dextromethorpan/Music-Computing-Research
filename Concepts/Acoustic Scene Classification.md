Acoustic scene classification is the task of teaching a computer to recognise *where* a recording was made just from the sound of it — for example, telling apart "a busy street", "a quiet office", "a park", or "a metro station" using only audio, with no camera or GPS involved. Unlike sound event detection (which spots individual sounds like a dog bark or a car horn), scene classification looks at the overall "sound texture" of an environment — its background noise, reverberation, and mix of typical sounds — to label the whole clip with a single category. It is one of the founding tasks of the DCASE (Detection and Classification of Acoustic Scenes and Events) challenge and has practical uses in context-aware devices, such as a hearing aid or phone that automatically adjusts its settings depending on whether you're in a quiet room or a noisy street.
