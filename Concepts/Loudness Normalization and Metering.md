"Loudness" is how loud a sound seems to a human listener, which is not the same thing as its raw signal level (its peak or average electrical value) — two clips can measure the same on a simple volume meter yet sound very differently loud to your ears, depending on frequency content and dynamics. Loudness metering is the practice of measuring audio using algorithms (like ITU-R BS.1770) that model human hearing more closely than a basic peak meter, so engineers can predict how loud something will actually sound. Loudness normalization then uses those measurements to automatically adjust different pieces of audio (songs, ads, TV programs) so they all play back at a similar perceived loudness, which is why you no longer have to constantly reach for the volume knob when a loud commercial follows a quiet TV scene, or when switching between tracks on a streaming service.
