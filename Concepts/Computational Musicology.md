Computational musicology is the practice of studying music history, style, and theory using computer programs instead of (or alongside) manual score-by-score analysis. Instead of a scholar comparing a handful of pieces by ear or by eye, a computer can systematically scan thousands of melodies or scores looking for patterns — for example, tracking how a particular melodic phrase spread and changed across hundreds of folk songs, or testing whether a music-theory rule actually holds up across a large, real dataset rather than a few hand-picked examples. It sits at the intersection of musicology (the academic study of music) and computer science.
