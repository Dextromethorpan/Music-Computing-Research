Music therapy is a healthcare practice where a trained therapist uses music (listening, playing, singing, or improvising) to help a patient work toward non-musical goals, such as improving mood, communication, or motor skills after an injury. It's relevant to music computing because therapists currently judge a session's progress mostly by ear, which is time-consuming and depends heavily on individual judgment — researchers are now exploring whether computer tools that automatically measure things like rhythm, pitch, and musical interaction could help therapists track progress more consistently.
