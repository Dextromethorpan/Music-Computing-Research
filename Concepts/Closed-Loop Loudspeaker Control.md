---
concept: Closed-Loop Loudspeaker Control
date_first_seen: 2026-08-12
---

Most loudspeaker amplifiers work "open-loop" — they send an electrical signal to the speaker and hope it moves the way it's supposed to, without checking. Closed-loop loudspeaker control instead measures what the speaker is actually doing in real time (for example, the air pressure created on each side of its moving cone, or "membrane") and feeds that measurement back into the amplifier's circuitry to correct errors on the fly — similar to how a thermostat reads the room's temperature and adjusts the heater accordingly. It matters because it lets a loudspeaker produce louder, more accurate sound with less distortion than an open-loop system could achieve from the same-sized hardware.
