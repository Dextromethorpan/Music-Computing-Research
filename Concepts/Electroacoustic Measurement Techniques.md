concept: Electroacoustic Measurement Techniques
date_first_seen: 2026-08-12

To design or evaluate a loudspeaker, microphone, or room, engineers need a reliable way to measure how it actually responds to sound — its "impulse response," the acoustic fingerprint left behind after a very brief test sound is played through it. Electroacoustic measurement techniques are the methods used to capture that fingerprint accurately: playing carefully designed test signals (like swept tones, pulses, or maximum-length pseudo-random sequences called MLS) through a device or space and mathematically extracting its response, while dealing with real-world problems like background noise, nonlinear distortion, and time drift between the loudspeaker and the microphone. These techniques are the practical backbone behind almost everything else in loudspeaker and room-acoustics research: you can't optimize what you can't measure precisely.
