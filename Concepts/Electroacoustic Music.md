Electroacoustic music is music made using electronic equipment — originally tape machines, oscillators, and hand-wired analogue synthesizer circuits, and later computers — rather than only acoustic instruments played live. It's one of the oldest branches of what's now called music computing, going back to mid-20th-century studios where composers physically spliced tape and patched circuits together to create sounds impossible to make with traditional instruments. Many institutions studying electroacoustic music today combine that historical, hands-on studio tradition with modern digital tools.
