concept: Voltage-Controlled Filter (Analog Synthesis)
date_first_seen: 2026-08-13

A filter shapes a sound by letting some pitches through and blocking others, like a tone knob. A voltage-controlled filter (VCF) does this automatically: instead of a person turning a knob, an electrical control voltage moves the filter's cutoff point, so a synthesizer can "sweep" the brightness of a sound over time or in response to how a note is played. This is one of the three classic building blocks of an analog synthesizer (alongside the oscillator that generates the raw tone and the amplifier that controls loudness), and specific circuit designs for building a VCF — like Bob Moog's transistor "ladder" design — became famous enough that they're still used today, decades after they were first patented.
