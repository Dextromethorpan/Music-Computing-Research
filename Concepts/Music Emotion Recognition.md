Music emotion recognition is the effort to build software that can automatically judge how a piece of music makes people feel — for example, whether it sounds happy, sad, tense, or calm. Researchers usually collect ratings from human listeners (sometimes on a 2D map of "how positive" versus "how energetic" a song feels) and then train machine learning models to predict those ratings from audio features like tempo, harmony, and timbre (the "color" or texture of a sound). This research underlies practical technology like mood-based playlists and music recommendation systems, and more advanced versions try to track how a song's emotional character shifts moment-to-moment rather than assigning just one label to a whole track.
