Automatic "mood detection" algorithms try to guess how a song makes people feel (happy, sad, angry, and so on) just from the audio. This study asked 166 listeners in Brazil, South Korea, and the US to rate the mood of 360 recent pop songs, using mood words translated to mean the same thing across Portuguese, Korean, and English. The researchers then compared these human ratings across cultures, and checked how well four existing automatic mood-detection algorithms matched what real listeners from different cultures actually felt. It was presented as an oral talk at ISMIR 2021, showing that mood perception isn't fully universal and that algorithms trained mostly on Western data may not generalize well.
