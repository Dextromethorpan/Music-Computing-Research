When a virtual or augmented-reality audio system slightly changes how a room "sounds" — its reverberation, its echoes — can listeners actually tell the difference? This paper builds a statistical model (a "generalized linear mixed model," a tool for predicting yes/no human judgments from several combined factors) trained on real listening-test data, to predict when people will notice these small acoustic changes. It matters for VR and AR audio designers, who need to know how much they can simplify or approximate a room's acoustics before a listener notices something is "off."
