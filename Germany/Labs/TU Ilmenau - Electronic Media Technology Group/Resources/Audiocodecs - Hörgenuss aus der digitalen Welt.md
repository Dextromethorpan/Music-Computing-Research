This German-language book chapter title translates to "Audio codecs: listening pleasure from the digital world." It is written by Karlheinz Brandenburg, the co-inventor of the mp3 format, explaining directly how digital audio compression ("codecs") works: taking a huge raw digital audio file and shrinking it dramatically — as mp3 does, by roughly ten times — while keeping it sounding good, by exploiting what the human ear can and cannot actually perceive (psychoacoustics). It's a general-audience explainer of the core idea behind Brandenburg's life's work, written for a broader "digitalization" reference book rather than a specialist journal.
