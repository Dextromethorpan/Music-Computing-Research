"Room acoustical simulation" means using a computer model to predict how sound will behave in a room — its echoes, reverberation, and clarity — before or without measuring it in real life, which is useful for concert hall design. "Auralization" is turning that simulation into an actual audio clip you can listen to, as if you were standing in the room. In this study, several different simulation software packages were tested against real microphone recordings of the same rooms to see how close the computer predictions come to reality. The result: most current simulation tools get the general "room sound" roughly right but still make audible errors, especially around early echoes and off-center sound source positions, meaning virtual acoustic simulations are useful but not yet perfectly realistic.
