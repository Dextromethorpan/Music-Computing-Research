Written by the institute's founding visionary while ZKM was still under construction, this essay lays out what the Institute for Music and Acoustics was meant to be: a place where artistic composition with computers and rigorous acoustic/psychoacoustic research would happen side by side. It discusses the history of computer-assisted composition (from 1960s mainframes to the MIDI standard), a planned collaboration with Stanford's CCRMA on shared composition software (Common Music), the need for both "open" programmable systems and "off-the-shelf" electronic instruments, and the plan to build entirely new instruments where none yet exist. It's a useful, plain-language window into why a place like ZKM's music research institute exists at all.
