A public call from the Chair of Acoustics and Haptics recruiting violinists as test subjects for listening/playing experiments about violin sound quality. This kind of study asks trained musicians to judge or compare how different violins sound and feel to play, which helps researchers connect physical, measurable properties of an instrument (like its vibration or radiated sound) to the subjective, hard-to-quantify judgment of "this violin sounds good." It's a concrete example of the chair's broader research into how auditory and haptic (touch/vibration) perception combine when playing an instrument.
