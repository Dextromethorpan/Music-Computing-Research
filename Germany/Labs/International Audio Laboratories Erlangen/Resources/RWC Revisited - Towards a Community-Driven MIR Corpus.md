The RWC (Real World Computing) Music Database is one of the oldest and most widely used shared collections of recordings in music research — a set of copyright-cleared songs that many labs use so their results can be fairly compared against each other. This paper looks back at RWC's nearly 25-year history, including an interview with its original creator, and proposes ways the research community can keep such shared datasets alive and genuinely useful for decades, including releasing updated annotations on GitHub and Zenodo. It's a good example of the "infrastructure" side of Music Information Retrieval (MIR) research — building shared benchmarks that everyone can test their algorithms against, rather than just building new algorithms.
