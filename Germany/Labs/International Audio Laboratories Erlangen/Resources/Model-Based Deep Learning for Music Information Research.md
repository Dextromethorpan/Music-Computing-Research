Deep learning (neural networks trained on large amounts of data) is powerful but often acts like a "black box" — it's hard to know why it made a particular decision. This paper, co-authored by AudioLabs' Meinard Müller together with researchers from Télécom Paris and Academia Sinica, argues for combining deep learning with existing scientific knowledge about sound and music — such as the physics of instruments, music theory, or how human hearing works — to build hybrid "model-based deep learning" systems. The payoff is AI models for music analysis that are more explainable, more controllable, and need less training data and compute, illustrated with concrete music-analysis examples.
