When people listen to music in different rooms or concert halls, they perceive the "room sound" — reverberation, echo, spaciousness — differently, but most past research relied on people describing these impressions in words, which can be imprecise. This study instead used Multi-Dimensional Scaling, a statistical technique that turns similarity-and-difference judgments into a map of a few underlying dimensions, letting listeners compare recordings played through different room responses without needing verbal labels. They found that perception of room acoustics boils down to five measurable dimensions: echo density, a "fractal correlation dimension" (a mathematical roughness measure), roughness, loudness, and early decay time. This connects psychoacoustics directly to objective acoustic measurements useful for concert hall design.
