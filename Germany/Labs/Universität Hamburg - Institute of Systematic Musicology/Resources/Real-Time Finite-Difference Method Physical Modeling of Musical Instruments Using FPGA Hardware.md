"Physical modeling" means simulating an instrument's actual physics — its shape, materials, and how it vibrates — on a computer to predict or synthesize its sound, rather than just recording samples. A common technique for this, the Finite-Difference Method, represents the instrument as a grid of points and calculates how vibrations move between them step by step, but this is normally too slow to run in real time on an ordinary computer. This paper shows how to run such simulations on an FPGA — a special reprogrammable computer chip that can process many calculations at once — fast enough for real-time, playable virtual instruments. It is a foundational hardware-acceleration paper for the group's decades-long work on physical modeling of instruments like guitars and banjos.
