A "metamaterial" is a material or structure engineered so its physical arrangement — not its chemistry — gives it unusual wave behavior. Here, the researchers glued small rings of magnets onto a hand drum's plastic membrane, creating a zone that traps certain sound frequencies inside it. Using laser measurements and a microphone array (a grid of microphones that maps how sound radiates from a surface), they showed the drum produces a genuinely new sound — a "band gap," a frequency range that gets suppressed — when struck inside the ring, while striking outside still sounds like a normal drum. This means a single instrument can be given two very different sound characters just by choosing where you hit it, showing how physics-based redesign can expand what a purely acoustic instrument can sound like.
