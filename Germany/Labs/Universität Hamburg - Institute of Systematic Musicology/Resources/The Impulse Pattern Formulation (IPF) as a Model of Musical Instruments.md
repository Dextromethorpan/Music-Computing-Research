When you play an instrument, physical "impulses" — short bursts of pressure or force — bounce back and forth inside it. For example, a saxophone reed's pulse travels down the tube, reflects, and comes back to restart the cycle. Impulse Pattern Formulation (IPF) is a simplified mathematical model that tracks just these back-and-forth impulses instead of simulating the entire physical instrument in full detail, making it much faster to compute. This paper tests how stable the model is and where it breaks down, showing it can also reproduce realistic, sometimes chaotic behavior like multiphonics — an instrument producing more than one pitch at once. IPF later became one of the group's signature modeling tools, later even extended to modeling neural and social systems.
