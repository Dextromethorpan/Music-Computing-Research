"Auralization" means computing what a room would sound like and then playing that simulated sound back through headphones or speakers, so you can listen to a space that may not even exist yet. This paper looks at using auralization in real time for musicians themselves while they perform — for example, letting a guitarist hear, live, how their instrument would sound on a concert-hall stage that doesn't physically exist. The researchers define what "real-time" auralization for performers actually requires (very low delay, accurate room and stage acoustics) and run a pilot study with pairs of guitarists playing duets under these simulated conditions. The goal is to let musicians rehearse or audition venues virtually, and to better understand how room acoustics affect how musicians play together.
