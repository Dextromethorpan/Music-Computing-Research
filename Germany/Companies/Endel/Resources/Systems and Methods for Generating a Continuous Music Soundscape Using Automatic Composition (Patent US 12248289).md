This patent describes the more detailed mechanics behind Endel's "Pacific" sound engine: sensor data about the user and their environment is used to automatically pick two or more short sound sections from a library, arrange them into a "sound phase," and then chain several phases together into one continuous, ever-changing soundscape that's delivered to the listener's device. Rather than looping a single pre-made ambient track, the software is effectively composing a new, never-repeating piece on the fly by rearranging pre-designed building blocks according to real-time input. This is the technical basis for Endel's claim that no two listening sessions are exactly the same.
