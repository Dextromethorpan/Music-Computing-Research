This article summarizes an independent study, run by the brain-data company Arctop and supported by several music companies including Endel, that measured people's brain activity while they used Endel's "Focus" soundscape, listened to regular focus playlists, or sat in silence while doing everyday tasks. The study reported that people listening to Endel's soundscape kept their focus for longer and more consistently than with playlists or silence — up to seven times better by the researchers' measure. It's an example of a music-tech company partnering with outside scientists to test whether its product actually does what it claims, rather than relying only on internal marketing claims.
