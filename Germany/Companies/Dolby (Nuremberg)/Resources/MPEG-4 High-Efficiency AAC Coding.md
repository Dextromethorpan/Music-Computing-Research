This paper (published in IEEE Signal Processing Magazine as part of a "Standards in a Nutshell" series) explains High-Efficiency AAC (HE-AAC), the audio compression format that lets streaming radio, mobile networks, and digital broadcasting deliver decent-sounding music at very low data rates. Its key trick, called Spectral Band Replication (SBR), is based on the observation that a listener's ear is much less sensitive to fine detail in high-pitched sounds — so instead of transmitting the high frequencies directly, the encoder sends a small amount of guidance data, and the decoder regenerates ("replicates") the missing high end from the lower frequencies it did fully transmit. Martin Dietz was a lead engineer at Coding Technologies, the German-founded company (later renamed Dolby Germany GmbH, based in Nuremberg) that invented SBR and turned it into the aacPlus/HE-AAC format now used worldwide in digital radio (DAB+, HD Radio, Digital Radio Mondiale) and mobile streaming. His co-author, Jürgen Herre, worked at the neighboring Fraunhofer Institute in Erlangen — the paper is a direct product of the close-knit audio-coding research scene in the Nuremberg/Erlangen area of Germany that produced both MP3 and HE-AAC.
