Presented at the 131st Audio Engineering Society (AES) Convention, this paper describes an improved, lower-computational-cost version of Spectral Band Replication (SBR) — the high-frequency-regeneration trick behind Dolby's HE-AAC audio codec family — developed for MPEG's Unified Speech and Audio Coding (USAC) standard. It compares two ways of rebuilding the missing high frequencies of a compressed audio signal: a Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) based method that sounds slightly better but costs more processing power, and a Quadrature Mirror Filter (QMF) based method that is much cheaper to run on small devices while still passing listening tests. The paper is a joint effort between Dolby Sweden and the Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Circuits IIS (in Erlangen, next to Nuremberg), illustrating how closely Dolby's audio-coding engineers and the German Fraunhofer audio-codec research group have worked together on the successors to HE-AAC.
