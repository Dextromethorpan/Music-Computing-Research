This technical whitepaper, published by Coding Technologies (the company whose German subsidiary later became Dolby Germany GmbH, headquartered in Nuremberg), explains "aacPlus," the commercial name for what became the MPEG-4 HE-AAC audio codec. In plain terms, it describes how a piece of audio can be squeezed to a much smaller file size than plain MP3 while still sounding reasonably full, by separating a signal into a "core" band handled by ordinary AAC compression and a high-frequency band that is regenerated at playback time using the Spectral Band Replication (SBR) technique. The document is aimed at engineers integrating the codec into hardware and software, but its explanations of why the technique works perceptually (how human hearing is less sensitive to precision in higher frequencies) are accessible to a beginner. It's a first-hand primary source from the exact company whose German engineering team became part of Dolby.
