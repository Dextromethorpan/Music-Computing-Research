The U 47, introduced in 1949, was the first microphone to let an engineer electronically switch its pickup pattern (cardioid, which picks up mainly from the front, versus omnidirectional, which picks up from all directions) at the flip of a switch, instead of physically swapping parts. It paired this with a very low self-noise design built around the rare Telefunken VF 14 vacuum tube. Because of its clear, slightly flattering high-mid-range tone, the U 47 became the standard vocal microphone behind countless classic recordings and is still considered a benchmark almost 80 years later — a good example of how a single, careful piece of electro-acoustic engineering can define an industry's sound for generations.
