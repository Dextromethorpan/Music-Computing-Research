This blog post looks back over zplane's 25-year history since three electrical-engineering students in Berlin founded the company in 2000. It traces how the company grew from building custom time-stretching software for a single client into a technology supplier whose audio engines are licensed inside major music software used by millions of people, and touches on the shift from hand-built signal-processing algorithms toward newer neural-network-based tools. It's a useful overview for understanding how a small research-driven audio company can end up as invisible infrastructure inside many different products.
