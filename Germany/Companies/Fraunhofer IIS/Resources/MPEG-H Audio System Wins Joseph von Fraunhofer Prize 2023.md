This press release explains why the Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft's internal Joseph von Fraunhofer Prize 2023 (worth 50,000 euros) went to the team behind MPEG-H Audio. Instead of recording a fixed mix of speakers and background sound, MPEG-H stores sound as separate "audio objects" (for example, one commentator's voice, the crowd noise, the music bed), each carrying metadata describing where it should sit in 3D space and how loud it should be. Only at the moment of playback are these objects combined into actual loudspeaker or headphone signals, based on the listener's device and preferences. That's what lets a viewer turn up dialogue, pick a different commentary language, or quiet the crowd noise in a live broadcast. The team — about 50 to 100 engineers at peak — worked on this system for over a decade, and it is now the mandatory TV audio standard in Brazil and used in Sony's 360 Reality Audio music format.
