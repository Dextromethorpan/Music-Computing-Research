This technical whitepaper explains xHE-AAC ("extended High-Efficiency AAC"), the newest member of Fraunhofer's AAC audio codec family. A codec is a piece of software that squeezes audio into a much smaller file size (compression) and then rebuilds it for playback (decompression). What makes xHE-AAC special is that it works well across an unusually wide range of bitrates (how many bits per second are spent on the audio) — from very low-bandwidth mobile streaming up to near-transparent quality — inside a single codec, and it can automatically adjust in real time as a listener's network connection changes. The document is aimed at engineers integrating the codec into streaming platforms, and it is why services like Netflix and Instagram Reels use xHE-AAC today. It builds directly on decades of Fraunhofer IIS's earlier codec work (mp3, AAC).
