DCASE (Detection and Classification of Acoustic Scenes and Events) is a yearly research challenge and community — started in 2013 and organized in large part by Tampere University's Audio Research Group — that asks teams around the world to build computer systems recognising everyday sounds, like a dog barking, a car passing, or a room being "an office" versus "a park". This paper looks back over ten years of DCASE: what tasks it has run, what datasets and baseline systems it has released for free so anyone can build on them, how well AI systems have gotten at these tasks over time, and what challenges (like sounds that are rare, ambiguous, or hard to label) remain unsolved. It's a good single overview of how far machine "hearing" of ordinary sound environments has come.
