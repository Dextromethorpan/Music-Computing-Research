AVCaps is a public dataset of about 2,000 video clips (roughly 29 hours total) where people were asked to write short text descriptions ("captions") of what they experienced — but crucially, separate captions were written for the sound only, for the picture only, and for both together, by different annotators who couldn't see or hear the other modality. This lets researchers study how well an AI model can describe a video's audio and visual content separately versus combining them, and whether models trained mostly on visual captioning actually understand sound at all. It is a resource for building and testing "multimodal" AI systems — ones that need to reason about both what they hear and what they see.
