DCASE (Detection and Classification of Acoustic Scenes and Events) is an annual research challenge and community, founded in 2013 by Tuomas Virtanen and colleagues at Tampere University, focused on teaching computers to recognise everyday sounds and environments — for example, telling a "park" soundscape apart from an "office" one, or detecting specific events like glass breaking or a baby crying. Each year DCASE releases free public datasets and rules for several tasks (the tasks change year to year, and recent editions have covered things like anomaly detection in machine sounds and audio question-answering), and teams worldwide submit systems to be scored and compared. Results are presented at an associated workshop alongside conferences like ICASSP. DCASE has become one of the main reference points in the sound event detection and acoustic scene classification research field.
