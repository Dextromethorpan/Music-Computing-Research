Aquarmonio is a sound installation-sculpture-performance created by three Sibelius Academy music technology researchers, inspired by "cenotes" — natural water-filled sinkholes found in Mexico. The team built a custom electronic system that captures real water sounds with microphones, processes them live, and plays them back through speakers hidden inside four metal containers standing in for the cenotes, plus two floating speakers representing water lilies. For most of each day it worked as a self-playing sound sculpture; once daily, performers interacted with it live, adding new objects that changed how it sounded afterward. It was shown at the Helsinki Music Centre and later toured museums in Mexico. This is a hands-on example of "electroacoustic" art — music and sound art made by designing custom electronic hardware and software as part of the artwork itself, documented as practice-based ("artistic") research.
