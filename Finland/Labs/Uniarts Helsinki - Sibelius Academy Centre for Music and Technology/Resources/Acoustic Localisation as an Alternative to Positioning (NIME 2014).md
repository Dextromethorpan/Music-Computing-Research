This paper, presented at NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression, a major conference on inventing new ways to control music electronically), asks a practical question: when a musical performance system needs to track where a performer is standing or moving, do you really need cameras? "Positioning" here means figuring out someone's location in a room; most interactive music systems do this with camera-based motion capture, which is expensive and needs an unobstructed line of sight. The authors reviewed over 1,100 past NIME papers and found that "acoustic localisation" — calculating position from the time it takes sound to travel between a microphone and multiple loudspeakers, similar to how a submarine's sonar works — could have served as a cheaper, simpler alternative in roughly three-quarters of the systems reviewed, since sound can bend around obstacles that would block a camera. The paper is a good example of practical engineering research supporting live electronic performance, one of the department's focus areas.
