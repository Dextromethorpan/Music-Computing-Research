This is a doctoral research project by Charles Quevillon, a doctoral researcher in the Sibelius Academy's Arts Study Programme. It investigates how musicians emotionally and even quasi-spiritually relate to their electronic gear — electric guitars, microphones, speakers, synthesizers, computers — treating these everyday tools "as if" they were sacred objects. The method is "autoethnography," meaning the researcher studies and writes about his own lived experience and reactions as primary research data, rather than studying other people. To generate material, Quevillon created four new ritual-like sound performances, each built around one piece of electronic music technology, staging and taking apart these objects according to his own sense of their significance. It's a beginner-friendly example of how "artistic research" at a music university can combine live performance with reflective, almost anthropological writing about technology and belief.
