This is an artistic doctoral thesis from the Sibelius Academy's Centre for Music and Technology. "Electroacoustic" music is music built from electronic and electric sound sources (speakers, microphones, synthesizers, computers) rather than only acoustic instruments. The author coins the term "Case-Specific Electroacoustic Systems" to describe custom-built combinations of electronic, digital, and acoustic gear that are designed together with a specific piece of music, rather than a composer writing a piece for generic, off-the-shelf equipment. The thesis documents five original sound works (including installations, sculptures, and live performances) built this way, and reflects in writing on how designing the equipment itself becomes part of the creative, compositional process. It is an example of "artistic research" — a research method common at arts universities where the art-making itself, not just writing about it, counts as the investigation. The full exposition, including audio, video, and photo documentation, is hosted on Research Catalogue, the online platform used across Europe for this kind of practice-based research.
