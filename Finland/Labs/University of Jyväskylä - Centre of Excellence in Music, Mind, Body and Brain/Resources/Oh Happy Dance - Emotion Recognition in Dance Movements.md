Researchers recorded 60 non-professional dancers moving to 30 different songs using motion-capture cameras, then selected clips from four dancers responding to music chosen to represent happiness, anger, sadness, and tenderness. Other participants then watched silent video of the dancers (with no sound), heard only the audio, or saw both together, and rated which emotion they perceived. The result: emotions could be picked up from silent dance movement alone, though not as reliably as from the audio, and sometimes the movement and the music sent mismatched emotional signals to the viewer. This helped establish that body movement carries real, measurable emotional information independent of sound — a building block for any technology that tries to read emotion from movement rather than audio or facial expression.
