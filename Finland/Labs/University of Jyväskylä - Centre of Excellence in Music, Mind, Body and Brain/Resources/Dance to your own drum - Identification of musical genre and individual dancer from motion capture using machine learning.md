The researchers recorded many people dancing to different music genres using motion-capture cameras, then fed the resulting movement data into machine learning models (computer programs that learn patterns from examples rather than being explicitly told the rules). The models turned out to be able to guess both which genre of music someone was dancing to, and — more surprisingly — which specific individual was dancing, purely from how their body moved. In other words, everyone has a movement "fingerprint" that is at least partly consistent regardless of the music. This shows motion-capture and machine learning can extract personal and musical information hidden in something as everyday as dancing.
