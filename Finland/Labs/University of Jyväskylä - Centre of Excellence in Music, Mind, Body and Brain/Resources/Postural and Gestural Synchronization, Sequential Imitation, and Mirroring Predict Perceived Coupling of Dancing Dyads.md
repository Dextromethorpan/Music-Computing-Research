Following on from earlier CoE MMBB work on dance coordination, this study used motion-capture recordings of pairs dancing together and broke down "being in sync" into several separate computational measures: matching body posture, matching hand/arm gestures, one dancer copying the other's moves with a short delay, and mirror-image movement. By comparing these separately-computed measures against how coordinated human observers judged the pairs to look, the researchers identified which specific combination of movement patterns actually drives our impression that two people are dancing "together." This kind of quantified, componentized model of social coordination is valuable for any computer system that needs to automatically assess joint movement, from motion-based games to physical therapy tools.
