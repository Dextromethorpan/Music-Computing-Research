A paper in Applied Acoustics testing a new sound-absorbing material made from softwood fibers arranged using "foam-forming" — a manufacturing process that uses foam (bubbles) instead of plain water to spread and bind wood fibers into a porous sheet, similar in spirit to how paper is made but fluffier and more open, which is what lets it soak up sound. The material is a bio-based (plant-derived, more sustainable) alternative to the plastic foams commonly used to line rooms and reduce echo. The paper measures how well it absorbs sound and builds a model to predict its performance from its physical structure.
