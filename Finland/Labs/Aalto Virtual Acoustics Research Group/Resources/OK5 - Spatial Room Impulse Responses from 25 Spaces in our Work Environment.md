A paper introducing "OK5," an open dataset of spatial room impulse responses recorded in 25 ordinary everyday spaces — offices, corridors, meeting rooms — rather than concert halls. A room impulse response is a room's acoustic "fingerprint": how it reflects and colors a short test sound. A "spatial" impulse response also records which direction each reflection arrived from, not just its loudness and timing. Researchers use datasets like this to test spatial audio and auralization (rendering how a room would actually sound) algorithms against real, everyday acoustic environments instead of only idealized concert spaces.
