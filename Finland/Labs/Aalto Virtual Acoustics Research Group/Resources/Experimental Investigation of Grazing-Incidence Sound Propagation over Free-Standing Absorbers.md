A study in Acta Acustica on how sound behaves when it travels almost parallel to a sound-absorbing panel ("grazing incidence") rather than hitting it head-on, using panels that stand freely in a room rather than being mounted flush against a wall. Sound-absorbing panels are used to soak up excess reflections in a space; this paper measures experimentally how much a free-standing absorber actually reduces sound that skims past it at a shallow angle, which matters for designing acoustic treatment in rooms, offices, and outdoor noise barriers.
