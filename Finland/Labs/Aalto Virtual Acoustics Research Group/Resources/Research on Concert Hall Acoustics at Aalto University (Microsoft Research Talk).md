A recorded talk given at Microsoft Research by Henna Tahvanainen, at the time a doctoral student in Aalto's Virtual Acoustics team. It walks through how the group measures and analyzes concert halls and runs listening tests to understand what makes some halls sound better than others. A key finding discussed is why "shoebox" halls (long rectangular rooms, like Vienna's Musikverein) tend to be the most successful concert hall shape. The talk also covers the "seat-dip effect" — a dip in bass loudness caused by sound grazing over rows of seated audience members — and how it affects the perception of bass in a hall.
