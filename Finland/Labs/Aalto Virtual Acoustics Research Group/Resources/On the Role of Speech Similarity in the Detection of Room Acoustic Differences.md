A perceptual study, published in the Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, asking whether people find it easier to notice that two recordings were made in different rooms when the same word or sentence is spoken in both, compared to when different words are spoken. This matters for how researchers design listening tests: if the specific words used change how easily listeners detect room differences, that has to be accounted for when using speech to study how people perceive room acoustics (the way a room's echoes and reflections shape what we hear).
