This NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression) conference paper presents a system that turns a dancer's movement into music using two connected AI models called "VAEs" (Variational Autoencoders — neural networks that learn a compressed, navigable representation of data, in this case one for movement and one for audio). A "Movement VAE" captures features of the dancer's real-time motion, while an "Audio VAE" called RAVE generates matching musical textures; an "alignment module" links the two so that changes in movement produce related changes in sound. The key idea is "human-in-the-loop" design: instead of a fixed, programmer-defined mapping between movement and sound, the dancer calibrates the connection themselves through exploration before performing, making the system feel more intuitive and personally expressive. The work was carried out as part of the SonicMove project, a collaboration between Aalto University, VTT, and the University of Eastern Finland.
