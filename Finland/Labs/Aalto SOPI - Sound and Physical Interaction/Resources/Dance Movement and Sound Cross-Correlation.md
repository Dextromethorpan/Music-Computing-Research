This paper, published at the Audio Mostly conference, studies how a dancer's body movements can be turned into sound in real time — a process called "sonification" (translating non-audio data, here motion, into audio). The researchers looked at how synthesis parameters (the knobs and settings that shape a generated sound, such as pitch or texture) can be linked to movement at two different timescales: "micro" (fast, small movements, like a flick of the wrist) and "meso" (slower, larger-scale movement phrases). By measuring the statistical relationship (cross-correlation) between movement data and the resulting sound, the paper helps instrument and interaction designers understand which mapping choices make a dance-controlled sound system feel responsive and expressive rather than random or disconnected.
