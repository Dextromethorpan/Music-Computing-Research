A "digital musical instrument" (DMI) is a musical instrument that uses sensors, computers and software to make sound, instead of (or alongside) mechanical or acoustic parts — think of a MIDI controller, a gesture-tracking glove, or custom electronic instruments built by researchers. This paper argues that we should think of the relationship between a musician and a DMI not as a fixed "tool used by a person," but as something that forms and changes together in the moment of playing — the paper calls this an "intra-action" (a term borrowed from philosophy of science, meaning the player and the instrument shape each other rather than acting on each other from the outside). The author, Koray Tahiroğlu, uses his own instrument-building and performance practice to explore how a musician's identity and playing style can shift depending on how a digital instrument behaves. This is a conceptual/theoretical paper rather than a technical one, aimed at helping instrument designers and performers understand what makes digital instruments feel expressive and alive rather than mechanical.
