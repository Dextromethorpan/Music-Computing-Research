Equalization is the process of boosting or cutting certain frequencies in a sound — turning up the bass, taming a harsh treble, and so on — and it is one of the most-used tools in music production and hearing technology alike. This review paper is a broad, readable survey of how equalizers are designed and used, from simple classic tone controls to more advanced designs used in mastering studios, hearing aids, and loudspeaker correction. It explains the engineering trade-offs involved (for example, how precisely a filter can target a frequency versus how much processing delay it introduces) and points to open research questions, making it a useful map of the whole field for newcomers as well as a reference for specialists.
