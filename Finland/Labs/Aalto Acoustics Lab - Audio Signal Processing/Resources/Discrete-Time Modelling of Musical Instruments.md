This is a long, widely cited review paper (78 pages) that explains how to make a computer produce realistic instrument sounds by simulating the physics of the instrument itself — how a string vibrates, how air moves inside a wind instrument, how a drumhead flexes — rather than just playing back a recording. "Discrete-time" means the simulation is calculated in small time steps that a computer can handle. The paper surveys several families of techniques for doing this (with names like digital waveguide, modal, and finite-difference models) and explains the physics and math behind each one, along with their strengths and weaknesses. It became a foundational reference for anyone building physically realistic virtual instruments.
