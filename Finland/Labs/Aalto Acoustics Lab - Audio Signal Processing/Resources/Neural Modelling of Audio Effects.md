This is Alec Wright's doctoral dissertation, which pulls together several years of research into using neural networks (machine-learning models that learn patterns from examples rather than being explicitly programmed) to imitate audio effects such as guitar amplifiers, distortion pedals, reverb, and time-based effects. The thesis covers how to train these models efficiently, how to shrink them so they run fast enough for live use, and how to train them even when there isn't perfectly matched "before and after" audio to learn from. Together, the work forms one of the more complete accounts of how "black-box" machine-learning modelling — treating the effect as an unknown system to be learned purely from its input/output behaviour, without needing to know its internal circuitry — can replace traditional physics-based circuit modelling for real-time audio effects.
