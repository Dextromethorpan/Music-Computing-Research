When calibrating a studio monitor speaker to sound accurate in a specific room, engineers measure the room's "frequency response" — basically, how loudly the room reproduces each pitch, from low bass to high treble — using a microphone. This AES (Audio Engineering Society) convention paper studies where in the room you should place that microphone, and whether taking several measurements at different nearby spots and averaging them gives a more trustworthy, stable picture than relying on one measurement at a single point (like the listener's exact seat). The authors find that combining several spaced-out measurements ("spatial averaging") makes the measurement less sensitive to small quirks of the room, which is directly relevant to how Genelec's GLM calibration software decides how to correct a monitor for the room it's placed in.
