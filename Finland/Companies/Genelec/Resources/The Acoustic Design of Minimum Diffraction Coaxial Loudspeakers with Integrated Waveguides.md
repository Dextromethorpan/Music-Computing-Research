A "coaxial" loudspeaker puts two or more drivers (the parts that push air to make sound, like a tweeter for highs and a midrange for mids) on the same central axis instead of side by side, so the sound seems to come from one single point rather than several spread-out sources — engineers call this a "point source." Coaxial designs are attractive because a point source sounds more consistent no matter where you stand in the room, but past designs introduced their own problems, including "diffraction" (unwanted extra sound caused by audio waves bending around the edges of a driver or horn) and distortion. This AES (Audio Engineering Society) convention paper by Genelec's R&D team reviews the history of coaxial driver design and describes a "minimum diffraction" coaxial design that also acts as part of a smooth, continuous "waveguide" (a shaped surface that helps control the direction sound spreads in), eliminating several of the classic drawbacks of older coaxial speakers.
