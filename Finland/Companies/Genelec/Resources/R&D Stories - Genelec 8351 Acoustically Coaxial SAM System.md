This audioXpress "R&D Stories" article (based on a conference paper first presented at the 137th AES convention) walks through how Genelec's engineers designed the 8351 studio monitor speaker so that its tweeter, midrange, and two woofers all behave like one single, consistent sound source ("acoustically coaxial"), even though the woofers are physically hidden at the top and bottom of the cabinet rather than stacked with the other drivers. The article explains, in practical terms, why matching how different drivers spread sound in different directions ("directivity") matters for accurate studio monitoring, and how hiding the woofers under the front panel avoids the sound-smearing effects ("diffraction") that plague older three-way speaker designs. It's a good example of how a single engineering idea (avoiding diffraction) drives a product's entire physical shape.
