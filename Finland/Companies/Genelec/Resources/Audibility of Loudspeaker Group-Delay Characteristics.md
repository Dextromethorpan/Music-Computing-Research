"Group delay" is a technical way of describing whether some pitches (frequencies) of a sound arrive at your ear slightly later than others after passing through a loudspeaker's electronics and physical parts — ideally a speaker reproduces all frequencies at the same relative time, but real hardware never does this perfectly. This AES convention paper, a collaboration between Aalto University researchers and Genelec's R&D director, asks a very practical question: how much group delay distortion can a listener actually notice before it audibly changes the sound? The answer helps loudspeaker designers know how much engineering effort should go into minimizing this specific type of imperfection versus focusing on other things listeners notice more.
