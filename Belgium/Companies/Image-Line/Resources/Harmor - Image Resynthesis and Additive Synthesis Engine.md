Any sound can be rebuilt out of many simple sine-wave tones added together (this idea, called additive synthesis, comes from 19th-century mathematician Joseph Fourier). Harmor is a synthesizer built entirely on this principle — it can generate up to 516 of these sine-wave "partials" per note. What makes it a documented, specific innovation is its "Image Resynthesis" mode: it turns a real recorded sound into a picture, where one axis represents time, and the picture's brightness represents the loudness and pitch of each partial. A musician can then literally draw or edit that picture — blur it, reshape it, distort it — and Harmor resynthesizes the modified picture back into sound in real time. This gives sample-editing the flexibility of image editing, which is unusual among commercial synthesizers.
