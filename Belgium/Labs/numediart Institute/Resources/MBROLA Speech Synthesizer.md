MBROLA is a free tool, started in 1995 by Thierry Dutoit, that turns written phonetic instructions (which sounds to say, how long, at what pitch) into synthesised speech audio. It works by gluing together tiny pre-recorded snippets of real human voice recordings and smoothly adjusting their pitch and length, rather than generating sound from a purely mathematical voice model. It became one of the most widely used free speech-synthesis building blocks worldwide for research and language technology, including tools for singing voice and expressive speech, and is a direct ancestor of the voice-technology research still done at UMONS today.
