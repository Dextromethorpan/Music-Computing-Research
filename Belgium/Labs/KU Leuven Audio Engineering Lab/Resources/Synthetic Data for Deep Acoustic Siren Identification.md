This paper tackles a practical problem: teaching a computer system to reliably recognise emergency-vehicle sirens in real-world street audio, which requires large amounts of training recordings that are hard and expensive to collect. The researchers generate artificial ("synthetic") siren sounds and mix them into realistic street noise to train a neural network, instead of relying only on rare real recordings. The general recipe — building realistic fake training data when real data is scarce — is widely used across sound-recognition research, including tasks closer to music, like recognising instruments or genres.
