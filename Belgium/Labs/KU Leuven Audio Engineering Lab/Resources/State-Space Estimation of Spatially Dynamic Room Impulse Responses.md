Every room "colours" sound in its own way — echoes, reflections off walls, and resonances all shape what a microphone actually picks up. This pattern is called a "room impulse response," and it changes as a sound source or microphone moves around the room. This paper proposes a mathematical method to track and predict how a room's impulse response changes over time and space, using a physics-based model of how sound behaves in rooms as a starting guide. Understanding room acoustics this well is used for things like acoustic echo cancellation, virtual/augmented reality audio, and recreating realistic room sound for music heritage and performance research.
