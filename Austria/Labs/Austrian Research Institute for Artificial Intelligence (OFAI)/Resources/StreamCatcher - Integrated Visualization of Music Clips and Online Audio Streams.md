Presented at the ISMIR 2008 conference (the main academic venue for music information retrieval, the field of getting computers to automatically understand and organize music), this paper describes StreamCatcher, a tool that visualizes internet radio streams and short music clips together so a listener can browse, compare, and make sense of audio they haven't listened to yet without playing every clip end to end. It combines waveform/feature visualizations with information retrieval techniques to help users quickly judge whether a piece of music or a live stream is worth listening to. It's a practical example of "audio content analysis" — extracting descriptive information directly from a sound recording — being used to build a better browsing interface rather than just a recommendation score.
