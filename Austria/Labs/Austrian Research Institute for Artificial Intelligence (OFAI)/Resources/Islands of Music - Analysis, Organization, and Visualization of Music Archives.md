Islands of Music is a project by former OFAI researcher Elias Pampalk that turns a music collection into a browsable "map," where songs that sound similar are placed close together and end up clustering into visual "islands" that roughly correspond to genres or styles — all without a human ever labeling the genres by hand. The system decides how similar two songs are by modeling how a human ear perceives loudness changes over time and frequency (a "psychoacoustic model"), rather than just comparing raw audio waveforms. This was one of the earliest content-based approaches to organizing and exploring large digital music libraries visually, predating today's streaming-service recommendation engines. It is closely tied to OFAI's broader body of work on audio similarity and self-organizing maps (a machine-learning technique for arranging data points on a 2D grid by similarity).
