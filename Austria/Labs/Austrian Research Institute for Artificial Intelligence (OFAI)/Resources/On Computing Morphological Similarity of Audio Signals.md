This paper, presented at the 8th Sound and Music Computing Conference, looks at a different way of comparing two sounds: not by their overall genre or mood, but by their "shape" over time — for instance, whether a sound starts sharp and fades away, or builds up slowly (its "morphology," borrowed from a term used to describe the physical shape of sound events). The authors propose ways to compute this kind of shape-based similarity automatically. This work sits inside OFAI's larger, sustained research thread on audio similarity measurement — the group also published on a known problem in this area called "hubness," where certain songs get wrongly recommended as similar to almost everything due to quirks in how similarity algorithms behave in high-dimensional data.
