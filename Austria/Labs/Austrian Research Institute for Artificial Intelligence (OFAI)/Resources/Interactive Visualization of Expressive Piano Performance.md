This master's thesis, completed at OFAI, is about "expressive performance": the small, deliberate variations a human pianist makes in timing, loudness, and articulation that turn a mechanically flat rendition of sheet music into an emotionally engaging performance. The thesis builds interactive visualizations that let a researcher see these variations — for instance, subtle speeding up or slowing down (tempo rubato) — laid out visually alongside the score, making patterns easier to spot than by staring at raw numbers. It grew out of OFAI's long-running "AI models of musical expression" research program, which used machine learning to try to find and explain the hidden rules behind great musical performances — work that in the early 2000s was covered by Wired Magazine and Der Spiegel.
