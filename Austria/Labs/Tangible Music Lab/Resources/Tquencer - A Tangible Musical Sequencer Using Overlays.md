A "sequencer" is a tool that plays back a pattern of musical notes or beats in a loop, and is a basic building block of most electronic music software. Tquencer is a physical, tabletop version of a sequencer: instead of programming a beat pattern by clicking on a screen, the performer places small physical "overlay" pieces onto a tracked surface to lay out the rhythm and notes by hand, similarly to arranging pieces on a board game. The system uses the same tangible-interaction and tracking approach as the lab's earlier reacTable and TUIO work, applied specifically to rhythm and sequencing rather than general synthesizer patching. It was presented at TEI (Tangible, Embedded and Embodied Interaction), the main conference for this kind of physical-object-based computer interface research.
