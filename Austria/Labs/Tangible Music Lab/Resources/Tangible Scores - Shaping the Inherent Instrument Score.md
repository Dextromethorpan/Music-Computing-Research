Presented at NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression, the main academic conference for people who design new kinds of electronic instruments), this paper explores the idea that every physical musical instrument already carries a kind of built-in "score," or set of instructions, in its shape and layout — think of how the position of piano keys silently tells a player what notes are possible. The authors design tangible instruments (instruments you interact with by touching and moving real objects) where the physical construction itself guides and constrains how a performer plays, blurring the line between an instrument's design and the music notation traditionally printed on paper. It reflects the lab's broader interest in "postdigital lutherie" — using both digital technology and traditional instrument-making craft together — to design new instruments.
