TUIO is a shared "language" (a communication protocol) that lets one piece of software describe what is happening on an interactive tabletop — which objects are present, where they are, how they're rotated, and where fingers are touching — and send that description to any other piece of software that knows how to read it. It was created alongside reacTIVision and the reacTable, but because it is openly published and technology-neutral, many other tabletop and multi-touch systems have adopted it too, making it a small industry standard. It is built on top of a lower-level standard called Open Sound Control (OSC), a general-purpose way of sending fast numeric messages between programs, originally developed for music.
