This paper describes the reacTable, a round, glowing tabletop synthesizer where musicians build and reshape an electronic-music instrument in real time by placing, rotating, and connecting physical wooden blocks on the table's surface. Each block is a musical building block — an oscillator, a filter, a sequencer — and moving the blocks close together visually and audibly "connects" them into a working synthesizer patch. The paper argues that this kind of tangible interface (controlling software through real graspable objects rather than a screen and mouse) is especially well suited to live music performance, because several people can gather around the table and manipulate it together, and the audience can actually see how the sound is being made. Martin Kaltenbrunner, head of the Tangible Music Lab, co-created the reacTable and its underlying reacTIVision tracking software while at the Music Technology Group in Barcelona, before later founding the Tangible Music Lab in Linz.
