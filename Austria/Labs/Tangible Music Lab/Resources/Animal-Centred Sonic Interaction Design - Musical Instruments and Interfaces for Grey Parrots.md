Most musical-instrument design assumes the player is a human, but this paper asks what a musical instrument designed for a grey parrot would look and sound like. The researchers built custom interfaces that let captive grey parrots trigger and shape sounds themselves, based on earlier tests of what the birds can hear and what sounds interest them. This falls under "Animal-Computer Interaction" (ACI), a research field that studies how to design technology for non-human animals rather than for people, treating the animal as a genuine user whose preferences and abilities should shape the design. The work connects to the lab's tangible-interaction expertise by exploring what "playable" and "graspable" mean for a creature with a beak and claws instead of hands.
