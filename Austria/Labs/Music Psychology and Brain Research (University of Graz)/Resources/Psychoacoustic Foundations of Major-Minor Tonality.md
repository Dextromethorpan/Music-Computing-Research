This open-access book (published by MIT Press) asks a very old question with modern tools: why do the major and minor scales that dominate Western music feel the way they do — "happy" versus "sad," stable versus tense? "Tonality" is the sense that some notes in a piece of music feel like "home" and others feel like they need to resolve toward that home note. Parncutt argues this feeling can be explained largely by psychoacoustics — the study of how the ear and brain physically process sound — rather than by cultural convention alone. He builds a computational model that predicts how "consonant" (pleasant/stable) or "dissonant" (tense/unstable) a chord or scale will sound, based on how the ear processes overlapping sound frequencies. This book is a summary of decades of Parncutt's research on this topic.
