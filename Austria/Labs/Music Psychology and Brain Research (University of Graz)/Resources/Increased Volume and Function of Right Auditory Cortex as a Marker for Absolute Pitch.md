Published in Cerebral Cortex, this paper investigates "absolute pitch" — the rare ability to identify or name a musical note (like "that's a C-sharp") just by hearing it, with no reference note to compare it to. It's very uncommon among non-musicians in Western countries (roughly 1 in 10,000 people) but far more common among professional musicians and speakers of "tonal languages" (languages, common in Asia, where the pitch/melody of a word changes its meaning). Using brain imaging, the researchers found that people with absolute pitch have a larger and more active area on the right side of the auditory cortex (the brain region that processes sound), together with extra activity in a nearby language-related area (Broca's area) that seems to "label" the pitch with a name. This was one of the first studies to identify a specific brain marker for this unusual ability.
