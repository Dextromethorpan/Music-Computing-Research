Published in the journal Music Perception, this is one of Parncutt's most-cited papers. It tackles the question of why, when we listen to a rhythm, some beats feel stronger or more "important" than others (this is called "metrical accent" — think of how you naturally clap or tap your foot on certain beats of a song and not others). "Pulse salience" refers to how strongly a regular beat stands out perceptually. Parncutt builds a mathematical/computational model that predicts, for any given rhythm, which beats a listener will feel as strong versus weak, based on properties like how often a beat repeats and how loud or long the notes are. This kind of model is foundational for software that tries to automatically detect tempo and beat structure in audio.
