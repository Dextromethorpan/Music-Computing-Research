Published in the Journal of Neuroscience, this is a 12-year study — meaning the same people were followed and re-tested over 12 years — comparing musicians and non-musicians from childhood into adulthood. "Neuroplasticity" is the brain's ability to physically change its structure and connections in response to experience (in this case, musical training). The researchers used brain scans (MRI, which shows brain structure) to see whether differences in the auditory brain regions of musicians are something people are born with ("neuroanatomical disposition") or something that develops through musical practice and general growing up. They found evidence for both: some structural traits are already present early on, while others change measurably with training. This helps explain why some people seem to have a "natural ear" while others develop strong hearing skills primarily through practice.
