Published in the Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, this study plays listeners sequences of tones where the underlying pitch (fundamental frequency) and the timbre (tone color, driven by overtones) send conflicting signals about whether the "melody" is going up or down. The researchers found that trained musicians and people without musical training actually perceive these ambiguous sequences differently — musicians are more likely to track the true underlying pitch, while non-musicians are more easily misled by timbre changes. This shows that musical training doesn't just make people better at music, it can change the basic way the auditory system resolves ambiguous sound information.
