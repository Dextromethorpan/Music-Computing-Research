Published in Nature Neuroscience, this study looks at "Heschl's gyrus," a small fold of brain tissue on both sides of the brain where sound is first processed in detail. Most musical sounds are made of a "fundamental frequency" (the main, lowest tone that we recognize as the pitch) plus quieter "overtones" (higher frequencies layered on top that give an instrument its distinctive timbre, or tone color). The researchers found that people naturally differ in whether they mainly pay attention to the fundamental frequency ("abstract hearing," linked more to the left hemisphere) or to the overtones ("concrete/spectral hearing," linked more to the right hemisphere), and that this preference is reflected in the actual physical size and activity of Heschl's gyrus on each side of the brain. This individual difference also correlates with which musical instruments people tend to prefer.
