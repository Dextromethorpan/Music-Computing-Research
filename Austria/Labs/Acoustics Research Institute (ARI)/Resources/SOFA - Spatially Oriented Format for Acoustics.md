SOFA is a standardized file format for storing directional acoustic measurements — most importantly Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTFs), which describe how a sound's frequency content subtly changes depending on the direction it comes from relative to a listener's head and ears. Before SOFA, every lab saved this kind of spatial-audio measurement data in its own incompatible format, making it hard to share. SOFA gives everyone a common format, so an HRTF dataset measured in one lab can be loaded and used directly by software in another lab, or by game engines and VR/AR audio tools that need to render realistic 3D sound. Led out of ARI, SOFA was standardized by the Audio Engineering Society as AES69 and won the 2013 Reproducibility in Audio and Music Research Prize.
