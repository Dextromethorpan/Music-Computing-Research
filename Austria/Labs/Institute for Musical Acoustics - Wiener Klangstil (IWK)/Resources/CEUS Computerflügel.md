"CEUS" is a real, acoustic grand piano (built by the Bösendorfer company) that has been fitted with sensors and motors under every key and pedal, so a computer can record exactly how hard and fast a pianist presses each key, and can later play the piano back by itself with the same touch — no synthesized sound, just the real piano replaying itself. This makes it a key piece of equipment at the IWK's Performance Science Lab for studying expressive piano performance: researchers can capture a pianist's playing in extreme detail and then analyze or even physically reproduce it. It is used alongside the lab's motion capture (cameras that track body movement) and eye-tracking (equipment that records where a person is looking) facilities.
