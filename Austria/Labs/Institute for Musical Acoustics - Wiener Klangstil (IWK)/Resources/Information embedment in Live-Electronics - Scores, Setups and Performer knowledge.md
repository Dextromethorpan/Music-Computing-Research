"Live-electronics" means music performances where a musician's acoustic sound (say, a clarinet) is captured live and transformed or mixed with electronic sounds in real time, often using a laptop or custom hardware. This paper, presented at the TENOR 2026 conference (a conference on technologies for music notation and representation), looks at where the knowledge needed to perform such a piece actually lives: in the written score, in the technical setup (cables, software patches, sensors), or in the performer's own experience and habits. The authors argue all three carry pieces of the puzzle, which matters for anyone trying to preserve or hand down live-electronic works so they can be performed again later. Alex Hofmann is deputy head of the IWK and works on live electronics and new interfaces for musical performance.
