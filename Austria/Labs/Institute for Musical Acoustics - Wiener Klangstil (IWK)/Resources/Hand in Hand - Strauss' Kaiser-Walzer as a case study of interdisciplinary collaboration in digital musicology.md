"Digital musicology" means using computer tools and digital data (encoded scores, recordings, databases) to study music history and performance, rather than relying only on paper scores and archives. This paper uses Johann Strauss II's famous "Kaiser-Walzer" (Emperor Waltz) as a case study to show how historians, musicologists, and computer scientists at IWK worked together to build a digital, searchable, richly annotated record of the piece and its performance history. It appears in Digital Scholarship in the Humanities and grew out of the institute's Vienna New Year's Concert research (the "Signature Sound Vienna" project), led jointly by Chanda VanderHart and David M. Weigl.
