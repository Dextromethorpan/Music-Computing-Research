This journal article studies what happens in the body when two classical musicians (a "duo") play together and feel like they are truly playing "as one." The researchers measured body coordination (how closely the two performers' movements sync up, tracked using motion-capture cameras that record body position over time) and pupil size (the pupil is the dark circle in the eye that widens or narrows partly based on mental effort and emotional arousal, so tracking it gives clues about a performer's inner state). It is part of the institute's "Togetherness" project, which investigates the psychology and physiology behind ensemble musicians' sense of connectedness. The paper appears in Psychology of Music and was co-authored with Werner Goebl, head of the IWK's performance science research.
