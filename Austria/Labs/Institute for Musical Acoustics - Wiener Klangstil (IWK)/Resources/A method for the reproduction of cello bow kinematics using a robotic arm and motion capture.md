"Kinematics" refers to the precise motion of an object — here, the movement of a cello bow as a musician draws it across the strings (speed, angle, pressure, position). This paper describes a system that first records a cellist's bowing motion using motion capture (cameras that track reflective markers to reconstruct 3D movement over time), and then reproduces that exact motion with a robotic arm gripping a real bow on a real cello. This lets researchers isolate and repeat a specific bowing gesture under controlled, repeatable conditions to study how it affects the instrument's sound, something impossible to do consistently with a human player. Published in Acta Acustica, it combines the IWK's acoustics and performance-science research strands.
