This paper looks at symphonic (orchestral) music and asks why the same piece sounds noticeably different when conducted by different conductors. A big part of the answer is "dynamics" — the expressive rises and falls in loudness that a conductor shapes throughout a performance. The researchers built a computational model that predicts these loudness variations directly from the written musical score, and then compares the model's prediction to real recordings to reveal each conductor's personal style. The model works well enough to spot recognizable "fingerprints" of individual conductors, showing that at least some of what listeners perceive as artistic interpretation can be captured and measured computationally.
