This is XARION's foundational patent, granted in 2012, for a microphone that has no membrane or moving parts at all. Instead, a laser beam is shone through a small pocket of air, and sound waves passing through that air change how fast the light travels through it. By precisely measuring that tiny change in the laser beam, the device can "hear" sound. Because nothing physically moves or vibrates, this sensor can respond to an extremely wide range of frequencies — far beyond what a normal microphone with a membrane can pick up — and it does not wear out or resonate the way a physical diaphragm can.
