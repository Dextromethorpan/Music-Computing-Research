A case-study blog post from the "HPC in Europe" portal (run by the EuroCC programme, which helps European companies get free access to supercomputers) explaining how Beat Shaper trained its music-generation AI. Instead of training on finished audio recordings, Beat Shaper used a "symbolic music" approach: the model is trained on structured data describing how a piece should be played — which notes, at what tempo and rhythm, plus synthesizer settings like oscillator and filter parameters — similar to a musician reading sheet music rather than listening to a recording. Because no such training dataset existed before, the team had to build it themselves. To find a good model without overfitting (memorizing the training data instead of learning to create new, original patterns), they trained more than 500 versions of their AI model on the Austrian Scientific Cluster (ASC, formerly the Vienna Scientific Cluster), a supercomputer, cutting training time by roughly 50x compared to their own hardware. The final model has about 5 million parameters (adjustable internal values a model learns during training), making it a small model by AI standards but still requiring heavy computing power to develop. The post also names the four founders and the funding/support organizations (INiTS, FFG, EuroCC Austria) behind the company.
