A "condenser microphone" is a type of microphone that senses sound using a thin, electrically-charged membrane (the "diaphragm") that sits very close to a fixed metal plate (the "electrode" or "backplate"); as sound waves push the diaphragm back and forth, the changing gap between the two creates the electrical signal. This patent, filed in 2019 by Austrian Audio, covers a microphone capsule (the small internal assembly holding the diaphragm and electrode) built with ceramic parts — specifically a ceramic holding ring and ceramic diaphragm ring — instead of the more common metal or plastic. Ceramic is stiffer and more dimensionally stable across temperature changes than plastic, and this improved consistency is what underlies Austrian Audio's CKR12 capsule used in its OC818 and OC18 microphones. This is the foundational patent behind the company's flagship microphone technology.
