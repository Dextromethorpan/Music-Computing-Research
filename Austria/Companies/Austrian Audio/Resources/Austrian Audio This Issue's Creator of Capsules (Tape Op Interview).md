This magazine interview, published as Austrian Audio was launching its first microphone, explains in plain terms how the company was formed from 22-23 former AKG (a famous older Austrian microphone brand) employees after AKG's Vienna factory closed. It describes why cheap microphones use outsourced, mass-produced "capsules" (the vibrating heart of a microphone) while high-end ones are hand-built one at a time, and how Austrian Audio designed a new capsule using ceramic parts (instead of the usual metal-and-plastic) to make the precise hand-assembly process more consistent and slightly more automatable. It's a good beginner-friendly explanation of why microphone manufacturing is mostly a mechanical-engineering problem, not just an electronics one.
