A detailed factory-visit article explaining, for a general audio-enthusiast reader, exactly why building a high-end microphone capsule (the vibrating diaphragm-and-backplate assembly at the heart of a microphone) by hand is so hard. Founder Martin Seidl explains how the old AKG "CK12" capsule that inspired their design had a manufacturing yield (percentage of working parts produced) as low as 15%, and how switching the capsule's structural ring from brass to a specific dense ceramic material solved the trade-off between weight, precision manufacturing, and stable sound. It also covers how a laser-based measuring tool is used to precisely set the gap between diaphragm and backplate (a distance thinner than a human hair), since this gap directly controls the microphone's sensitivity and its "polar pattern" (which directions it best picks up sound from).
