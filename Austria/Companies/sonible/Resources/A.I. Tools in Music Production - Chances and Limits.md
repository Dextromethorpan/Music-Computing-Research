A beginner-friendly company blog post that explains, in plain language, where AI genuinely helps in music production and where it doesn't. It argues AI/machine-learning tools are good at fast, repetitive, technical corrections (like fixing "boomy" or resonant frequencies) that a trained audio engineer would otherwise do by ear over many iterations, but that AI cannot replace creative artistic decisions (like choosing a song's overall tone or writing a melody with intent). It also notes a key limitation: AI correction tools can't fix a badly recorded source — they can only improve what's technically fixable. Includes audio examples (before/after) of sonible's smart:EQ correcting a boomy guitar recording and smart:comp resolving "masking" between a piano and a pad track.
