This patent, filed in 2001 by VSL's founder, describes the core engineering problem sample libraries face: a real orchestra never plays the exact same note the exact same way twice, but early sample players only had one recording per note, so fast repeated notes sounded fake and "stuttering." The patent's solution is a searchable database that links every recorded sample (single notes, but also recorded repetitions, legato transitions between notes, and trills) to descriptive tags, and automatically time-stretches or time-compresses the closest matching recorded sample and cross-fades it into place to match the tempo the composer is actually playing — all happening automatically in the background. This "sample-matching and stretching" engine became the technical basis of VSL's sample players.
