This technical documentation explains, at a beginner level, what a Class-D amplifier is: instead of continuously varying an electrical signal like older amplifier designs, a Class-D amp rapidly switches fully on and off many thousands of times per second (a technique called pulse-width modulation). This wastes far less energy as heat, meaning a Class-D amp can be smaller, lighter, and more power-efficient for the same output level. The related DSP (digital signal processor) page explains how Powersoft's onboard chips digitally process, filter, and protect the audio signal before it reaches the amplifier stage.
