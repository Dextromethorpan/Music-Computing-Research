Two ordinary stereo speakers can make instruments sound like they're in roughly the right left-right position, but the whole soundstage tends to feel flat, with everything crowded onto one plane instead of some sounds feeling closer and others further away. Sonus faber's patented Sound Field Shaper tackles this by adding a second module of rear-facing speaker drivers (a tweeter and a midrange driver) to the back of a loudspeaker, aimed away from the listener so their sound reaches the ears indirectly, after bouncing around the room. By adjusting the volume and aim of this rear module with controls on the speaker's back panel, a listener can add back a sense of depth and reflected, "reverberant" space to the sound field — a specific, patented mechanism for controlling perceived depth, not just a general claim about warmer or richer sound.
