Violin makers have relied on centuries of tradition and personal intuition to shape their instruments, without fully understanding exactly how a violin's shape affects its sound. This paper uses statistics and machine learning (software that finds patterns in data) to predict a violin top plate's "modal frequencies" — the natural pitches at which the wood vibrates, which strongly shape the instrument's tone — directly from measurements of its shape. It shows that AI can uncover real, previously undocumented links between a violin's geometry and its acoustic behavior, and proposes this as a predictive tool that makers ("luthiers") could use when shaping and thinning the wood.
