When musicians play together over the internet, small chunks of audio data ("packets") sometimes get lost or arrive too late on the network — and unlike a video call where a frozen frame is just annoying, a dropped audio packet during live music creates an audible click or gap that throws off the timing. This paper proposes using a neural network to predict and fill in those missing bits of audio on the fly, fast enough to stay within the very tight time budget that live music needs (a fraction of a second), so the gap in the audio stream isn't noticeable to the musicians.
