This paper investigates how software can automatically figure out which part of a moving body — for example a dancer's arms versus their torso — is most visually and expressively significant at any given moment. It's a step toward computers that "notice" expressive movement roughly the way a human observer instinctively would, which is useful for building interactive dance and music installations that react convincingly to a performer rather than just to raw motion data.
