This paper is a history of CSC, one of Europe's oldest "computer music" research labs — a field that uses computers to create, transform, or study musical sound. It traces the lab from 1970s analogue electronic-music experiments through to today's digital research, including some of Europe's first tools for linking a performer's physical gesture (movement, touch, breath) to real-time control of sound. It's a good entry point for understanding how a single Italian university lab helped shape 50 years of computer music research, moving from tape-based studios to today's affective-computing and audio-preservation work.
