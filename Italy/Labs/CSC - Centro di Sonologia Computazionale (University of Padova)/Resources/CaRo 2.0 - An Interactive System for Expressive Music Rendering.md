CaRo is software that changes how "expressively" a computer plays back a piece of music, for example making a performance sound sadder, brighter, or more aggressive by adjusting timing, loudness, and articulation (how notes are shaped and connected) — without changing which notes are played. This is called "expressive content processing": analyzing and controlling the emotional character of a performance rather than just its notes. In CaRo, a user drags a point around inside an on-screen "emotion space" (for example, a 2D map with axes like calm-to-energetic and sad-to-happy) and hears the same piece of music morph in real time to match that emotional target. It's a concrete, interactive example of a computer modeling musical expressiveness.
