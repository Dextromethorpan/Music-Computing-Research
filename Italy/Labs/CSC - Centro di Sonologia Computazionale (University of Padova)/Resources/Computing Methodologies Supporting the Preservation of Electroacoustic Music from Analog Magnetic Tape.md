Many historic pieces of electronic music only exist on old analogue magnetic tape, which physically decays over time and can be lost forever. This paper explains software tools CSC built to digitize and restore those tapes before that happens — for example, automatically detecting splices (places where the tape was physically cut and joined) and figuring out the correct equalization (tone-correction) settings that were used originally, so a modern digital copy sounds as close as possible to what the composer intended. It's a clear beginner example of "digital preservation": using computing to rescue physical audio media that would otherwise be lost.
