A "Class-D" amplifier is a type of audio amplifier that boosts a signal by rapidly switching on and off (rather than continuously varying, like older amplifier designs), which makes it much more energy-efficient — important for battery-powered speakers and for reducing heat. This article from an audio engineering trade publication profiles Axign's approach to Class-D amplifier chips, explaining how the company uses fully digital control circuits, measured after the output filter, to keep distortion very low even though the underlying switching process is inherently noisy.
