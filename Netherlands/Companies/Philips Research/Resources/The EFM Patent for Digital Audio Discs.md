To store music digitally on a Compact Disc, the music first has to be turned into a very long string of 1s and 0s, and then that string has to be physically encoded as a pattern of microscopic pits on the disc's surface. "Eight-to-Fourteen Modulation" (EFM) is the specific method Philips engineer Kees Schouhamer Immink invented for doing this conversion in a way that avoids patterns a CD player's laser can't reliably read, while still packing in as much music as possible. This patent describes that method, and it became one of the core technologies underlying every audio CD, CD-ROM, and MiniDisc ever made.
