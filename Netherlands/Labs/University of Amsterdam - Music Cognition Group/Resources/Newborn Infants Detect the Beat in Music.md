Can babies feel a musical beat before they've had any real exposure to music? This study measured the brain activity of newborn infants (only a few days old) while they listened to simple drum rhythms, using a method that detects surprise responses in the brain. When a beat was unexpectedly skipped, the babies' brains showed a measurable "surprise" reaction — suggesting that a basic ability to track a musical beat is present from birth rather than something that must be learned. This is one of the foundational studies behind the idea that rhythm perception is part of our biology.
