When people hear a rhythm and then try to tap it back, their tapped version is often systematically distorted compared to what they actually heard. This paper uses "Bayesian" reasoning — a mathematical framework for combining what you expect with what you actually observe — to explain those distortions as the result of the brain balancing the sound it hears against its own internal expectations of "typical" rhythms. The same mathematical model is used to explain both how people perceive rhythms and how they reproduce them, showing the two processes share a common underlying mechanism.
