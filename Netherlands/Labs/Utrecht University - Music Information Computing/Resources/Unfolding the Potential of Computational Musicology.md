"Computational musicology" means using computer programs to study music the way a musicologist would — looking at melody, harmony, form and style — but at a scale no human could do by hand, across thousands of pieces at once. This paper explains what the field can offer traditional music scholarship: for example, automatically finding recurring melodic patterns across a large collection of folk songs, or testing a music-theoretical idea against real data instead of just a handful of hand-picked examples. It also discusses the practical and conceptual challenges of getting musicologists and computer scientists to work together productively.
