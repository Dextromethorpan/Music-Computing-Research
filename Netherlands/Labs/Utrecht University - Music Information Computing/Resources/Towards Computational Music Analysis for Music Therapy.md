This paper looks at whether computer tools that automatically analyze music (measuring things like rhythm, pitch, and how much a piece changes over time) could help music therapists in their daily work. Music therapists currently analyze recordings of therapy sessions mostly by ear, which is slow and depends on the individual therapist's judgment. The authors argue that "Music Information Retrieval" — the computer-science field that builds software to search, measure, and compare musical audio — has tools that could make this analysis faster and more consistent, but almost no one has actually built tools with therapists in mind. The paper maps out what such collaboration could look like and what technical building blocks are still missing.
