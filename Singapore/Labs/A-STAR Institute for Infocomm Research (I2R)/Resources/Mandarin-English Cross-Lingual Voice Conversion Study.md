This paper addresses "cross-lingual" voice conversion: making a voice recorded speaking one language (Mandarin) sound like a target speaker even though the target speaker's available recordings are in a different language (English), or vice versa. The authors combine two families of methods — "non-parallel" ones (CycleGAN-VC2 and phonetic-posteriorgram-based conversion, which don't need matched recordings of the same sentence from both speakers) with a "parallel" refinement stage (which does use matched pairs, generated using the non-parallel system's own output) to sharpen the converted speech's naturalness and how closely it resembles the target speaker. It was presented at ICASSP 2024, one of the largest annual speech and signal processing conferences, by two I2R researchers.
