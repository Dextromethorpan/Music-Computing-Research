RSR2015 is a large recorded-speech dataset built by I2R's Human Language Technology department to help researchers test and improve "text-dependent" speaker verification — systems that confirm someone's identity by voice when they say a specific known phrase (like a voice-based passcode), as opposed to free, unscripted speech. It contains over 150 hours of short recordings from 300 speakers (143 female, 157 male, ages 17-42) reading multiple fixed pass-phrases. Datasets like this are the foundation that voice-biometrics and speaker-verification research (a close cousin of the voice-modeling techniques used in singing voice conversion) is built and benchmarked on. Presented at Interspeech 2012.
