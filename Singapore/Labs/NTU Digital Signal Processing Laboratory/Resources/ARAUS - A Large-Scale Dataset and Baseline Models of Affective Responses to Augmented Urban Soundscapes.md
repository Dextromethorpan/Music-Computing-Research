ARAUS stands for "Affective Responses to Augmented Urban Soundscapes." It is a large public dataset built by playing people audio-visual clips of real city locations (like a busy street) with extra background sounds digitally mixed in — birds, water, wind, traffic, or construction — at different volumes relative to the original scene. Over 25,000 listener ratings were collected on how pleasant, calm, chaotic, or vibrant each mix felt. The dataset also comes with baseline machine-learning models that try to predict how "pleasant" a soundscape will feel to a listener just from the audio itself. It is a widely used benchmark for research on designing better-sounding cities and public spaces.
