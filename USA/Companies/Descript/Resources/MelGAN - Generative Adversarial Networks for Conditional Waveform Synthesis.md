MelGAN is a fast neural "vocoder" — a model that converts a spectrogram (a visual-style representation of sound) back into an actual audio waveform — built using a generative adversarial network (GAN), where two neural networks compete so one gets better at producing realistic audio and the other gets better at spotting fakes. The paper's authors include Lyrebird co-founders Kundan Kumar, Jose Sotelo, and Alexandre de Brébisson, published the same year Lyrebird was acquired by Descript. MelGAN was notable for being much faster than earlier autoregressive vocoders while still sounding natural, which matters for products like Descript's Overdub that need to generate speech responsively. It represents the kind of GAN-based waveform generation research that underlies modern voice-cloning tools.
