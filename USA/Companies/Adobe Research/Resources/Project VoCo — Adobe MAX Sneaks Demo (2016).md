This is the live demo, shown at Adobe's "MAX Sneaks" event in November 2016, that made "Project VoCo" famous as the "Photoshop for voice." Researcher Zeyu Jin took a short recording of comedian/actor Jordan Peele and, live on stage, typed a changed sentence — the software then spoke the new sentence in what sounded convincingly like Peele's own voice. The demo made clear both the creative promise of the technology (fixing a flubbed line in a podcast or voiceover without re-recording) and its risks (the ability to put words in someone's mouth they never said), which is why Adobe kept VoCo as an internal research prototype rather than releasing it as a product.
