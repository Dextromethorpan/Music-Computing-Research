This Spotify Research blog post explains "music source separation" in plain terms: the process of taking a finished song and splitting it back apart into its individual ingredients, such as the vocals, drums, bass, and other instruments, even though the original recording only exists as one mixed-together audio file. The post discusses why this is useful — for example, it can help other music-understanding software work better, since analyzing an isolated vocal line is easier than analyzing a full, busy mix. It also touches on how separating a song into stems (industry term for the isolated tracks) can support creative applications like remixing or karaoke-style tools. It's a good non-technical entry point into a topic that comes up throughout Spotify's audio research.
