This is one of the core Shazam patents (US 6,990,453 B2), filed by Avery Wang and his Stanford advisor Julius O. Smith III and assigned to Shazam Entertainment. It legally describes the same core idea as Wang's ISMIR paper: extracting distinctive "fingerprint" landmarks from an audio signal's frequency content and matching them against a reference database even when the incoming audio is noisy or distorted (like a song overheard through a phone microphone in a loud room). Patents like this one are how companies protect an invented algorithm from being copied, and this filing shows the fingerprinting technique was being formalized as intellectual property around the same time it was published academically.
