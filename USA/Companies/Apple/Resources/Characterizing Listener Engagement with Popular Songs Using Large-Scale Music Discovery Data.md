This study, co-authored by Shazam R&D researcher Casey Baker together with Stanford's CCRMA lab, analyzes millions of real Shazam "what song is this?" queries to understand when during a song people are most likely to reach for their phone and tag it. The researchers found that query timing isn't random — it spikes around musically memorable moments (like a chorus or hook), and that this pattern shifts over a song's lifetime, from its release to its later popularity decline. It's a nice example of how a fingerprinting product like Shazam generates a huge byproduct dataset that can be mined for music-psychology insights, not just for song identification.
