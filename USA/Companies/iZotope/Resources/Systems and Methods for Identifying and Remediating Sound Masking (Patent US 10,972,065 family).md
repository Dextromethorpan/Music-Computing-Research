In a multi-track mix (separate recordings for vocals, drums, guitar, etc. layered together), two instruments that sit in the same frequency range can "mask" each other — like two people talking at the same pitch, making both harder to hear clearly. This iZotope patent family (with US patents including 10,972,065 and 11,469,731, originally filed 2017) describes software that automatically detects where and how much masking is happening between tracks, modeling it as "loudness loss" — how much one track's perceived loudness drops because another track is crowding its frequency space. The system then helps the user fix it, typically by suggesting or applying targeted equalization (EQ, boosting or cutting specific frequencies) to carve out separate space for each instrument. This is the technology behind iZotope's Neutron "Masking Meter" feature.
