Audacity is a free, open-source audio editor originally created at Carnegie Mellon by graduate student Dominic Mazzoni, with Roger Dannenberg as his advisor (Dannenberg also designed Audacity's underlying scripting language, based on his Nyquist language). It lets anyone record, cut, splice, and apply effects to audio through a simple waveform-editing interface, without needing to understand digital signal processing. Audacity became one of the most widely used pieces of free audio software in the world and is a good example of academic computer-music research producing a tool used far outside academia.
