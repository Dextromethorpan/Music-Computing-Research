This paper (published in Computer Music Journal) describes ChucK, a programming language built specifically for making music with code in real time. Unlike most programming languages, ChucK lets a programmer precisely control the timing of sound events down to the sample, and lets them change running code live, on the fly, while the music is playing — a practice known as "live coding." The paper explains ChucK's unusual approach to timing and "concurrency" (running many musical processes at once) and how this design grew out of Ge Wang's PhD work at Princeton before he joined CCRMA. ChucK has since become widely used for teaching, composition, and interactive performance.
