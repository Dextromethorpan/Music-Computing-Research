The Neuromusic Lab is a research group inside CCRMA, founded by Associate Professor Takako Fujioka, that studies how the brain processes and produces music. Rather than working with software or synthesizers, this group uses brain-imaging tools like MEG (magnetoencephalography, which measures tiny magnetic fields produced by brain activity) and EEG (electroencephalography, which measures electrical brain activity via scalp sensors) to observe what happens in a person's brain while they listen to, play, or move along with music. Their research topics include how musicians unconsciously coordinate timing when playing together, how the brain's sense of rhythm changes with age or after a stroke, and how the brain processes structures like rhyme in rap music. This lab represents CCRMA's psychoacoustics and music-cognition side, distinct from the more engineering-focused synthesis and audio-programming work also done there.
