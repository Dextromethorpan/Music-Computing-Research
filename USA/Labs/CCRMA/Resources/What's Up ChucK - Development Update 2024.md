This is a status-report paper presented at NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression, a conference on new tools and technology for making music), describing how the ChucK programming language has grown and changed since its creation over 20 years earlier. It covers new features, performance improvements, and companion tools like ChuGL (which adds real-time graphics alongside sound) and web-based versions of ChucK that run directly in a browser. The paper is useful for understanding how a long-running open-source music programming language keeps evolving to stay relevant for both artists and researchers. It also documents the community and educational side of the project, including its use in courses and performances.
