Written by CCRMA professor Chris Chafe (co-creator of JackTrip) for the AES (Audio Engineering Society) International Conference, this paper looks at how musicians actually cope with the unavoidable time delay ("lag") that happens when they perform together over the internet from different cities or even continents. Instead of treating lag purely as a technical problem to eliminate, Chafe documents five real concerts and rehearsals where lag was ignored, tolerated, or even creatively built into the music itself — for example, one piece used the network delay as a musical timing device. It's a rare example of research that blends deep technical detail about network audio streaming with first-hand accounts of live musical performance. The paper is useful for understanding both the JackTrip software's real-world use and the broader idea that technical limitations can shape (rather than just hinder) artistic choices.
