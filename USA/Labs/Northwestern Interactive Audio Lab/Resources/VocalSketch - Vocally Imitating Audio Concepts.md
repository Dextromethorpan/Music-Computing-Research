This paper explores a simple idea: instead of typing keywords to search for a sound, what if you could just imitate it with your voice, the way people naturally hum a tune or make a "whoosh" sound to describe something? The researchers built a large collection (a "dataset") of vocal imitations of everyday and musical sounds, gathered from many different people online, and studied how well these voice imitations captured the character of the original sounds. This work became the foundation for later "query by vocal imitation" search systems, where a computer listens to your imitation and finds the matching real sound in a database. It also underlies the VocalSketch dataset that many later Interactive Audio Lab projects reused.
