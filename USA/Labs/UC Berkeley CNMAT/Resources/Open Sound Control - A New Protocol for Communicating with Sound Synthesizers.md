This 1997 ICMC paper introduced Open Sound Control (OSC), a communication protocol that lets computers, synthesizers, lighting rigs, and other performance devices send precisely-timed control messages to each other over a network. Before OSC, most electronic instruments communicated using MIDI, an older standard with fairly rigid, low-resolution messages; OSC instead uses flexible, human-readable, URL-style names (like "/synth/1/volume") and can carry much richer, higher-precision data. OSC has since become a de facto industry standard — it's commonly used to synchronize sound and lighting at concerts and events, well beyond its original computer-music research context — and remains free and openly available.
