This paper tackles "multi-pitch tracking" — figuring out, at each moment in a recording, which musical notes (pitches) are being played simultaneously by multiple instruments or voices, and following how those pitches change over time. The method uses the fact that musical instruments produce "harmonics" (a note's pitch comes bundled with quieter copies at whole-number multiples of its frequency) to help work out which frequency components belong together as a single note. This is a foundational building block for automatic music transcription — turning an audio recording into sheet music or a note list — and for separating overlapping instruments in a mix. It predates the AIR Lab's founding (from Duan's PhD work) but is listed among the lab's core publications.
