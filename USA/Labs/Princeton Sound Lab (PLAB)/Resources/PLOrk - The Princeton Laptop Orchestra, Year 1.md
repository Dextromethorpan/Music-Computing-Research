This paper documents the founding year of the Princeton Laptop Orchestra (PLOrk), an ensemble where each performer plays a laptop computer paired with a custom hemispherical "orb" speaker instead of a traditional instrument. A hemispherical speaker projects sound in many directions at once, similar to how a violin or cello radiates sound around a room, rather than beaming sound forward like a typical stereo speaker. The authors describe how they designed the hardware, wrote much of the software in ChucK, and built a teaching curriculum so students without a traditional performance background could compose and perform together live. It is a foundational case study in ensemble design for "many people, many laptops, one orchestra."
