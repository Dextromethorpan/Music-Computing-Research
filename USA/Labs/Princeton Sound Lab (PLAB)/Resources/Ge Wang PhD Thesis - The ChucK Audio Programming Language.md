This PhD dissertation is the full, in-depth account of how and why ChucK was designed, explaining its underlying philosophy of giving programmers extremely precise, moment-to-moment control over timing in sound (called "strongly-timed" programming) while still allowing live, on-the-fly changes to running code. It covers the language's internal technical design — how it represents time and runs multiple processes at once — as well as its use as a creative and teaching tool, including in the Princeton Laptop Orchestra. The thesis remains the most complete technical reference for understanding ChucK's internals and the ideas behind treating programming itself as a musical instrument.
