Hyperinstruments is the foundational project of the whole Opera of the Future group, started by Tod Machover in the late 1980s. The idea is to add sensors and computer intelligence to musical instruments (or to a performer's body) so that a skilled musician's small movements — bow pressure, breath, gesture — can control extra layers of sound beyond what the acoustic instrument alone can produce. Famous performers such as cellist Yo-Yo Ma, and pop artists like Prince and Peter Gabriel, have used Hyperinstruments in performances. The project also expanded into instruments for people with limited mobility, letting them compose and perform music through simplified gestures. Hyperinstruments set the template for most later projects in the group: use sensors and software to extend, not replace, human musical expression.
