This is a research project page from UCSD's Sonic Arts R&D group (based at the Qualcomm Institute, and descended from CRCA's audio research tradition) on personalizing Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTFs) — the way each individual person's head, ears, and shoulders subtly change the sound of something before it reaches their eardrums, which is what lets us judge where a sound is coming from in 3D space. Off-the-shelf, "average" HRTFs used in headphone-based 3D audio (like in games, VR, or spatial audio apps) often don't match a given listener's own ears well, making virtual sounds feel like they're inside the head rather than out in the world. Personalizing the HRTF to an individual improves how convincingly 3D audio is perceived through headphones. Sonic Arts R&D has industry collaborators including Google, DTS, and Qualcomm.
