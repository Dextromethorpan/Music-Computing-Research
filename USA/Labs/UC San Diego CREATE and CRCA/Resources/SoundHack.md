SoundHack is a spectral sound-processing application created by UC San Diego music professor Tom Erbe, and one of the earliest freeware computer music programs. "Spectral processing" means analyzing a sound's frequency content over time and manipulating it directly — for example, freezing a moment of sound, stretching it in time without changing its pitch, or cross-blending the spectrum of two different sounds. SoundHack's effects became well known partly because they were used in the sound design for the film The Matrix. Erbe has continued developing spectral tools (as freeware and, later, commercial plugins) for over three decades.
