An "oscillator" is the basic building block of electronic sound — a circuit or piece of code that repeats a wave over and over to produce a pitch. Most digital oscillators just track a single repeating angle (like a clock hand going around a circle) to know where they are in the wave. This paper proposes a new kind of oscillator that instead tracks its position using "quaternions" — a way of describing rotation in three dimensions (the same math used in 3D graphics and robotics) — which lets the oscillator trace much richer, non-repeating paths through sound instead of a simple repeating loop. The result is a family of new, more complex electronic tones that stay stable (they don't blow up or die out) while sounding less mechanically periodic than typical digital oscillators.
