This award-winning paper describes an experiment in using AI to interpret "Treatise," a famous 1960s graphic score by composer Cornelius Cardew that uses abstract lines and shapes instead of standard musical notation, leaving performers to interpret it freely. The researchers used a large language model (like ChatGPT) to look at the graphic score and describe what it suggests in words, then fed those text descriptions into MusicLDM, a pre-trained AI model that generates audio from text prompts, to produce the actual sound. The project is an example of treating AI not as a tool that just fills in a template, but as an improvising collaborator that makes its own interpretive choices, similar to how a human performer would interpret an open, graphic score.
