This paper describes a new kind of musical instrument built with a Leap Motion controller, a small sensor that tracks the position and shape of a person's hand in the air without touching anything. The researchers mapped different hand shapes and movements to "timbre" — the quality or color of a sound that lets you tell, say, a violin from a trumpet even when they play the same note — so that moving your hand changes the character of a continuously playing electronic sound in real time. The instrument was built and performed live as a duet piece called "Who Was That Timbre I Saw You With?" The work is a practical example of designing a gesture-controlled interface for expressive electronic music performance.
