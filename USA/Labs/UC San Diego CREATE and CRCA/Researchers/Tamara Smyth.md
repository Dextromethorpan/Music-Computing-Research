Tamara Smyth researches "physical modeling synthesis" — building sound not by recording an instrument but by simulating, with math and code, how the instrument's physical parts (like a vibrating string, an air column in a trombone, or the human vocal tract) actually behave, so a computer can generate realistic instrument sounds from first principles. Her work combines this with digital signal processing, musical acoustics, and human-computer interaction to design new musical instrument technology. She teaches UCSD's graduate course on physical modeling, which continues the department's long-running computer music curriculum.
