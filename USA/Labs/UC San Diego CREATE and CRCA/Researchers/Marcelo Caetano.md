Marcelo Caetano studies how the sound of musical instruments can be analyzed, transformed, and "morphed" into one another using signal processing — for example gradually turning the sound of a violin into a flute, which is useful both for understanding how humans perceive timbre (the quality that distinguishes one instrument or voice from another) and for giving composers new creative tools. He earned his PhD in signal processing from UPMC Paris 6/IRCAM, worked at research institutes in Greece, Portugal, and Spain, and led a Marie-Curie project on sound morphing (MORPH) at CIRMMT-McGill and CNRS-PRISM before joining UCSD's Music Department in 2023.
