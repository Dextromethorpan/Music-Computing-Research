This paper introduces a robot built to play a real, commercially available acoustic guitar with the kind of nuance a skilled human guitarist has — controlling how loud or soft each note is, timing notes down to the millisecond, and performing techniques like strumming, picking, muted "overtone" notes, and hammer-ons (sounding a note by tapping a fret instead of picking the string). The team built the robot's hardware — including a robotic arm for strumming/plucking and a separate 12-motor module that shifts along the guitar's neck to select frets — by first studying how human guitarists actually move and play, then encoding those patterns into the robot's control software. Tests showed the robot's playing was rated close to human-level in expressiveness by listeners, which matters because most previous robotic instrument players sounded stiff or mechanical.
