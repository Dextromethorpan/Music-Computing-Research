Gil Weinberg's team built a robotic prosthetic arm for Jason Barnes, a drummer who lost part of his right arm in an accident. The prosthesis holds two drumsticks: one is controlled directly by Barnes using electromyography (EMG), a technology that reads electrical signals from his remaining arm muscles, while the second stick moves on its own, "listening" to the music and improvising rhythms a human hand alone could not physically produce (like extremely fast, evenly-timed hits). This project grew directly out of GTCMT's robotic musicianship research on Shimon, applying the same idea — a machine that listens and responds musically — to help a person play again and even exceed normal human drumming ability. Barnes has since performed at venues including the Kennedy Center.
