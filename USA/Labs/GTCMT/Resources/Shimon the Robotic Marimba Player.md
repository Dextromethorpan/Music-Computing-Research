Shimon is a robot that physically plays a marimba (a large wooden xylophone) using four robotic arms with mallets, and it improvises music in real time alongside human musicians. Instead of just following a pre-written score, Shimon "listens" to the human players, analyzes what they're playing using models of how people perceive music, and generates its own musical responses on the fly — a skill researchers call "robotic musicianship." Because Shimon is a physical robot rather than a speaker playing computer-generated audio, its sound is naturally rich (real mallets hitting real wood) and its motions let human collaborators see and anticipate what it's about to play, similar to watching a human bandmate. Shimon has performed at major public events including the World Economic Forum and Google I/O, and later versions gained the ability to sing and rap as well as play.
