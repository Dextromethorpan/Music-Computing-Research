This article, written by a classical music producer for the label NMC, describes a real production that used Blue Ripple Sound's tools. The producer recorded an ensemble album using an ambisonic microphone to capture the room as a full 3D soundfield, then used Blue Ripple's plugins (including reverbs and a custom decoder) to build a master 3D mix. A newer Blue Ripple plugin then converted that ambisonic mix into a "dome" of virtual speakers that could be exported straight into Dolby Atmos, and also into a binaural (headphone-based 3D audio) version. It's a good concrete example of Blue Ripple's research-grade Ambisonics tools being used to bridge older 3D-audio formats with modern consumer spatial-audio platforms like Dolby Atmos and Apple Spatial Audio.
