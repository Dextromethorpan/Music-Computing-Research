This is a real, granted US patent (No. 12,524,462; filed November 2022, granted January 2026) assigned to Audoo Limited, co-invented by CTO Stephen (Steve) Robbins. It describes the core logic of a "media identification system": the device takes in an audio signal, cuts out a short clip of it, and analyses that clip to generate metadata (descriptive information about the clip). Only if that metadata indicates the clip actually contains music — as opposed to speech, silence, or background noise — does the device send identification data about it onward to a remote "media identification server" for matching against a song database. Beginner note: this filtering step matters practically, since it means the device doesn't waste bandwidth or processing sending non-music sound for matching, and it is one concrete piece of engineering evidence behind Audoo's claim that no non-music audio is captured or transmitted.
