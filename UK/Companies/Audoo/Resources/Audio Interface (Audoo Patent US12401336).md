This is a real, granted US patent (No. 12,401,336; filed March 2022, granted August 2025) assigned to Audoo Limited. It describes a small hardware circuit — an "audio interface" — that sits between an incoming audio line and the rest of the device. Real-world audio signals from different venues (a gym's amp, a shop's ceiling speakers) vary hugely in loudness, so the circuit automatically checks whether the incoming signal is too quiet or too loud, and applies gain (boosting) or attenuation (reducing) to bring it into the right range before passing it on. Beginner note: "amplitude" here just means how loud/strong the electrical audio signal is at a given moment. This kind of automatic level-adjustment is exactly the kind of engineering problem a device like the Audoo Audio Meter has to solve if it's going to reliably "hear" and fingerprint music across thousands of differently-wired venues.
