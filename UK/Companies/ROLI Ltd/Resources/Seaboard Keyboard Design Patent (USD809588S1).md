Unlike a normal "utility" patent that protects how something works, this is a design patent — it legally protects the distinctive visual look and shape of ROLI's Seaboard keyboard instrument itself (its wavy, rippled, piano-key-like silicone surface). It's a good, simple example of how a hardware company protects the actual industrial design of a product, separately from the underlying sensor technology (covered by other ROLI patents such as US 8,994,648). For a beginner, it's useful evidence that the Seaboard's now-famous rippled keyboard shape was a deliberate, legally protected design decision, not just a manufacturing side effect.
