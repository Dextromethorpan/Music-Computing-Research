This is one of ROLI's foundational patents, filed while founder Roland Lamb was still developing the idea behind the Seaboard as a graduate student. It describes an "interface for inputting data into a processor" built from a three-dimensionally shaped, soft, squishable surface sitting on top of a grid of pressure sensors: when a finger presses anywhere on the surface, the soft material transmits that force down to the sensors below, which work out both how hard the finger is pressing and exactly where on the surface it is. In beginner's terms, this is the patent that describes the physical trick behind the Seaboard's rubbery, piano-like keys — turning a soft touch surface into precise, continuous multi-finger control data, which is what later became the raw input ROLI's instruments send out as MPE data.
