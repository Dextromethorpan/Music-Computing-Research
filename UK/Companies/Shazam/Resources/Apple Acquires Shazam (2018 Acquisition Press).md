On 24 September 2018, Apple closed its acquisition of the London-founded company Shazam for a reported $400 million, after the European Commission cleared the deal earlier that month. Apple's own announcement framed the acquisition around Shazam's audio-recognition technology — the same "audio fingerprinting" approach from Avery Wang's 2003 paper — which Apple wanted to fold into Apple Music, Siri, and iOS. The reporting also notes that the app would become ad-free under Apple. This resource is included as the key business/press record of how Shazam's UK-originated research and technology ended up owned by a US tech giant, and it links to Apple's later ShazamKit framework (2021), which lets any developer build apps using Shazam's recognition technology.
