This is one of Shazam's foundational patents, covering the same core idea as the 2003 Wang paper: recognizing an audio sample by finding the closest match in a large database, where each stored song is represented by a set of "landmark" timepoints (reliably reproducible peaks in the sound) and a "fingerprint" describing the signal near each landmark. In plain terms, it patents the method of turning a song into a short list of distinctive time-stamped codes so a short, noisy recording can be compared against millions of songs quickly, without needing to compare full audio waveforms. This patent (and its later continuations) formed part of the legal and technical foundation that let Shazam scale to a global song-identification service before those rights transferred to Apple after the 2018 acquisition.
