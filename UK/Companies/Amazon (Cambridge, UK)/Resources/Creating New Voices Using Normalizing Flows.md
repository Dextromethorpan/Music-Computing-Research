Making a text-to-speech system speak with a completely new voice usually means recording plenty of audio from a real person first. This paper investigates whether a mathematical technique called "normalizing flows" (a way of building generative models that can be run forwards or backwards exactly) can create entirely new, plausible-sounding voices that were never in the training data at all — with no recordings of that "person" needed. Their approach can both convert existing speech into a new synthetic voice and generate speech from scratch in that new voice, and it reached state-of-the-art results for realism and speaker consistency at the time — a step toward speech and voice-assistant technology that isn't limited to a small fixed set of pre-recorded voices.
