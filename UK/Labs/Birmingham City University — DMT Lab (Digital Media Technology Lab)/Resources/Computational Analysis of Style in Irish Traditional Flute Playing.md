This PhD thesis (Birmingham City University, 2019) develops computer methods for analysing what makes one Irish traditional flute player's performance style sound different from another's — things like how they place and shape their ornaments (the fast decorative notes described in the lab's related ISMIR 2014 paper), timing, and phrasing. The work includes building and carefully annotating a collection of Irish flute recordings so that computers can be trained and tested on real performances, then using signal-processing and pattern-recognition methods to pull out measurable, comparable style features from the audio. The result is a computational toolkit for studying performance style in folk music traditions that were previously analysed almost entirely by ear.
