This ISMIR 2016 paper tackles "automatic drum transcription" — teaching a computer to listen to a recording and write out exactly which drum (kick, snare, hi-hat, etc.) was hit and when, without a human doing it by ear. The authors use a type of neural network called a bi-directional recurrent neural network, which reads audio both forwards and backwards in time so it can use context from both before and after a drum hit to decide what instrument it heard. At the time, this approach pushed the accuracy of automatic drum transcription systems higher than earlier methods, forming the foundation the DMT Lab's later drum-transcription work built on.
