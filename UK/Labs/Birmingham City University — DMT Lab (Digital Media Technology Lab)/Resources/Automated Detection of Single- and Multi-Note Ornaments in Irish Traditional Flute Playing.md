In Irish traditional flute music, players decorate melodies with small, fast embellishments called "ornaments" — quick extra notes, trills, or cuts woven around the main melody notes — and the specific style of ornamentation is one of the clearest fingerprints of a musician's individual playing style. This ISMIR 2014 paper builds an automated system that listens to a recording and detects where these ornaments occur, distinguishing simple single-note ornaments from more complex multi-note ones. Doing this by ear normally requires an expert with deep knowledge of the tradition, so an automated detector opens the door to studying large collections of folk recordings systematically rather than one recording at a time.
