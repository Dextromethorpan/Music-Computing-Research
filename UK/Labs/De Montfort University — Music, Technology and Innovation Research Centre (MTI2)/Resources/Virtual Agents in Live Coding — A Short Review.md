This paper surveys past attempts to bring "virtual agents" — software helpers that can act semi-independently, sometimes using AI — into live coding performances, where a musician programs music in real time in front of an audience. Anna Xambó (formerly of MTI2) reviews how these agents have been used to suggest musical material, respond to what a performer is doing, or even improvise alongside them, and discusses open questions about how much creative control should be handed over to the software versus kept by the human performer. It's a useful map of the field for anyone curious about AI as a collaborator in live music-making rather than just a tool.
