This paper, presented at the International Computer Music Conference (ICMC) 2011, introduces a new way to generate unusual, evolving sounds called "Compressed Feedback Synthesis" (CFS). It works by feeding a sound's own output back into itself in a loop (feedback), while an automatic gain control — the same kind of technology used to keep volume levels steady in broadcasting — constantly adjusts the loop's strength. The result behaves like a supercharged version of a "comb filter" (a standard effect that boosts or cancels certain frequencies), producing rich, unpredictable textures that composers can shape. Bret Battey, a composer and professor at MTI2, uses this technique in his own algorithmic and audiovisual compositions.
