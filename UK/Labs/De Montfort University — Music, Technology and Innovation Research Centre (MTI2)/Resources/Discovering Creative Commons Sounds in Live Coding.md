"Live coding" is a style of music performance where the musician writes and edits computer code on stage in real time to generate the sound the audience hears. This article, published in the journal Organised Sound, looks at how live coders can search and pull in sounds from Freesound — a huge, free online library of "Creative Commons" licensed audio clips (meaning they're free for anyone to reuse) — while performing live, rather than pre-loading a fixed set of samples beforehand. Anna Xambó, who worked at MTI2, studies how giving performers on-the-fly access to a vast shared sound library changes their creative process and what tools make that kind of searching fast enough to use mid-performance.
