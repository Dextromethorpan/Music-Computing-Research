Presented at the Sound and Music Computing Conference 2024, this project uses laser Doppler vibrometry — a technique that measures the tiny vibrations of a surface by bouncing a laser off it, without touching the object — to capture how everyday objects vibrate and turn those measurements into raw material for sound design. Instead of recording sound with a microphone, the researcher records physical vibration directly, which can pick up detail a microphone would miss and opens up new ways to create realistic or unusual synthetic sound effects for games, film, or immersive audio (sound designed to surround a listener, as in VR).
