Philip Jackson works on spatial audio — recording, reproducing, and evaluating sound so that it feels like it's coming from the right place in 3D space — as well as machine listening and audio-visual perception (combining what a system "hears" with what it "sees" to better understand a scene, such as tracking who is speaking in a video). He led Surrey's research in the S3A programme on next-generation spatial audio with BBC R&D, and now leads a research stream on personalised media production within the £15m AI4ME Prosperity Partnership. He supervised PhD student Davide Berghi's work on audio-visual speaker detection and localization.
