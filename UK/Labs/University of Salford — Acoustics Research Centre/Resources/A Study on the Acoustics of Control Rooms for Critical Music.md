A "control room" is the room where a sound engineer sits at the mixing desk while recording or mixing music — if that room's own acoustics colour the sound, the engineer can't trust what they're hearing and may make bad mixing decisions. This study by Bruno Fazenda, whose PhD and career at Salford have centred on room acoustics and psychoacoustics, examines how to design and evaluate control rooms so they give an accurate, uncoloured picture of a recording, particularly at low frequencies (bass), where small rooms are notoriously prone to problems because sound waves bounce around in ways that boost or cancel certain bass notes depending on where you sit. The paper is closely tied to Salford's own teaching studios, which were built following this design thinking, and feeds into broader Acoustics Research Centre work on critical listening spaces used across their audio engineering research.
