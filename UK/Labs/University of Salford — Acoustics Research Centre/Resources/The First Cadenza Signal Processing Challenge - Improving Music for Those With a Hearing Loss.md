The Cadenza project asks a simple but under-researched question: how should music sound different for someone with a hearing loss, so that it's still enjoyable rather than just louder? This paper launches Cadenza's first open research competition, inviting machine-learning researchers worldwide to build systems that automatically remix or process music tracks (starting with electronic dance/club music, which is dense and hard to hear clearly with hearing loss) to make them clearer and more enjoyable for hearing aid users. It explains how entries are judged using both computer-predicted "intelligibility" scores and real listening tests with people who have hearing loss. Salford's Acoustics Research Centre — spanning several of the co-authors here — is one of the universities running the wider Cadenza programme alongside Leeds, Sheffield, Nottingham and Glasgow, with support from the BBC, Google and the hearing-loss charity RNID.
