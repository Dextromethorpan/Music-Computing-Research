What actually makes a music recording sound "good"? This Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper, part of Salford's "Good Recording Project" (run with BBC R&D and the British Library Sound Archive), took real songs from commercial CDs and had listeners rate them for both perceived audio quality and how much they simply liked the song. The surprising finding: liking and quality are different things — how familiar someone was with a song strongly affected how much they liked it, but had little to do with how they rated its technical quality, which instead tracked measurable signal properties like loudness and how much the dynamic range had been compressed (squashed to sound louder). The paper also found that people describe quality using a fairly consistent vocabulary covering tone colour, sense of space, and audible flaws — useful for anyone trying to build automated tools that judge recording quality the way a human would.
