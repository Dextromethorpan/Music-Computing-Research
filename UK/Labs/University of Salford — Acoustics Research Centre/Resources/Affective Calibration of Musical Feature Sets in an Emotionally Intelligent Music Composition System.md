Can a computer write music aimed at making a listener feel a specific emotion, like tension or calm? This paper — co-authored by Duncan Williams, now a Senior Lecturer at Salford's Acoustics Research Centre — describes a system for "affectively driven algorithmic composition," meaning software that automatically generates new music tuned to a target emotional quality rather than a fixed melody someone wrote by hand. The system was tested by generating short musical stimuli meant to sit at nine different points across a two-dimensional map of emotion (how positive/negative and how calm/excited a sound feels), then checking whether listeners actually rated the results the way the system intended. It's an early, rigorous example of using measurable musical features (like rhythm density or harmony) as dials a computer can turn to steer emotional response.
