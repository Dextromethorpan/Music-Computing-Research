"Immersive spatial audio" means sound that surrounds a listener in 3D, so it feels like being at a live concert or event rather than just hearing sound from two stereo speakers. This project, part of the EPSRC-funded S3A programme (which Salford's Acoustics Research Centre helped lead alongside Surrey, Southampton and BBC Research & Development), came up with a clever trick called Media Device Orchestration: instead of needing an expensive purpose-built surround-sound speaker setup, it uses whatever ordinary devices are already in the room — a laptop, a phone, a smart speaker — and coordinates them together as an impromptu surround array. The system splits an audio mix into separate "objects" (like a commentator's voice, crowd noise, or music) and sends each one to whichever nearby device is best placed to play it, creating a 3D sound experience out of everyday gadgets. It was presented at the Audio Mostly conference and demonstrated with BBC content.
