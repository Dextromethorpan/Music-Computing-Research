This paper is a long-term case study, not a typical technical paper. It follows composer and performer Laetitia Sonami, who has spent eight years building and refining an unusual gesture-controlled instrument called the "Spring Spyre" using Wekinator, the interactive machine-learning tool created by Rebecca Fiebrink (a former EAVI/Goldsmiths researcher). Instead of reporting a new algorithm, it reflects honestly on what actually happens when a working artist relies on machine learning in a musical instrument over many years: what worked, what broke, what had to be redesigned, and how Sonami's ongoing practice shaped later versions of the Wekinator software itself. It's valuable as a rare, grounded look at the everyday realities of using AI/ML tools in real creative practice rather than in a lab demo.
