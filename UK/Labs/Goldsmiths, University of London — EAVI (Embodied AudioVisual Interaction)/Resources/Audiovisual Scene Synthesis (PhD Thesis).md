This PhD thesis, completed at Goldsmiths/EAVI, explores how a computer can automatically generate new combined sound-and-image "scenes" by learning from large collections of audiovisual material — essentially a computational form of collage-making. It draws on ideas from human perception research (how our eyes and ears combine information) and builds computational models that borrow and recombine fragments of existing audio and video to synthesize new scenes with a coherent audiovisual character. It's an early example of generative, learning-based media synthesis research done in a music/sound computing context, years before today's wave of AI-generated audio and video tools. The full thesis text and source files are openly available on GitHub.
