The Myo armband was a commercial wristband that reads the tiny electrical signals your muscles produce when you flex them (called EMG, or electromyography) plus how your arm is moving and tilting. Myo Mapper is a free, open-source app built at EAVI that takes that raw sensor data and converts it into a standard message format called OSC (Open Sound Control), which almost any music software can understand. In practice this means a performer can flex or twist their arm and have that instantly control a synthesizer, sample player, or visual effect, without needing to write custom code to talk to the armband. The accompanying paper, presented at the NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression) conference, explains the design choices and how musicians used it to build new gesture-controlled instruments.
