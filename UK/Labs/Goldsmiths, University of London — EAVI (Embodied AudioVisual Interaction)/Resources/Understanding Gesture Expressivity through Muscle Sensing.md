When a musician moves expressively — say, a tense, forceful gesture versus a relaxed, gentle one — that expressiveness isn't just in how the hand moves through space, it's also in how hard the muscles are working underneath. This paper (published in ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction) studies how muscle activity, captured via EMG (electromyography) sensors on the skin, relates to a performer's sense of "effort" and expressive intent during musical gesture. The researchers ran experiments with performers and analyzed the muscle data to build a clearer picture of what expressive movement "looks like" at the level of the body's own electrical signals, which helps designers build instruments that respond not just to where a hand is, but to how it's moving.
