"Timbre" is the particular quality or color of a sound — what makes a violin note sound different from a trumpet note even when they're playing the same pitch. This paper describes a system that lets a performer control fine details of timbre using gesture (arm and hand movement captured via sensors), by connecting the two through machine learning. It uses a technique called "concatenative synthesis," which builds new sounds by intelligently stitching together tiny fragments (grains) of pre-recorded audio chosen to match a target quality — here, chosen based on the performer's movement. The result is an instrument where subtle changes in gesture produce subtle, related changes in the resulting sound's texture, rather than a simple on/off trigger.
