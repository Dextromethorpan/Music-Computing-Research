"Beat tracking" is the task of getting a computer to tap along to the beat of a piece of music, the way a human foot naturally does. This paper by C4DM's Simon Dixon describes and evaluates BeatRoot, one of the earliest and most influential beat-tracking systems. Rather than committing to a single guess, BeatRoot runs several competing hypotheses at once about where the beat is and how fast it's going, letting the most convincing one win as more of the music plays — so it recovers quickly if it "loses the beat" and degrades gracefully on tricky, ambiguous rhythms. The paper reports a large-scale evaluation showing how accurately the system tracks beats across many different recordings. BeatRoot's ideas underpin much of the beat- and rhythm-tracking research that followed at C4DM and elsewhere.
