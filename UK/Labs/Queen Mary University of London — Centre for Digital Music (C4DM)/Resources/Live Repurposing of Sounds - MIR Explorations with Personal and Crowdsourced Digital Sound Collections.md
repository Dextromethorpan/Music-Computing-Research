This NIME conference paper by C4DM's Anna Xambó explores a performance style called "live coding," where a musician writes and edits computer code on stage in real time to generate music, with the audience often watching the code itself as part of the show. The paper looks at how performers can pull sounds from large crowdsourced sound-effect libraries (like Freesound) or their own personal recordings and instantly search, retrieve, and repurpose them live, using music information retrieval techniques (automatically analysing audio to find sounds with particular qualities) rather than pre-loading everything in advance. It studies how this changes the performer's creative workflow and what new interface and interaction challenges it raises — connecting live coding practice with search-driven sound design.
