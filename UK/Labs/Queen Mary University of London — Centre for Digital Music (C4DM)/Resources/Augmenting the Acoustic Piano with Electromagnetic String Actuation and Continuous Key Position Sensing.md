This NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression) conference paper describes the Magnetic Resonator Piano, a project led by Andrew McPherson during his time at C4DM. It uses electromagnets placed near a normal acoustic grand piano's strings to make them vibrate directly — without any loudspeakers — producing effects an ordinary piano can't do, like notes that swell in from silence, sustain forever, or bend in pitch. A separate optical sensor under the keys tracks the exact position of every key continuously (not just "pressed" or "not pressed"), capturing subtle finger gestures a normal piano keyboard ignores. Together these let a pianist play using completely normal technique while also unlocking a much wider range of expressive sound, blending acoustic and electronic instrument design. It's a landmark example of C4DM's "augmented instruments" research, which explores adding new sensing and sound-producing technology onto or into existing acoustic instruments.
