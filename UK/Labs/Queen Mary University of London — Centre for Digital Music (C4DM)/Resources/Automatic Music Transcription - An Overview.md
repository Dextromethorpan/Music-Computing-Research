Automatic Music Transcription (AMT) is the task of teaching a computer to turn a recording of music into readable notation (the note names, timings, and loudness that a human would write on a score, similar to how speech-to-text turns spoken words into text). This overview paper, published in IEEE Signal Processing Magazine and co-authored by C4DM's Emmanouil Benetos and Simon Dixon alongside researchers from Rochester and Fraunhofer, surveys the whole AMT field: detecting which notes are playing and when (pitch and rhythm), figuring out which instrument is playing them, and capturing subtle expressive timing. It explains the main families of methods, from classic signal-processing techniques to deep learning, and lays out open challenges such as transcribing dense, multi-instrument recordings accurately. It's a widely cited entry point for understanding how "music to sheet music" software actually works.
