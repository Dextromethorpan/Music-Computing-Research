This paper, co-authored by C4DM's Josh Reiss (with a C4DM PhD alumnus and an Adobe researcher), tackles a practical studio problem: making one recording's audio effects (equalisation, compression, reverb, and so on) sound like they were applied in the style of a different reference recording — for example, making a rough home recording sound processed the way a professional reference track was. The system is built from "differentiable" audio-effect building blocks, meaning the effects are implemented in a way a neural network can automatically learn to adjust, letting it match the target style through training rather than requiring an engineer to hand-tune every parameter. This approach, called differentiable digital signal processing, connects traditional audio engineering knowledge with modern deep learning and has become influential in intelligent audio production research.
