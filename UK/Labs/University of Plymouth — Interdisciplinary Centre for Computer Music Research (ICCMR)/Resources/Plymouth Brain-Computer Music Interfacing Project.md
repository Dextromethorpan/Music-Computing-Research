This paper reports the progress of ICCMR's flagship research programme in Brain-Computer Music Interfacing (BCMI) — technology that lets people make music using signals detected directly from their brain, with no need to move a hand or press a key. It surveys tools built at Plymouth (and with the University of Glasgow) for reading brainwave signals with EEG (electroencephalography, a way of measuring electrical activity on the scalp), turning aspects of those signals into an "EEG audio mixer," and shaping musical compositions informed by cognitive neuroscience (the study of how the brain supports thought and perception). The work is explicitly aimed at people with severe motor impairments who cannot otherwise play an instrument, as well as at music therapy and entertainment applications.
