This PhD thesis, completed at ICCMR, focuses on making brain-computer music interfacing (BCMI) practical — moving it beyond one-off lab demos toward systems that can reliably be used for real creative and performance work. It tackles the everyday engineering problems of building a working BCMI system: picking up meaningful signals from affordable EEG headsets, filtering out noise, and turning fuzzy brain-signal patterns into musically useful control data (for example, controlling tempo or picking which sounds play). The thesis also documents actual compositions and performances built with these systems, showing how the technology moves from research into artistic use.
