This paper reports one of ICCMR's earliest practical demonstrations of using a quantum computer to actually make sound, rather than just simulate quantum ideas on paper. The team used quantum information processing to control an "inverse FFT" sound synthesizer — the Fourier Transform is a standard technique that converts sound into a spectrum of frequencies, and running it backwards (inverse FFT) turns a chosen spectrum back into audible sound, so the quantum computer effectively gets to "paint" sounds by choosing frequencies. They also built an adaptive musical sequencer (software that decides what note or sound event comes next) driven by quantum computation. A resulting composition, "Zeno," is presented as a real-world piece of music built with this approach.
