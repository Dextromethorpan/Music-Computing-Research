Song of the Sea is an ICCMR project that turns real weather and ocean measurements from a 2014 storm — wind speed, air pressure, temperature and wave height, recorded every 30 minutes — into changes in a recording of the traditional sea shanty "Drunken Sailor." This technique of mapping data to sound is called sonification: instead of showing the storm as a graph, the project lets you hear it, as rising wind speed speeds up the tempo, falling air pressure deepens and slurs the vocals, and growing wave height distorts the words with an echo effect. The project was released around the COP26 climate conference to make the abstract story of climate change viscerally audible rather than just something you read about in numbers.
