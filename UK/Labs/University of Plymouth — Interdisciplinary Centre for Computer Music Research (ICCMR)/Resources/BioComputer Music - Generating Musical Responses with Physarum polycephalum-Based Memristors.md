This paper describes one of ICCMR's most unusual projects: building electronic circuit components out of a living, single-celled slime mould organism called Physarum polycephalum, then using them to make music with a human pianist. The slime mould grows into thin strands that behave like "memristors" — electrical components whose resistance changes based on their history of use, similar to a very simple biological memory. The team wired these living memristors into a circuit connected to electromagnets placed on piano strings, so the organism's changing electrical behaviour could trigger and shape musical responses during a live piano duet between a human and the slime mould. It's an early, concrete example of "biocomputing" — building computers out of living material instead of silicon chips — applied to music.
