"Beat tracking" is the task of getting a computer to find the steady pulse in a piece of recorded music — the same beat you'd naturally tap your foot to — which is useful for DJ software, music analysis, and syncing visuals to music. Most AI beat trackers need large datasets of songs that have been manually labeled with exactly where each beat falls, which is expensive to produce. This paper introduces a "self-supervised" method — meaning the model teaches itself useful patterns from unlabeled music by solving an easier related puzzle first — that reaches strong beat-tracking accuracy with little to no manually labeled data, published in a leading IEEE audio-processing journal.
