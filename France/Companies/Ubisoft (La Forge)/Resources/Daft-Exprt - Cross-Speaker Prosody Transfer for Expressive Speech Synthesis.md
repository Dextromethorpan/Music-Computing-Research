This paper describes a system that lets a video game generate spoken dialogue in a "borrowed" performance style — for example, making a character's voice sound excited, calm, or sarcastic — even from a voice actor's neutral recording. "Prosody" refers to the melody, rhythm, and emphasis of speech (the parts of talking that aren't just which words are said). The system, called Daft-Exprt, learns to transfer this expressive style from one speaker's recording onto any text spoken by a different voice, which is useful for games that need thousands of dialogue lines with limited recording budgets. It builds on text-to-speech (TTS) technology, meaning software that turns written text into spoken audio.
