Most music is recorded in "stereo," meaning it only has a left and a right channel — like sound coming from two speakers in front of you. "Spatial" or "immersive" audio instead places different instruments and voices all around the listener (above, behind, to the sides), closer to how we actually hear sound in the real world, and is what powers formats like Dolby Atmos on streaming services. Stereo to Spatial is a tool, built on IRCAM's sound-spatialization research, that automatically converts an existing stereo song into this immersive spatial format using an AI engine — without needing the original separate instrument tracks ("stems"). Ircam Amplify built this by training the AI on thousands of tracks so the converted mix keeps the artist's original intent rather than sounding artificial. It launched as a beta program in 2023 with music distributor Believe/TuneCore as an early adopter, aimed at making spatial audio production affordable for artists and labels who could not previously access it.
