DeepBach is a computer program that writes four-part chorale music (the kind Johann Sebastian Bach wrote for choir, with soprano, alto, tenor and bass parts) in a style that sounds convincingly like Bach's own. It works by training a machine-learning model on hundreds of real Bach chorales so it learns the patterns of harmony and voice-leading Bach used. Unlike earlier systems that generate music strictly left-to-right, DeepBach can be "steered" — a user can fix some notes and let the model fill in the rest, similar to how a text autocomplete fills in missing words. In listening tests, people (including music experts) often could not tell DeepBach's chorales apart from real Bach. This paper, published at ICML 2017, was one of the projects that first put Sony CSL Paris on the map for AI music generation.
