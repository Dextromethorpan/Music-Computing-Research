Flow Machines was a research project (funded by a European Research Council grant and led by François Pachet at Sony CSL Paris) that built AI software able to learn the "style" of existing songs from a large database of music, then generate new melodies or full songs imitating that style. Its best-known demo, "Daddy's Car" (2016), is a full pop song composed by the AI in the style of the Beatles, with human composer Benoît Carré arranging it and writing the lyrics — one of the first widely publicized examples of an AI-assisted pop song. The project showed that a computer could capture recognizable stylistic fingerprints of a band and reuse them to write new, never-before-heard material, while still needing a human to arrange, produce and finish the track. Pachet left Sony CSL Paris for Spotify in 2017, but Flow Machines remains a foundational project in AI music generation and its prototype lives on as a tool on the current Sony CSL Music Team website.
