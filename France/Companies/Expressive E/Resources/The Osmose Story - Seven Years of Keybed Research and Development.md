This is Expressive E's own account of how Osmose was developed over roughly seven years, describing the engineering journey behind the Augmented Keyboard Action mechanism and its collaboration with Haken Audio (maker of the EaganMatrix sound engine used inside Osmose) to make the keybed's continuous, per-note sensing actually drive expressive, physically-modeled sounds in real time. It is a useful beginner-friendly look at how a "regular-looking" keyboard can hide a genuinely new sensing mechanism inside each key.
