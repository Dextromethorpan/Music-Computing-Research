This NIME (New Interfaces for Musical Expression) paper describes Amstramgrame, a GRAME program that brings simple music-technology activities into French schools to teach scientific ideas (like waveforms, frequency, and digital signal processing) through hands-on sound-making rather than abstract lessons. Using the Faust programming language and low-cost hardware, students build small musical devices themselves, which the authors argue makes otherwise dry technical concepts concrete and memorable.
