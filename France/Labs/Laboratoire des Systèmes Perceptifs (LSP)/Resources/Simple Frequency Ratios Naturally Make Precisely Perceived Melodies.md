This study looks at why some melodies feel more precise or "locked in" to the ear than others. The researchers found that when the pitches in a short melody relate to each other by simple mathematical ratios (like the ratios that create familiar musical intervals such as octaves or fifths), listeners perceive the tune far more accurately than when the pitches are more randomly related. This helps explain, from a hearing-science point of view, why certain scales and intervals recur so often in music around the world. It is a beginner-friendly example of how psychoacoustics (the science of how we hear) connects directly to music theory.
