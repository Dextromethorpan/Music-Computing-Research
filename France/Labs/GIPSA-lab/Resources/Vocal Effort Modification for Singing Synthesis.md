Singers naturally change how much physical "effort" they put into a note — singing softly and gently versus pushing hard and loud — and that effort changes the timbre of the sound, not just its volume. This paper, presented at Interspeech (a major speech-science conference), adds control over vocal effort to the Cantor Digitalis singing synthesizer, so a performer can shape not just the pitch and vowel of a synthesized voice but also how "effortful" it sounds. This makes computer-generated singing more expressive and closer to how a real human voice behaves under different levels of vocal energy.
