Cantor Digitalis is a digital "singing instrument" that lets a performer make a computer sing in real time using hand gestures instead of typing lyrics or a musical score. The performer controls a graphics tablet (or similar gesture-sensing device) with movements that map onto pitch, vowel sound, and vocal effort, similar to how a conductor's hand movements ("chironomy," an old term for conducting by hand signs) shape a performance moment by moment. Underneath, the system uses a parametric voice-synthesis model — a mathematical description of how a human voice is produced — that can be controlled smoothly and expressively rather than just played back from recordings. The paper, published in a leading audio/speech signal processing journal, describes the design of the instrument and the synthesis engine behind it; the software itself is freely available online.
