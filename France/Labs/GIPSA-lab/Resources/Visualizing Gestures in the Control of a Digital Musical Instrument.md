When a musician plays a "digital musical instrument" — a device that turns physical gestures like drawing on a tablet into sound rather than into ink — it can be hard for them (or a teacher, or an audience) to see exactly what gesture produced what sound. This paper, presented at NIME (a leading conference on New Interfaces for Musical Expression), explores ways to visually display the gestures used to control the Cantor Digitalis singing instrument, so performers can better understand, refine, and teach the physical movements that shape the resulting singing voice. It is a piece of interface-design research: making an otherwise invisible control action visible, in the same spirit as sheet music makes a performance visible and repeatable.
