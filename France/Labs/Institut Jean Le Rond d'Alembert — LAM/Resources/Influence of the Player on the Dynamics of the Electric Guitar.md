An electric guitar sitting on a stand vibrates differently than the same guitar being held and played by a musician — the player's body and grip change how the instrument's strings and body vibrate, and therefore how it sounds. This paper, published in the Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, measures and analyzes exactly how much the human player changes the guitar's vibration behavior compared to the instrument alone. This matters for musical acoustics research generally, and specifically for anyone trying to build accurate sound-synthesis or simulation models of electric guitars, since ignoring the player's influence can make a virtual instrument sound noticeably "off."
