Every musical instrument's body has natural ways it likes to vibrate, called "modes" — think of them like the instrument's own resonant frequencies, similar to how blowing across a bottle makes it hum at a specific pitch. This paper identifies two important vibration modes of the concert harp's soundbox (called A0, an air-related mode, and T1, a structural mode) using both measurements on real harps and mathematical modeling. Understanding these modes helps explain why a harp sounds the way it does and gives instrument makers (luthiers) and acoustics researchers a scientific basis for understanding or even improving the instrument's design.
