The clavichord is an old keyboard instrument (a predecessor of the piano) where a small metal blade called a "tangent" strikes and stays in contact with the string, letting the player control the sound's pitch and even a slight vibrato by varying finger pressure after the key is pressed — a subtlety no other keyboard instrument allows. This paper builds a "physical model," meaning a computer simulation grounded in the actual physics of vibrating strings and mechanical contact, that reproduces how the clavichord produces sound from the moment the tangent strikes the string onward. Such models are useful both for understanding an old and delicate instrument scientifically and, potentially, for building realistic computer-based (digital) versions of it that respond to a player the way the real thing does.
