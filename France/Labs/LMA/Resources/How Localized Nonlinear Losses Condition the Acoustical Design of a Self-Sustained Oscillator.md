This paper studies the clarinet's "register hole" - a small hole that, when opened, helps the player jump to a higher-pitched register - and how energy is lost in a very localized, non-simple ("nonlinear") way right at that hole as air rushes through it. The authors show that this small, localized loss actually plays an important role in shaping the acoustics of the whole instrument. Getting this right matters for instrument makers refining real clarinets and for anyone building an accurate physics-based digital clarinet.
