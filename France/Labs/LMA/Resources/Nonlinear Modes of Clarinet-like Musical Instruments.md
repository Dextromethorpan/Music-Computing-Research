A clarinet makes sound because a vibrating reed and the air column inside the instrument's tube constantly interact and reinforce each other, a "self-sustained oscillation." This paper treats that whole system mathematically as a nonlinear vibrating system (rather than a simple, linear one) and shows that even a deliberately simplified clarinet model has surprisingly rich and complex behavior as it starts to sound. Understanding this complexity matters for building accurate physical models that can be turned into realistic digital synthesizers of wind instruments.
