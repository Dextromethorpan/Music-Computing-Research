Most modern AI music systems are "data-driven": they learn everything from huge piles of examples, with no built-in knowledge of how instruments work or how music is put together. This overview article argues for a middle path called "model-based deep learning," where engineers bake real knowledge about sound and music directly into the AI system instead of making it learn everything from scratch. It walks through three real examples: separating individual singers out of a choir recording by giving the AI a built-in model of how a human voice is produced; matching a synthesized sound to a target sound using an distance measure closer to how our ears actually perceive similarity; and generating new piano music that stays faithful to a repeated musical theme, the way real compositions repeat and vary a core idea. The overall lesson is that adding a bit of music/audio expertise into an AI's design can make it need less training data, be easier to control, and produce more musically sensible results.
