Teaching a computer to listen to a piano recording and write out the sheet music it hears — a task called "automatic music transcription" — requires many recordings of real piano notes paired with the exact correct answer of which keys were pressed and when. MAPS is a large database built at Télécom Paris specifically for this purpose: it pairs recorded and synthesized piano audio with precise, ground-truth annotations of every note played, including cases where several notes are played together (called "multipitch," i.e. more than one pitch sounding at once, like a chord). Because it was one of the first large, carefully annotated resources of its kind, MAPS became a standard benchmark that many later piano-transcription and multipitch-estimation research papers use to test and compare their systems.
