Researchers who study how computers can understand music (a field called Music Information Retrieval, or MIR) rely on collections of labeled example recordings — think "audio files plus notes about the beat, key, or instruments playing." The problem is that every research group used to load and format these collections differently, making results hard to compare and reproduce. mirdata is an open-source Python software library, with a co-author from Télécom Paris' ADASP group (Magdalena Fuentes), that provides one standard way to download, check, and load dozens of these datasets so every researcher works from the same consistent format. This kind of shared infrastructure work, while less flashy than a new algorithm, is what lets the whole MIR research community build on each other's work reliably.
