When sound is played in a room, the room's own reflections (echoes bouncing off walls) mix with the direct sound and can muddy the listening experience — a problem for recording studios, listening rooms, and immersive-audio research. This paper, published in the Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, presents a method using Ambisonics (a 3D, full-sphere approach to sound-field capture and playback) to cancel out unwanted room reflections not just at one point but across an entire listening area, so that several people in a room can enjoy a cleaner sound field rather than only the single "sweet spot" that many spatial audio systems rely on.
