Jean-Claude Risset (1938-2016) was a pioneering French composer and researcher who, working partly at Bell Labs in the 1960s, was one of the first people in the world to make music with a computer and to scientifically study how we perceive synthetic sound (he is known for creating the "Risset glissando", an audio illusion of a tone that seems to fall in pitch forever). He was a founding member of PRISM. This resource is an ongoing project to digitize and catalogue his entire personal archive of scores, recordings, and research notes, making this foundational slice of computer music history available to researchers.
