CataRT is a tool that lets you make new sounds by intelligently rearranging tiny fragments ("grains") cut from a large library of pre-recorded sounds. Instead of picking sounds by name, you navigate a 2D map where sounds are placed by how they actually sound (bright vs. dull, loud vs. soft, etc.), and the system picks the grain closest to wherever you point in real time. This lets composers and sound designers explore huge sound archives intuitively, for example turning a database of voice recordings or field recordings into a playable instrument. It was created by Diemo Schwarz and colleagues at IRCAM and has been used in many musical works and installations since the mid-2000s.
