Turning text or a compressed audio representation back into an actual sound wave you can listen to is a hard, slow problem for AI models — many earlier neural vocoders (the part of a speech system that generates the final waveform) needed enormous computing power to run in real time. This paper, co-authored by the Lyrebird team along with Mila researchers Yoshua Bengio and Aaron Courville, introduces MelGAN, a much faster generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture that can produce high-quality, natural-sounding speech waveforms without the heavy computational cost of prior approaches, making real-time voice synthesis on ordinary hardware much more practical.
