Every amplifier's very first stage (where a weak incoming audio signal is first boosted) tends to add a small amount of unwanted distortion, which then gets amplified along with the music. This patent describes the "Salomie circuit," an input-stage design using twelve active components arranged in a specific way — two complementary feedback pairs plus a further gain stage — to buffer and lightly amplify the incoming signal with far less distortion and noise than a conventional input stage. Bryston's own measurements put its distortion at under 0.001%, about ten times lower than the input stage it replaced.
