Video game music usually needs to change with the action on screen — calm during exploration, tense during a fight — which is normally done by hand-composing many short, pre-recorded music clips that a game engine switches between (this is called "adaptive" or "dynamic" game music). PreGLAM-MMM instead uses the lab's PreGLAM model, which predicts how a player is likely to feel moment-to-moment based on what's happening in the game, and feeds that prediction into MMM (the lab's multi-track music generator) so it can extend and vary a composed musical score on the fly to match the mood. The system was tested against two normal, human-composed scores (one adaptive, one fixed/linear) in an action role-playing game, and players rated the AI-generated version nearly as good as a composed adaptive score for emotional fit and immersion. This paper is a direct precursor to the lab's current Mitacs-funded collaboration with Audiokinetic on generative game audio.
