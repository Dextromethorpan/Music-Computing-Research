This widely cited review paper explains why playing or even just listening to music engages the brain's movement-control system, not only its hearing system. When a trained musician hears a melody, brain areas involved in planning and executing physical movement (like pressing piano keys) become active even if the person's hands stay still — a phenomenon the authors call "auditory-motor coupling." The paper reviews brain-imaging evidence (mainly fMRI and PET, which show which brain regions are active) showing that this two-way link between hearing sound and controlling movement is what allows a musician to translate a sound they imagine into precise finger movements, and to correct their playing in real time based on what they hear. It also explains how this coupling strengthens with years of musical training, tightening the connection between the brain's auditory and motor regions.
