This paper introduced the Montreal Battery of Evaluation of Amusia (MBEA), which became the standard test worldwide for diagnosing "amusia" — significant, measurable trouble perceiving or producing music. The test is a set of six short tasks: some check whether you can notice when a note in a melody is wrong (out of scale, or the wrong distance from its neighbors), some check memory for tunes, and one checks whether you can feel and tap along with a beat. Because it was built and validated using patients with known brain damage as well as ordinary listeners, a person's scores can be compared against a normal-population baseline to say confidently whether their musical perception falls outside the typical range. This tool is what let later researchers move from individual case reports of "tone deafness" into rigorous group studies and genetic research on congenital amusia.
