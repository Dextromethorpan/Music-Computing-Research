Written by BRAMS's two co-founders, this review pulls together what brain-lesion studies (looking at patients with damage to specific brain areas) and brain-imaging studies had shown, up to that point, about how the brain handles four separate jobs: perceiving music, remembering it, feeling emotion from it, and performing it. A central theme is that music perception is not handled by one single "music center" in the brain — instead it's built from several more general-purpose processes (like pitch tracking and timing) that are shared with other tasks, including some aspects of spoken language. The paper also discusses how music and speech both depend on picking up patterns of pitch and rhythm over time, which is why the two domains can partially overlap in the brain while still being distinguishable — as shown by patients who lose musical ability but keep language ability, or vice versa.
