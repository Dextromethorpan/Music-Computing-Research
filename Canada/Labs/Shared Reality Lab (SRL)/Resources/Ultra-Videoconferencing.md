Ultra-Videoconferencing is SRL's long-running project to build a flexible, very-low-delay system for sending audio, video, and touch/vibration data over the internet, instead of relying on ordinary video-call software (which compresses and delays signals too much for musicians to play together comfortably). Starting in 1999 with a surround-sound audio stream sent between Montreal and New York, the system was used for milestones like a 2001 violin duet performed live between two rooms with only about 20 milliseconds of delay (fast enough for the musicians to feel like they were on the same stage), a 2002 cross-continental jazz jam between McGill and Stanford, and remote masters classes with violinist Pinchas Zuckerman. The core engineering problem is "latency" — minimizing the time between a sound being made in one place and being heard in another — which the project tackled through custom low-level video and audio transport software rather than standard video-conferencing tools. This work later influenced commercial telepresence videoconferencing systems from companies like Cisco, HP, and Polycom.
