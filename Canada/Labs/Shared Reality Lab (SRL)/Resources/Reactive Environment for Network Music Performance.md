This NIME 2013 paper (pages 158-163) takes a different angle on Networked Music Performance (musicians in separate locations playing together over a network): rather than just trying to make a remote performance feel exactly like being in the same room, the authors built a "reactive environment" that gives musicians new, real-time controls over their performance — such as adjusting volume or exaggerating the stereo positioning of a distant player's sound — to reinforce a feeling of "shared space" even though the players are physically apart. The design borrows user-centered methods from Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research, treating the network itself as a new kind of performance space with its own creative possibilities rather than a limitation to be hidden.
