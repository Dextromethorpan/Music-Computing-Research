This NIME 2009 paper (pages 135-140) tackles a very practical problem: how do you actually measure whether an audio-streaming system is good enough for musicians to play together remotely? The authors developed methods to precisely measure two things — "end-to-end latency" (the total delay from a sound being played in one location to being heard in another) and audio quality — and then used those methods to test and compare several different streaming software engines. High-quality, low-latency audio streaming is the technical foundation underneath any networked music performance system, since without it, distant musicians cannot stay in rhythm together. The paper also discusses what remained to be solved for future interactive audio applications at the time.
