Marcelo M. Wanderley is a professor at McGill's Schulich School of Music, director of IDMIL, and director of CIRMMT (the wider interdisciplinary music-technology research centre IDMIL belongs to). His research is about how to design new electronic musical instruments so that they are genuinely expressive to play — meaning a performer can shape subtle qualities of the sound the way a skilled violinist shapes tone on a violin, rather than just triggering pre-set sounds. He has worked for decades on the sensors, "mapping" software (which connects a performer's movements to sound parameters), and design methods behind these new instruments, and analysis published in 2021 found his work to be the most-cited research from the first two decades of the NIME conference, the main academic venue for this field.
