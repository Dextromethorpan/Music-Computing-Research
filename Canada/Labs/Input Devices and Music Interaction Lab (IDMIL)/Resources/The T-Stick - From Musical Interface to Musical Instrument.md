This paper, presented at the NIME conference (New Interfaces for Musical Expression — see that concept note), introduces the T-Stick and makes a broader argument about what separates a mere "musical interface" (a device that produces sound when you interact with it) from a true "musical instrument" (something a performer can develop real expressive skill and a lasting relationship with, the way a violinist relates to a violin). The authors describe the design choices behind the T-Stick's sensors and shape, and argue that instrument builders should think as much about long-term playability and repertoire-building as about the initial "wow" of a new sound-control gesture. It is one of the foundational texts in the NIME field for thinking about instrument design as a design process, not just an engineering exercise.
