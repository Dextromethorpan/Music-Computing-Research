Every digital musical instrument (an instrument built from electronics and software rather than acoustic parts) relies on sensors — small components that turn a physical action, like pressing, bending, or tilting, into an electrical signal a computer can read. This paper surveys five years of published designs from the NIME research community (2009–2013) to see what sensor types instrument builders were actually using in practice. The surprising finding: most builders reached for the same handful of basic, off-the-shelf sensors (like simple pressure pads or bend sensors) rather than more advanced sensing techniques that could capture a performer's gestures with much finer detail. The authors argue this represents a missed opportunity, since better sensing could let new instruments respond to a musician as richly and precisely as a well-built acoustic instrument does. It functions as a kind of state-of-the-field snapshot and practical reference for anyone designing a new instrument's electronics.
