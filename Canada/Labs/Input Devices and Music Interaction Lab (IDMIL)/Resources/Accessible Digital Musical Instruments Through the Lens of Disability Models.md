This paper looks at digital musical instruments designed for d/Deaf people — for example, devices that let someone experience or make music through vibration and visual feedback rather than hearing. Rather than just describing the instruments themselves, the authors use "disability models" (frameworks from disability studies about how society defines and responds to disability — for instance, whether a disability is treated as something to "fix" in the person, versus a mismatch between the person and a poorly designed environment) as a lens to evaluate whether these instruments were actually designed with and for Deaf musicians, or designed for them without real input. It is a case study in why building genuinely accessible technology requires more than adding a sensor or a vibration motor — it requires the design process itself to include the people who will use it, and to reflect on unstated assumptions about what "making music" is supposed to look or feel like.
