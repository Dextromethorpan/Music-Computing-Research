The T-Stick is a "digital musical instrument" (DMI) — a new kind of instrument, invented from scratch rather than descended from an acoustic one, whose sound is generated entirely by software. Physically it is a slim tube (a bit like a flute or clarinet without any acoustic sound-making parts of its own) covered in sensors that detect how it is touched, squeezed, shaken, and tilted; those sensor readings are then converted into control signals that shape synthesized sound in real time. What makes the T-Stick unusual in this field is its longevity: it has been developed and refined since 2006, has several physical variants of different sizes, and — unlike many one-off research prototypes — has been adopted by a small community of expert performers and composers who have written real repertoire for it and performed it internationally. That long-term relationship between instrument and performer is itself something IDMIL studies, since most new electronic instruments are abandoned after a single demo.
