When a computer records audio, it has to round each sample to the nearest representable number — a process called quantization. This rounding introduces a subtle form of distortion that can sound harsh or add unwanted overtones, especially at low volumes. This landmark Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper builds the mathematical theory for "dither" — a tiny bit of noise deliberately added before rounding — showing that it converts that ugly, signal-correlated distortion into plain, statistically well-behaved noise (hiss) instead. It compares subtractive and non-subtractive dithering methods and works out exactly how much dither is needed and what its statistical properties should be. This paper became one of the standard references used across the audio industry (it underlies how noise-shaping and bit-depth reduction are done in essentially all modern digital audio software). It is the clearest single summary of the Audio Research Group's decades of dither research.
