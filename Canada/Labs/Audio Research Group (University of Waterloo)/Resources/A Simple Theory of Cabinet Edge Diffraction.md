When sound leaves a loudspeaker cone, it doesn't just travel forward cleanly — some of it bends ("diffracts") around the sharp edges of the speaker cabinet, arriving at the listener's ear slightly delayed and altering the overall tone. This 1991 Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper by John Vanderkooy works out a simplified, practical mathematical model for predicting this cabinet-edge diffraction effect, using asymptotic results that are valid at high frequencies. Instead of requiring a full, computationally heavy simulation of the sound field, the model treats each cabinet edge as if it were its own small secondary sound source. This gave loudspeaker designers a much faster way to predict and correct for a real, audible artifact of enclosure shape, and the paper remains a standard reference cited in loudspeaker engineering literature and later diffraction models.
