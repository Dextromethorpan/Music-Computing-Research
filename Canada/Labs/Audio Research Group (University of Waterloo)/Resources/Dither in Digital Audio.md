This 1987 Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper is one of the founding texts of digital dither theory, and it is one of the pieces of work the Audio Research Group is best known for. It explains, for an audience with basic engineering background, why rounding audio samples to a fixed number of bits creates distortion, and why adding a carefully chosen small amount of random noise ("dither") before that rounding step removes the distortion and replaces it with a much less objectionable hiss. The paper walks through practical dither shapes (rectangular vs. triangular probability distributions) and shows the audible difference dithering makes at low signal levels — the point where quantization artifacts are most noticeable. It laid groundwork that the group expanded in its later, more mathematically complete 1992 survey paper.
