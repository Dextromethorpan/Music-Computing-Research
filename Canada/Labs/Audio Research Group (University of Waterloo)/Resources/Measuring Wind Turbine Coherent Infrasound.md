"Infrasound" is sound below roughly 20 Hz, too low for humans to consciously hear but sometimes felt as pressure or vibration. Wind turbines produce infrasound as their blades pass the tower, and there has been public concern about whether this affects nearby residents. This 2014 paper by John Vanderkooy and R. Mann describes a measurement technique for isolating the specific, repetitive ("coherent") infrasound signal generated by a turbine's blade-pass rate from all the other background noise (wind, traffic, distant machinery) that a microphone would otherwise pick up. It's a practical example of the Audio Research Group's core specialty — careful, rigorous acoustic measurement — applied outside of pure hi-fi audio to an environmental noise question, and it reflects John Vanderkooy's continued research activity into the 2010s.
