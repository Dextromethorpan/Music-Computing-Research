A "maximum-length sequence" (MLS) is a special, pseudo-random test signal that has a very useful mathematical property: when it's played through a loudspeaker or room and the resulting recording is compared against the original sequence, you can quickly compute the impulse response of whatever you're measuring (essentially the "acoustic fingerprint" of that speaker or space). This 1994 Journal of the Audio Engineering Society paper by John Vanderkooy examines what actually goes wrong with MLS measurements in the real world — how nonlinear distortion in the loudspeaker or microphone corrupts the result, showing up as spurious extra reflections or reduced accuracy. Understanding these failure modes was important groundwork for reliable, practical acoustic measurement equipment, an area the Audio Research Group's Waterloo Audiolab specialized in.
