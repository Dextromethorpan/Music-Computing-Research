Presented at NIME 2006 (New Interfaces for Musical Expression, a conference on inventing new ways to control and perform music with computers), this paper describes "Different Strokes," a software system built for improvised laptop performance. Rather than a fixed acoustic-modeling paper, it's about the performance side of CAML's work: how a musician can control real-time synthesis on stage in a flexible, improvisational way, using a laptop as the instrument itself. Mark Zadel worked on this as a CAML PhD student under Gary Scavone. It's a useful example of the lab's broader remit — not just modeling how physical instruments make sound, but also building the software interfaces musicians use to perform new, computer-based instruments live.
