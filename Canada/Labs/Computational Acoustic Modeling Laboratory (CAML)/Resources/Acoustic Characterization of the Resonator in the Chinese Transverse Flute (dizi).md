This paper studies the dizi, a traditional Chinese transverse (side-blown) bamboo flute, using the same scientific toolkit CAML applies to Western instruments. The researchers measured the instrument's "acoustic impedance" (how the tube's shape resists or passes different pitches of vibrating air) and compared it against mathematical models that predict how the flute should behave. This work is by Xinmeng Luan, a current CAML PhD student, together with Gary Scavone and collaborators. It matters because it extends physical-modeling and acoustic-measurement methods, originally built for instruments like the clarinet or trumpet, to a non-Western instrument, and it's a concrete example of how CAML's measurement equipment and modeling techniques are applied to real instruments beyond the usual orchestral set.
