This paper, presented at the 2006 International Conference on Digital Audio Effects (DAFx-06) in Montreal, tackles a technical problem in simulating a clarinet on a computer. "Acoustic impedance" describes how much a tube (like a clarinet's body) resists or lets through vibrating air at different pitches — it's the mathematical fingerprint of the instrument's shape. "Digital waveguides" are a common, efficient way of simulating a vibrating tube by bouncing a digital signal back and forth through a virtual pipe, similar to how sound actually travels and reflects inside a real instrument. Scavone and Smith show how to build a version of this simulation that stays numerically stable (doesn't blow up or produce noise) while still accurately matching a real clarinet's behavior. This kind of work underlies real-time software instruments that sound and respond like the physical original.
