Presented at the 2002 International Computer Music Conference, this paper addresses how to simulate instruments whose tube widens steadily along its length — a "conical bore" — such as the saxophone or oboe, as opposed to a cylindrical tube like a clarinet. "Time-domain synthesis" means the simulation runs step by step through time, sample by sample, computing the instrument's sound as it's played, rather than pre-calculating a fixed waveform. Scavone works out how to adapt the digital-waveguide modeling approach (bouncing a signal back and forth through a virtual tube) to this cone shape so it can run in real time on a computer. This is a direct example of CAML's core specialty: turning the physics of wind instrument bodies into efficient, playable computer models.
