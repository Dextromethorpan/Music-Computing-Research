The AGNI Methodology is Sandeep Bhagwati's own framework, developed in 2005, for how to do rigorous "research-creation" — research where the artwork itself is a legitimate part of the investigation, not just an illustration of conclusions reached elsewhere. It is loosely adapted from landscape architect Lawrence Halprin's 1960s "RSVP Cycle" theory of collective creativity, which Bhagwati reworked to combine the perspective of the artist making a piece with the perspective of the researcher analyzing it. This document is essentially matralab's house method: it explains how the lab tries to make interdisciplinary, intercultural art projects (like Nexus or Sound of Montréal) count as genuine scholarly research and not only artistic output.
