Nexus was a roaming outdoor music performance commissioned by Concordia University for the 2010 Congress of the Humanities and Social Sciences. Five wind musicians each carried a backpack rig — laptop, wireless internet connection, small loudspeaker, microphone, and a "Follow Me" sign — and walked separate routes across campus toward a shared meeting point. As they walked, each musician's laptop ran custom software that sent them an "interactive score": live, changing instructions generated from what the other musicians were playing over the network, so the piece was assembled in real time out of the performers' improvised responses to each other (a technique Bhagwati calls "comprovisation" — part composition, part improvisation). It is an early, concrete example of how matralab treats notation as something that can be generated live over a network rather than fixed on paper in advance.
