This paper describes a full, end-to-end pipeline for automatically reading scanned pages of medieval and Renaissance music and turning them into structured, searchable digital data. Old music manuscripts don't look like modern sheet music — the notation systems, page layouts, and even the physical condition of the paper vary widely, which makes automatic recognition much harder than for printed modern scores. The DDMAL team's system uses convolutional neural networks (a type of image-recognition AI) to break a scanned page down into its parts — background, staff lines, note symbols, and text — before reconstructing the actual musical content and exporting it in the MEI symbolic-music format for further study. Presented at the 2019 Music Encoding Conference in Vienna, this work represents DDMAL's flagship demonstration of optical music recognition (OMR) applied to historical, non-standard notation.
