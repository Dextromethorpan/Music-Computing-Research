This paper introduces SIMSSA (Single Interface for Music Score Searching and Analysis), a large, long-running DDMAL project (funded 2014-2021) aimed at making the world's music scores as searchable as text documents already are. The core problem: most sheet music exists only as scanned images, which computers cannot search or analyze the way they can a digitized book. SIMSSA set out to build a pipeline — scanning, optical music recognition (converting page images into computer-readable notes), and search tools — so that researchers, librarians, and musicians could search across huge collections of digitized scores by melody, rhythm, or other musical features rather than just by title or composer. Many of DDMAL's other tools (Neon, Rodan, PatternFinder) were built as pieces of this larger SIMSSA infrastructure.
