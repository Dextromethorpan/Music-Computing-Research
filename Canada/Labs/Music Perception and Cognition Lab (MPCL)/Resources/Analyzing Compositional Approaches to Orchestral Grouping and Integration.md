Published in the Revue musicale OICRM as part of a special issue on the CORE project (Composer-performer Orchestration Research Ensemble), this paper by two MPCL PhD students (Yuval Adler and Joshua Rosner) and music theorist Robert Hasegawa looks at orchestration from the composer's side of the process. Rather than only measuring how listeners perceive finished orchestral passages, the authors analyze the compositional strategies composers actually use to make instruments "group" together into a unified sound or stay distinct and separately audible ("integration" versus separation). It connects music-theoretical analysis of scores with the lab's broader research on auditory grouping, bridging how composers plan orchestration and how that planning translates into what an audience perceptually experiences.
