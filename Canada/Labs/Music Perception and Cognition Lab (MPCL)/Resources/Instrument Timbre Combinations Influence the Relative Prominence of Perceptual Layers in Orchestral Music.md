When an orchestra plays several musical lines at once (say, a melody, a countermelody, and accompanying chords), listeners don't hear them all equally — some lines feel like they're "in front" and others fade into the background. This paper investigates how the specific combination of instrument timbres (the tonal "color" of each instrument) used for each line changes how prominent that line sounds to a listener. Using excerpts of real orchestral music, the researchers tested how different instrument pairings shift a passage's perceived layering, essentially asking: which instrument combinations make a line pop out, and which make it recede? The findings give composers and orchestration researchers a more scientific basis for a skill that has traditionally been taught only through apprenticeship and intuition — matching instruments to achieve a desired balance between foreground and background.
