Written for the public-facing outlet The Conversation by MPCL PhD student Linglan Zhu and co-author Aidan McGartland, this accessible article explores why certain instrument sounds — jingling bells, choir voices, sleigh bells, brass — have become so strongly associated with Christmas music specifically. Rather than being an arbitrary tradition, the piece explains how these sounds' timbral qualities (their bright, shimmering, or "cold"-sounding acoustic textures) get culturally linked to holiday imagery and repeated in seasonal music until the association becomes automatic. It's a good entry point for a beginner because it applies the lab's core research interest — timbre and how listeners perceive it — to something everyone has experienced, without requiring any background in acoustics. The piece got enough public attention that it was picked up and discussed on CBC Radio's Daybreak program in Montreal.
