Published in the online journal Music Theory Online, this article by lab director Stephen McAdams builds a structured catalogue ("taxonomy") of the different ways instruments in an orchestra can perceptually group together or separate in a listener's mind. For example, two instruments playing together can "blend" into what sounds like one new timbre, or a melody passed between instruments can sound like it's continuously moving, or contrasting instrument groups can create a call-and-response effect. Rather than relying only on traditional music-theory description, McAdams grounds each category in auditory scene analysis — the psychological process by which the brain organizes a mixture of sounds into distinct streams. The result is a practical vocabulary composers, theorists, and perception researchers can use to talk precisely about how orchestration shapes what a listener actually perceives, not just what's written on the page.
