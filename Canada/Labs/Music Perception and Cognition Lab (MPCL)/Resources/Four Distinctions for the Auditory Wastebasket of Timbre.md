Timbre is often jokingly called an auditory "wastebasket" — the leftover category researchers use for anything about a sound that isn't pitch, loudness, or duration. This paper, published in Frontiers in Psychology, argues that this vagueness has become a real scientific problem: different studies use "timbre" to mean quite different things, making results hard to compare. The authors (Kai Siedenburg, a former MPCL member, and lab director Stephen McAdams) propose four clarifying distinctions — for instance, whether timbre is being studied as a raw sensory quality versus as a cue that helps identify a sound source (like recognizing "that's a clarinet"), and whether researchers mean small-scale differences within one instrument's sound versus large-scale differences between whole instrument families. The paper functions as a conceptual toolkit meant to sharpen how future timbre research is designed and discussed.
