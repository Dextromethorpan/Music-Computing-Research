## Shortlist
1. **Auditory Illusion Shorts Generator** — web app that turns classic auditory illusions (Shepard tone, Risset rhythm, others later) into ready-to-post captioned vertical videos for TikTok/YouTube Shorts creators and science communicators. Build effort: S. Distribution: r/illusions (~2.2k members, "share illusions of all types, audio, visual, mental") plus direct outreach to specific creators who already post audio-illusion content (e.g. the TikTok account idea.soup, whose Shepard-tone-style videos have hit millions of views). Incumbency risk: Medium — free raw tone generators already exist (mynoise.net's binaural Shepard tone generator, BigSoundBank's Shepard generator, a standalone Risset Rhythm generator by Canton Becker), so the *sound* isn't scarce; the gap is nobody packages it as an export-ready captioned clip, which is a real but modest time-save, not a hard-to-replace capability.
2. **Podcast/VO-editor EQ ear trainer** — narrower spinoff of "guess the boosted frequency" ear training, aimed specifically at podcast and voiceover editors rather than music producers generally. Build effort: M. Distribution: no clearly gathering community found specifically complaining about this (r/podcasting is about production/growth, not perceptual EQ skill) — weak/speculative channel. Incumbency risk: High — Quiztones (theproaudiofiles/Soundgym) already owns "EQ ear training for audio pros" broadly, and switching cost for an editor to just use that instead is Low.
3. **Embeddable "Can You Hear the Difference?" ABX widget for plugin/gear marketing pages** — sell audio plugin developers a drop-in ABX test widget to prove their product's effect is audible. Build effort: M. Distribution: would require cold outreach to plugin developers (KVR Audio, gearspace) — no existing community was found asking for this; treat as a weak signal of demand, not neutral. Incumbency risk: High — multiple free, capable ABX web tools already exist (abx.digitalfeed.net, theaudiostuff.com/tools/abx-test, foobar2000's ABX Comparator plugin), any of which a marketer could link to for free, so switching cost to a paid widget is essentially zero.

## Build plan (top pick: Auditory Illusion Shorts Generator)
v1 covers exactly two illusions — Shepard tone and Risset rhythm — not the full psychoacoustic catalog; both have well-documented synthesis algorithms (additive/log-frequency oscillator banks) so no novel DSP research is needed. Flow: user picks illusion type, duration, and a caption ("why your brain hears this"), the app synthesizes audio client-side via the Web Audio API, renders a simple animated Canvas visual (waveform/spectrogram-style bars) with burned-in caption text, and captures the canvas+audio stream directly with the MediaRecorder API to export an MP4/WebM — deliberately skipping ffmpeg.wasm to avoid the complexity of in-browser transcoding. No user accounts, no cloud rendering, no customization beyond duration/color theme/caption text. Monetize as a one-time Gumroad purchase or a capped free tier (watermarked, limited exports) with a small paid tier for clean exports. Realistic solo timeline: ~2-3 weeks — week 1 audio synthesis for both illusion types, week 2 canvas capture + caption overlay + export pipeline, week 3 landing page, payment wiring, polish, ship.

## Distribution plan (top pick: Auditory Illusion Shorts Generator)
Primary channel is r/illusions — small (~2.2k members) but exactly on-topic ("audio, visual, mental" illusions); post genuine illusion clips made with the tool as native content rather than a sales pitch, with a free tier so people can try it without friction. Secondary and probably higher-leverage: direct, personal outreach (not mass posting) to the handful of named creators already making audio-illusion content for TikTok/YouTube — e.g. idea.soup — offering free access in exchange for a tool credit in a video description, since this niche runs on a small number of individual creators rather than a large forum. Tertiary: one-off posts in science-communication spaces (r/science/r/AskScience threads that surface audio-illusion questions) linking back to example clips. Honest weak point: the total addressable audience across these channels is small and thin — this reads as a nice side-income product riding an existing niche, not a scalable business, and success leans heavily on whether 1-2 creators actually adopt and amplify it, which isn't guaranteed.
